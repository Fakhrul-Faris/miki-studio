# Camera Presets: Movement Vocabulary

Use these presets as templates for animation briefs. Each preset includes the prompt language that AI video models respond to most clearly.

## Dolly Presets

### DOLLY IN: Intimate Revelation

**Move:** Dolly in. Parallax preserved.

**Effect:** Camera physically moves toward the subject. The world doesn't flatten. Depth is maintained.

**When to Use:** Character realization, discovery, entrance into a subject's space, focus narrowing, intimacy building.

**When NOT to Use:** Establishing shots (push out instead), moments where environment context is essential, action sequences needing spatial awareness.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Dolly in. Slow. Parallax preserved. Stop when we see her eyes. The window stays out of focus. Depth intact."

**Speed Baseline:** 3–5 seconds for a 1–2 foot move.

---

### DOLLY OUT: Context Revelation

**Move:** Dolly out. Parallax preserved.

**Effect:** Camera physically moves away. The world expands. Context is revealed. Isolation becomes apparent.

**When to Use:** End of scene, realization of scale or powerlessness, retreat, context expanding.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Dolly out. Medium speed. Parallax preserved. Start close on her hands. End wide, she fills frame left, the harbor fills frame right. Let her get smaller."

**Speed Baseline:** 3–5 seconds for a 1–2 foot retreat.

---

### DOLLY ZOOM: Psychological Disorientation

**Move:** Dolly in while focal length lengthens (or vice versa). Subject stays same size; background distorts.

**Effect:** Hitchcock vertigo. Subject size stays constant but world distorts.

**When to Use:** Psychological shock, dread, disorientation, reality destabilizing, a moment of profound realization.

**When NOT to Use:** Subtle character work, comedy, moments needing visual stability.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Dolly zoom. Camera moves in while focal length extends. Subject stays same size. Background warps. Very slow. Hold for 4 seconds. Her face does not change size."

**Speed Baseline:** 4+ seconds.

---

## Crane Presets

### CRANE UP: Ascent & Reveal

**Move:** Crane up. Vertical ascent.

**Effect:** Camera rises. Perspective reveals context above. Scale expands vertically.

**When to Use:** Environmental reveals, escape, transcendence, showing the enormity of a space or situation.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Crane up. Slow. Start frame on her hands at the sink. Rise to show the entire kitchen, the window, the harbor beyond. Reveal the scale of the space."

**Speed Baseline:** 3–5 seconds for a 2–3 foot rise.

---

### CRANE DOWN: Diminishment

**Move:** Crane down. Vertical descent.

**Effect:** Camera lowers. Subject becomes smaller. Isolation increases.

**When to Use:** Diminishment, isolation, entrapment, insignificance, gravity increasing.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Crane down. Slow. Start frame on her face. Descend to show her at the sink, alone in the kitchen. Make her small in the frame."

**Speed Baseline:** 3–5 seconds for a 2–3 foot descent.

---

## Tracking Presets

### TRACKING: Lateral Parallel

**Move:** Tracking move. Horizontal across space. Subject stays in frame.

**Effect:** Camera moves horizontally while maintaining subject in frame. Parallax is strong (foreground and background move at different speeds).

**When to Use:** Parallel journey, comparison, revelation of spatial relationships, following action while maintaining frame.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Track right. Medium speed. She walks to the window. Camera moves parallel, keeping her centered. The kitchen shifts behind her."

**Speed Baseline:** 2–3 seconds for a 3–4 foot lateral move.

---

### FOLLOW (Steadicam / Handheld)

**Move:** Follow. Camera as companion observer.

**Effect:** Camera follows the subject's movement. Intimacy. Witness. Real-time accompaniment.

**When to Use:** Character-led sequences, intimacy, documentary feel, alliance with the character.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Steadicam follow. She walks through the kitchen. Camera moves with her, close, intimate. Not shaky. Smooth but alive. Parallax is clear."

**Speed Baseline:** Real-time (matches character's walking speed).

---

### SNORRICAM: Psychological Dislocation

**Move:** Snorricam. Camera mounted to actor. Actor moves through static or drifting background.

**Effect:** Extreme disconnection. The actor moves, background flows behind.

**When to Use:** Psychological isolation, dislocation from space, surreal moments, the character is untethered.

**When NOT to Use:** Grounded character work, comedy needing spatial clarity.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Snorricam. Camera locked to her face. She walks through the kitchen, the background drifts behind her. No parallax. She is isolated in space."

**Speed Baseline:** Real-time.

---

## Orbit Presets

### ORBIT AROUND SUBJECT

**Move:** Orbit. Camera circles subject while maintaining center on subject.

**Effect:** Inspection. Revelation of context around subject. Disorientation (fast orbit).

**When to Use:** Inspection, 360 reveal, psychological disorientation (fast), environmental discovery.

**When NOT to Use:** Quick action sequences (orbit is slow and deliberate), moments of rest.

**Prompt Phrase (Slow):** "Orbit around her, slow, 180 degrees. Start frame left, move around to frame right. She stays centered. Reveal what's around her."

**Prompt Phrase (Fast):** "Orbit around her, fast, 360 degrees. Vertigo effect. Dread. She is the center; the world spins."

**Speed Baseline:** Slow: 4–6 seconds. Fast: 2–3 seconds.

---

## Static Presets

### STATIC: The Frame as Witness

**Move:** Static. No motion.

**Effect:** Compositional control. Witnessing. Waiting.

**When to Use:** Reaction shots, moments of waiting, repose, moments where stillness is power, establishing shots, interviews.

**When NOT to Use:** Action sequences needing energy, scenes where camera presence would be intrusive.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Static frame. No camera move. She enters frame right. The frame waits for her. Composition locked. The window is frame left, her hands frame right."

**Speed Baseline:** Hold for 3–5 seconds minimum.

---

## Handheld Presets

### HANDHELD: Operator Presence

**Move:** Handheld. Unsteady but intentional.

**Effect:** Realism. Documentary feel. Urgency. Human operator is present, breathing, reacting.

**When to Use:** Action, realism, intimacy (when handheld is earned), documentary feel, human scale.

**When NOT to Use:** Dialogue scenes (usually), moments needing visual stillness, intimate character work (usually).

**Caveat:** "Unsteady" does not mean "shaky." The operator is a person, not a seizure. There is intention in the movement.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Handheld. Unsteady. The operator is running. Small instability on the cut. Not jittery. Alive. She enters frame, the camera reacts slightly, the frame settles."

**Speed Baseline:** Real-time or intentional rhythm matching action.

---

## Whip Pan

### WHIP PAN: Attention Shift

**Move:** Whip pan. Fast horizontal movement from subject A to subject B.

**Effect:** Reaction. Surprise. Attention snap. Energy spike.

**When to Use:** Cut on action, attention shift, surprise reveal, punctuation on a beat.

**When NOT to Use:** Character-driven dialogue, slow narrative moments.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Whip pan left. Fast. From her face to the window. On beat. Energy shift."

**Speed Baseline:** 0.5–1 second.

---

## Specialty Combinations

### PUSH IN + SLIGHT ORBIT

**Move:** Push in while gently orbiting (5–10 degrees).

**Effect:** Approach with perspective shift. Intimacy with context reveal.

**When to Use:** Character approach with slight environmental discovery, moving into someone's space.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Push in. Slow. Slight orbit right, 8 degrees. Parallax preserved. Approach her while revealing the window behind her."

---

### FOLLOW + SLIGHT CRANE DOWN

**Move:** Follow (Steadicam) while gently descending.

**Effect:** Accompaniment with diminishment.

**When to Use:** Journey downward, descent into a space, following someone's emotional descent.

**Prompt Phrase:** "Steadicam follow. She walks down the stairs. Camera descends with her, close, intimate. No jitter. Smooth. Parallax clear. Make her get smaller as we descend."

---

## Prohibited Combinations

**Do NOT combine these moves in a single keyframe unless explicitly motivated by story:**

- Dolly in + zoom out (conflicting intentions, creates vertigo unless that is the point)
- Orbit + crane (disorienting in ways that read as a mistake, not intention)
- Handheld + Snorricam (contradictory philosophies; one is observer presence, one is actor presence)

## Quick Reference: Speed Baselines

| Movement Type | Typical Duration | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| Dolly In (character) | 3–5 sec | Slow–Medium |
| Dolly Out (reveal) | 3–5 sec | Slow–Medium |
| Dolly Zoom | 4+ sec | Very Slow |
| Crane Up | 3–5 sec | Slow–Medium |
| Crane Down | 3–5 sec | Slow–Medium |
| Tracking | 2–3 sec | Medium |
| Follow (Steadicam) | Real-time | Medium |
| Snorricam | Real-time | Medium |
| Orbit (180°) | 4–6 sec | Slow |
| Orbit (360°, fast) | 2–3 sec | Fast |
| Whip Pan | 0.5–1 sec | Fast |
| Handheld | Real-time | Medium–Fast |
| Static | 3–5 sec | N/A |

---

**Note:** These are baselines. Story beats and emotional intention may justify departing from them. If your move needs to be slower or faster, state the reason in the brief.

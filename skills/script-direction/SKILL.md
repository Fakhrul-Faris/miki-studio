# Script Direction

## Core Principle

A script in directive voice is not dialogue and action description. It is a beat sheet where each beat is either a keyframe (a moment of visual intensity) or a transition (filler, movement between keyframes). Identifying these beats before you draw tells you exactly how many keyframes you need to generate and what each one must communicate.

A keyframe is a moment where:
- The emotional state changes.
- The action changes.
- The camera position must change to see the new action.
- Story information is revealed.

Between keyframes: transition time. Movement. Reaction. Breath.

## Scene Structure in Directive Voice

Write the scene as a beat sheet, not as dialogue. Each beat states what is happening and why it matters. Use the field mapping from directive-voice skill: CHARACTER, LOCATION, ACTION, EMOTION, VISUAL INTENT.

**Do not write:**
```
INT. KITCHEN - DAY
Maria enters the kitchen and looks out the window. She sees the harbor. 
She feels sad about her husband. She starts washing dishes.
```

**Write:**
```
BEAT 1: ENTRANCE
MARIA pushes the door open. Wind from outside. The kitchen is cold.
VISUAL: She enters frame right. Kitchen recedes left. One door behind her, 
still open, light spilling in. Her face: shock at the cold.
EMOTION: Disorientation. The familiar is hostile.

BEAT 2: RECOGNITION
She closes the door. Silence. She sees the window, the harbor, the boat.
VISUAL: Wide. Marina fills the window. Her hand on the doorframe, weathered.
EMOTION: The weight of knowing. Not sentimentality. Recognition.

BEAT 3: WORK
She walks to the sink. Fills it. Plunges her hands in. The water is cold.
VISUAL: Close on her hands entering the water. Forearms scarred from work.
EMOTION: This is what she does. This is all she does.
```

Each BEAT will become a keyframe or a pair of keyframes. Each VISUAL statement tells you the shot size, composition, and what matters. Each EMOTION statement is the interior state that informs performance and camera choice.

## Keyframe Identification

Once the scene is written, mark the beats that must be keyframes (moments of visual intensity or emotional weight) and the beats that can be transitions.

**Keyframes (must generate):**
- Entrance/exit (moment of arrival or departure)
- Reaction (look, expression, realization)
- Action (reaching, grabbing, turning, falling)
- Revelation (seeing something new, understanding something)
- Peak emotion (confrontation, surrender, decision)

**Transitions (can dissolve/fade/cut, may not need generation):**
- Walking from A to B (unless the walk itself is the story)
- Looking around a room (unless one thing in that room is the revelation)
- Waiting or thinking (unless we need to see the thought on the face)

In the scene above:
- BEAT 1 (entrance) = KEYFRAME 1
- BEAT 2 (recognition) = KEYFRAME 2
- BEAT 3 (work) = KEYFRAME 3

Three beats, three keyframes. Each will get a camera move.

## The Directive Script Template

For any scene, use this structure:

### SCENE HEADING
INT. [LOCATION] — [TIME OF DAY]

### SETTING
Location description in directive voice. Materials, wear, light source.

### CHARACTERS
List each character with age, condition, what they wear, what they are NOT.

### BEATS
For each beat:

```
BEAT [N]: [BEAT NAME]
[What is happening, stated as action, not mood.]

VISUAL: [Shot size. Composition. What matters in frame. Why this angle.]

KEYFRAME: [Yes/No]

EMOTION: [Interior state. What the character knows or realizes.]

CAMERA MOVE: [Named move if keyframe. Omit if transition.]
```

### REFUSALS
Everything this scene is NOT. Written as directives.

## Example: Fisherman's Return

```
INT. KITCHEN — GOLDEN HOUR

SETTING
A kitchen in a working fishing village. Linoleum worn to white at the sink. 
Dishes don't match. Calendar from 2019 on the wall. The window faces the harbor. 
Not a restaurant. Not aspirational. Used every day.

CHARACTERS
ROSA. Seventies. Hands rope from salt water and bone knife work. Sweater darned 
at the elbow. No makeup. Not a professional. A survivor.

BEAT 1: ENTRANCE
Rosa pushes the door open. Wind from outside. The kitchen is colder than she 
expected. She pauses.

VISUAL: She enters frame right. The kitchen recedes left. The window is bright, 
a harsh rectangle of light. She is silhouetted.

KEYFRAME: Yes.

EMOTION: Shock at the cold. The familiar hostile.

CAMERA MOVE: Static. Rosa enters. The frame waits for her.

---

BEAT 2: THE HARBOR
She closes the door. In that moment, silence. She sees the boat in the harbor. 
She stops looking at the window and looks through it. Her eyes change.

VISUAL: Close on her face. The window behind her, out of focus, the boat a shape 
of light. She is in darkness. We see her understand something.

KEYFRAME: Yes.

EMOTION: The weight of knowing. Not sentimentality. Knowledge that is old and heavy.

CAMERA MOVE: Push in. Slow. Stop on her eyes.

---

BEAT 3: TRANSITION (not keyframe)
She walks to the sink. The camera doesn't move. She fills the basin. The water 
runs. We hear it. She doesn't speak.

VISUAL: Wide. She fills frame left. The window fills frame right. She is smaller 
than the harbor. The work is smaller than the waiting.

KEYFRAME: No.

CAMERA MOVE: (None. Static hold or dissolve to next beat.)

---

BEAT 4: HANDS
She plunges her hands in the water. The water is cold. Her forearms are scarred. 
We see the scars clearly.

VISUAL: Close on her hands in the water. Forearms filling frame. The scars are 
visible. The water distorts them. Older light, film light.

KEYFRAME: Yes.

EMOTION: This is work. This is what hands are for.

CAMERA MOVE: Dolly in. Very slow. Let us see the detail of the scars.

---

REFUSALS
Not a love story. Not slow-motion beauty. Not a meditation on memory. Not 
sentimental. Not soft focus. Not clean. Not an advertisement. Not her looking 
wistfully. The work is the emotion. Her hands show her age. The scars show use.
```

From this beat sheet, you identify 3 keyframes that need generation:
1. Entrance (static)
2. The harbor (push in)
3. Hands (dolly in)

The transition (beat 3) does not need a full generation; you can cut between keyframes or fade through it.

## Quality Gate

- [ ] Scene written as beat sheet, not as dialogue.
- [ ] Each beat states action and emotion separately.
- [ ] At least one explicit refusal present.
- [ ] Keyframes and transitions clearly marked.
- [ ] Each keyframe has a stated visual intent and camera move.
- [ ] Character descriptions written in directive voice.
- [ ] Location description includes materials, wear, light source.
- [ ] No vague mood language; every line is actionable.

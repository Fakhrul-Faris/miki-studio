# Shot Analysis — Kaufland "Take it Veggie" (~80s)

Source: `reference-library/kaufland-veggie/source.mp4`
Extracted via `tools/shot_extractor.py` (scene-cut detection + fixed-interval fallback). 29 real shots across 7 scenes/locations, many quick sitcom-style cuts. Given the volume, this writeup is organized **by scene** (each scene's camera/lighting/composition DNA is consistent across its several cuts) rather than one entry per shot — representative frame files are cited per scene for traceability.

**Reference use:** camera, lighting, composition and location DNA only. Do not carry over the Kaufland/Purland branding, product packaging, or on-screen dialogue/copy — those are the source ad's, not ours.

**Style summary:** a warm, feel-good multi-scene family comedy with a fake-out dark middle act (a vegetarian imagines/fears a spiral of misfortune for having no food options, which turns out to be misdirection before a happy supermarket-aisle resolution). Classic sitcom/dramedy multi-camera coverage throughout: wide masters, medium over-the-shoulders, tight reaction singles — nothing handheld-chaotic like the other two references in this library. Strong anchor for warm domestic/family-comedy beats.

## Scene 1 — Family Sunday lunch (≈0.00s–18.6s)
**Frames:** `shot01_*` through `shot09_*` (RS1–RS7)

- **Camera:** Classic multi-camera sitcom/dramedy coverage — a wide master (eye-level, static, deep focus holding the whole table) intercut with medium over-the-shoulder two-shots and tight reaction singles (85mm-feel, shallow DOF, subject sharp against a softly blurred background). No handheld movement; every setup is locked off.
- **Location:** A warm, cluttered retro family dining room — busy vintage floral/diamond wallpaper, oil landscape paintings in gilt frames, a wooden pendulum clock, dark wood sideboard and china cabinet, a round dining table with a lace tablecloth, orange-juice pitchers and floral china.
- **Proportion & composition:** Wide master centers the table with family members framed symmetrically on both sides, one or two figures in the near-foreground off-axis (a doorway entrance beat). Singles/OTS tightly frame one face at a time, three-quarter angle, with a warm-toned soft background of wallpaper and picture frames.
- **Mood:** Cozy, chatty, mildly awkward (a vegetarian guest among enthusiastic meat-eaters) — played as warm sitcom comedy, not tension.
- **Lighting:** Warm practical-feeling interior key (motivated by the room's own pendant lamp and window light), soft and even, minimal shadow, a slightly amber/golden color grade overall.

## Scene 2 — Thrown out (≈18.6s–20.4s)
**Frames:** `shot17_*`, `shot18_*`, `shot19_*`

- **Camera:** A high steep downward angle from above the front steps (almost bird's-eye) looking down at the character descending, then a direct top-down shot looking straight down as a thrown box lands on his head.
- **Location:** A brick exterior stairwell/porch with an iron railing, a parked car and bins below.
- **Proportion & composition:** Strong diagonal composition following the stairs down through the frame in the descending shot; the top-down box shot is symmetrical and centered, using the character's face and raised arms as the focal point.
- **Mood:** Abrupt, comedic rejection beat.
- **Lighting:** Cooler, flatter late-afternoon exterior light, less golden than Scene 1 — a deliberate tonal step down as the story turns sour.

## Scene 3 — Office birthday party → let go → night bus stop (≈20.4s–39.12s)
**Frames:** `shot20_*` through `shot38_*` (RS9–RS16)

- **Camera:** Handheld but restrained (small drift/breathing, not chase-style shake), medium-close coverage on the lead, occasionally pulling back to a wider shot showing the decorated office.
- **Location:** Three connected beats: (1) an open-plan office decorated for a birthday/leaving party — balloons, party hats, a buffet table of cold cuts and snacks, fluorescent ceiling panels, floor-to-ceiling windows; (2) a wood-paneled elevator interior, low-key and dim; (3) a night bus shelter — a glass-and-steel shelter with a cool fluorescent tube light, dark apartment blocks behind, a homeless man with a shopping cart alongside.
- **Proportion & composition:** Office coverage keeps the lead centered with the party's chaos softly blurred behind him; the elevator shot is a symmetrical, centered corridor composition (character walking straight toward camera); the bus-stop shot is a wide two-shot, both men seated/standing under the shelter's single overhead light, dark negative space filling the rest of the frame.
- **Mood:** Uncomfortable/deadpan at the party, quietly bleak in the elevator and bus stop — the "low point" of the fake-out arc.
- **Lighting:** Office: bright, slightly cool fluorescent, overexposed window backlight. Elevator: dim, warm-brown wood-paneled practicals only, high contrast. Bus stop: cold blue-white overhead fluorescent tube as the only light source against a near-black night exterior — a real day-for-scene lighting swing across the three beats.

## Cross-shot notes for prompt-writing

- **Lighting is a deliberate emotional signal across scenes**, not just a location property: warm/golden = safe and belonging (Scenes 1, 7), cooling/flattening = rejection and misfortune (Scenes 2–4), bright neutral retail = the "real world" pivot point (Scenes 5–6). Anchoring a Meikigo emotional arc could reuse this exact lighting-as-mood-signal technique.
- **Sitcom/dramedy coverage (static wide master + medium OTS + tight singles) is the default grammar** for the two "warm" scenes — a strong anchor for any dialogue-heavy, multi-character domestic or workplace scene that needs plain, legible coverage rather than a stylized camera move.
- **A single location can serve two narrative purposes via lighting/dressing alone**: the same supermarket aisle set reads first as "the problem" (Scene 5, meat wall) and then "the solution" (Scene 6, veggie wall) purely through what's on the shelves and a slightly different camera angle — no lighting or camera-language change needed between them.
- **The bus-stop and elevator beats use a single hard overhead practical as the only light source** — an efficient, moody way to shoot a brief low-point beat without a full lighting setup.

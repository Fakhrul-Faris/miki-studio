# Shot Analysis — "Runners (Involuntarios)" (Perenteryl, ~32.6s)

Source: `reference-library/runners-involuntarios/source.mp4`
Extracted via `tools/shot_extractor.py` (scene-cut detection + fixed-interval fallback). 11 real shots, 10 hard cuts (two long shots were subdivided by the extractor for coverage but confirmed continuous by comparing their sub-frames).

**Reference use:** camera, lighting, composition and location DNA only. Do not carry over the Perenteryl branding, product box design, or on-screen Spanish copy — those are the source ad's, not ours.

**Style summary:** a comedy-chase premise (people with an urgent bathroom need "involuntarily" running through daily life) shot almost entirely handheld with an extreme wide-angle lens, low camera height and continuous tracking-in-front-of-the-runner coverage. Gritty, naturalistic, slightly desaturated everyday lighting — the opposite end of the spectrum from Cloudmonster's glossy slow-motion commercial look. Useful anchor for any "urgent/comedic physical panic" beat.

## Cross-shot notes for prompt-writing

- **Two camera languages inside one ad:** exterior daylight chase shots (1–4, 7's back half, 11) use hard direct sun with lens flare; interior shots (5, 6, 7's front half, 8–10) use flat, cool fluorescent light. Keep these paired with their location type when anchoring — don't mix the exterior sun-flare look onto an interior beat.
- **The extreme-low-angle continuous front-tracking shot (Shots 5 and 7) is the signature device** — a single unbroken handheld take that stays locked on one running character's face for 6+ seconds, including a location transition inside Shot 7. Strong anchor for any "sustained urgency/comedy run" beat.
- **Wide-angle barrel distortion is a deliberate, consistent lens choice** across nearly every shot — carry this over explicitly (not just "wide lens" but "visible edge distortion, extreme wide/action-cam feel") since it's what gives the piece its frantic, documentary-comedy texture.
- **Short cutaway inserts (Shot 6, 1.17s) work as connective tissue** between two long continuous takes — useful pattern for hiding a location/character switch without a jarring hard cut.
- **End card reuses a mid-ad character shot as its background** (Shot 11 revisits Shot 3's framing) rather than cutting to a flat graphic card — a possible technique to reuse for a Meikigo end card that still feels of a piece with the rest of the spot.

# Meikigo content brainstorm

## Part 1: 34 new content ideas

Each idea lists the concept, why it reaches shop owners (not just views), the effort level (L/M/H), and how to re-skin it for other verticals. ⚠️ marks ideas that could backfire.

### A. Formats borrowed from other industries

**1. Shop Autopsy** (from air-crash investigation channels)
- **Concept:** Rebuild one lost customer minute by minute from a real story: "14:02 walks in. 14:09 asks 'berapa lama lagi?' 14:21 leaves. Cause of death: no visible wait time."
- **Why it works for B2B:** Owners recognise their own shop in the timeline. The format pitches the problem without needing to show the product.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Lost table (restaurant), lost walk-in (nail salon), lost appointment (clinic).

**2. Shop Floor Commentary** (from football commentary)
- **Concept:** Run play-by-play commentary over a top-down animated shop on a Saturday. "OH, a TURNOVER! Walk-in lost to the shop across the road!" Stats come up on screen at half-time.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's comedy that only works if you know how a shop floor runs, so the audience it attracts is owners.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Any floor with a queue.

**3. Rate My Price List** (from "financial advisor reacts to your budget")
- **Concept:** Owners send in their price board. You review the structure, anchoring, combo pricing and the services that are "secretly losing money per chair-hour."
- **Why it works for B2B:** It gives owners free consulting. A shop owner who submits a price list is effectively a warm lead.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Menu engineering (restaurants), treatment menus (beauty, clinics).
- ⚠️ Opt-in only, and never mock the shop.

**4. Shop Tour: Where Does the Rent Go?** (from real-estate tours)
- **Concept:** Tour an animated shop and price every square metre. "This chair costs RM38 a day before anyone sits in it."
- **Why it works for B2B:** It speaks directly to per-staff economics.
- **Effort:** L–M
- **Re-skin:** Per table, per grooming station, per treatment bed.

**5. The Case of the Missing Ringgit** (from true crime)
- **Concept:** A till-reconciliation mystery, such as commission that doesn't add up or a DuitNow payment nobody can match to a service. The investigation ends at the system gap.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It touches one of the biggest hidden owner pains, trust between owner and staff.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Universal.
- ⚠️ High risk. If staff are framed as the thieves, barbers (your users) turn on you. The villain must always be the process, never a person.

**6. Wildlife Documentary: The Malaysian Walk-in** (from nature documentaries)
- **Concept:** Narrate customer behaviour in a calm documentary voice: "The walk-in circles twice, checks his phone, and retreats to the mamak."
- **Why it works for B2B:** It turns owner observations into a story owners can share.
- **Effort:** L, since it reuses your animation pipeline.
- **Re-skin:** Diners, pet owners, clinic patients.

### B. Community and UGC

**7. Rules on the Wall** (strongest candidate for a signature format, see Part 3)
- **Concept:** Collect real handwritten shop signs, like "NO BOOKING SATURDAY", "CASH ONLY" or "JANGAN TANYA BILA SIAP". Decode the pain behind each sign and work out what that rule costs the shop.
- **Why it works for B2B:** Every sign is a pain point the owner chose to make public. Followers can supply them for free, and the format is very Malaysian.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Every vertical has signs.

**8. Animated Voice Notes** (from StoryCorps and Blank on Blank)
- **Concept:** Owners send a 30-second voice note about their worst day, and you animate it.
- **Why it works for B2B:** The real voice is the proof, and your animation pipeline is the advantage. It also adds a "feel understood" format.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Any owner can send a voice note.
- ⚠️ Get written consent, and offer to anonymise or re-voice.

**9. WhatsApp Archaeology**
- **Concept:** Show real booking chats with names blurred: "bro ada slot x?" at 11:47pm, 9 messages to book one fade, the customer who replies 3 days later.
- **Why it works for B2B:** Every owner has these exact chats.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Universal. Malaysian SMEs run on WhatsApp.

**10. Shop Policy Court**
- **Concept:** A recurring "case." Should you charge no-show deposits? Should walk-ins wait behind bookings? Should regulars skip the queue? Animate both sides, let owners argue in the comments, and deliver a verdict next episode with the vote and data.
- **Why it works for B2B:** Owners love arguing about policy. It differs from polls because it's a series with a record of past verdicts.
- **Effort:** L–M
- **Re-skin:** Swap in each vertical's policies.

**11. Tag Your Barber** (end-customer content as a route to owners)
- **Concept:** Consumer-facing tips like "How to never wait at the barber", etiquette, and "what your barber wishes you knew." Customers tag their barber.
- **Why it works for B2B:** End customers carry the content to the owners for you. The tag is the distribution channel.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Tag your groomer, tag your nail tech.

**12. Apprentice Diaries**
- **Concept:** An animated series from a junior barber's point of view: commission maths, waiting for chair time, getting skipped for walk-ins.
- **Why it works for B2B:** Today's barbers are tomorrow's shop owners, and it builds loyalty among the people who touch the tablet every day.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Junior chef, junior groomer.

### C. Data-driven

**13. Google Maps Review Mining**
- **Concept:** Analyse around 1,000 Klang Valley barbershop reviews and present "The top 5 reasons Malaysians give a barber 1 star." My guess is that waiting outranks bad haircuts.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's problem-first content backed by evidence. It's also a press pitch and sales collateral.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Run the same script per vertical.
- ⚠️ Never name or screenshot a specific shop's bad reviews.

**14. Malaysia Queue Index**
- **Concept:** A monthly data drop on busiest hours, the payday effect around the 25th, and the post-Raya surge. Start from Google "popular times" and surveys, then add pilot data later.
- **Why it works for B2B:** You become the source people quote, which is credibility that ads can't buy.
- **Effort:** M–H
- **Re-skin:** A per-vertical index.

**15. The Shop Calendar Nobody Prints**
- **Concept:** Cover the non-obvious demand patterns: the Friday-prayer gap from 1 to 2:30pm, school reopening, Champions League nights, the week before and after payday.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's useful scheduling knowledge that owners save. It's distinct from Raya/CNY content.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Each vertical has its own rhythm.

**16. A Barber's Real Hourly Wage**
- **Concept:** Break down the economics of commission vs. chair rental vs. salary.
- **Why it works for B2B:** Barbers will flood the comments.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Universal.
- ⚠️ This can alienate owners if it reads as "you're underpaid." Frame it as "which model works for which shop."

### D. Collaborations

**17. Supplier Station Check**
- **Concept:** Co-create with local pomade brands and clipper distributors: "What pros keep at the station, plus the one thing that isn't a product." The answer is the queue system.
- **Why it works for B2B:** This is the Yellow Pages principle from the thread. Suppliers already have the owners' trust and their WhatsApp numbers.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Restaurant suppliers, beauty distributors.

**18. Barber Academy "First Shop" Series**
- **Concept:** Partner with academies to make "Opening your first shop: the 10 things nobody teaches."
- **Why it works for B2B:** Students are future owners at their most open moment, before they have a system.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Culinary schools, beauty academies.

**19. Same Problem, Next Door**
- **Concept:** Split-screen a barber and the nasi lemak stall beside them handling the same lunch rush.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It tests the restaurant audience now and quietly sets up Phase 2.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** This idea is itself the re-skin engine.

**20. SME Compliance Explainers with an Accountant**
- **Concept:** Explain LHDN e-invoicing, SST and record-keeping for small shops.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's a real, timely anxiety for owners, and accountants can share it to their clients.
- **Effort:** L–M
- **Re-skin:** Universal.
- ⚠️ Getting the facts wrong is a serious risk. E-invoicing thresholds and phase dates have changed several times, so have the accountant verify every number and date each episode.

### E. Malaysian and cultural

**21. Bahasa Kedai**
- **Concept:** A glossary of shop-floor phrases, such as "Lepas ni saya ya?", "Abang, berapa orang lagi?" and "Nanti datang balik." Each phrase is decoded as a system failure.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's a hyper-local, insider language people want to share. It can run in BM, Chinese and Tamil versions.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Each vertical's own phrases.

**22. Three Shops, One Street**
- **Concept:** Show how an old-school kedai gunting, an Indian barbershop and a hipster fade studio each handle a queue.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It shows respect for different operating styles.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Kopitiam vs. mamak vs. café.
- ⚠️ There's a stereotyping risk. Research it with real shops from each community, and celebrate rather than caricature.

**23. Kampung vs. KL**
- **Concept:** The same shop problem in Kota Bharu and in Bangsar.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It shows you aren't a "KL startup," which signals that later phases will cover all of Malaysia.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Universal.

### F. Interactive

**24. Queue Puzzle**
- **Concept:** "8 customers, 3 barbers, 2 bookings, 1 no-show. Minimise the wait." Owners answer in the comments, and you reveal the best solution next post.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's like chess puzzles for owners, who love being right. The people who comment are your target audience.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Tables, grooming slots, treatment rooms.

**25. You're the Owner** (choose your own path)
- **Concept:** A barber calls in sick on a Saturday. Should you (A) cancel bookings, (B) absorb the walk-ins or (C) call a freelancer? The next post shows the consequences.
- **Why it works for B2B:** Decision-making is the owner's real job.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Universal.

**26. Guess the Shop**
- **Concept:** Show an anonymised day heatmap and have viewers guess the shop type, the day, or whether it's a good or bad week.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It builds data literacy and makes your future dashboard feel familiar.
- **Effort:** L once pilot data exists.
- **Re-skin:** Built in.

### G. Founder and designer angle

This is your edge, since you're the designer.

**27. The Killed Features Graveyard**
- **Concept:** Show a screen you designed, then the barber who said "useless," then why you deleted it.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It proves you listen. It's narrower than general building-in-public and maps directly to "feel understood."
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Each vertical's feedback.

**28. The Wet-Hands Test**
- **Concept:** Film only hands. A barber with a clipper in one hand and gel on their fingers tries to check someone in with one thumb, against a timer.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's real UX research footage and only takes minimal filming.
- **Effort:** L–M
- **Re-skin:** Greasy hands (kitchen), wet hands (groomer), gloves (clinic, tattoo).

**29. The Shadow Day**
- **Concept:** The founder spends a full day in a shop and tallies everything: "'Berapa lama lagi?' was asked 23 times. 4 people left. 11 WhatsApp bookings came in mid-cut." Present it as animated field notes.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's discovery and content in one. Owners respect someone who sat in the shop.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Repeat the same day per vertical.

**30. The Design Crit**
- **Concept:** Take a real shop's analogue system (a booking book or whiteboard) and critique it as a designer, respectfully: "This is actually brilliant, and here's why."
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's the opposite of "roast the old way." Praise builds trust.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Universal.

### H. Series structures

**31. Casting the 10**
- **Concept:** Run the pilot selection as a public casting call: the criteria, the applications and the shortlist process.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's content and the conversion engine at once (see Part 4).
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Do a new casting for each vertical's pilot.
- ⚠️ Never show rejections publicly.

**32. One Chair, 52 Weeks**
- **Concept:** Follow one pilot chair's numbers every week for a year.
- **Why it works for B2B:** Long-term proof that builds as the series runs.
- **Effort:** L per episode.
- **Re-skin:** One table, one grooming station.

**33. The Number of the Week**
- **Concept:** One stat, one shop, 15 seconds. "RM340: what one no-show cost Shop X this month."
- **Why it works for B2B:** It's low-effort filler between bigger pieces, and it keeps the posting cadence without UI content.
- **Effort:** L
- **Re-skin:** Universal.

**34. Ask the Tablet**
- **Concept:** The counter tablet becomes a recurring animated character that answers owners' questions, dry and a little judgmental.
- **Why it works for B2B:** It personifies the product without being an ad. It's different from human characters because the tablet *is* the product.
- **Effort:** M
- **Re-skin:** Same tablet, different shop.

## Part 3: Signature repeatable format, 3 options

Belzona's mechanic is "a visible broken state, a satisfying process, then proof." For software, the "broken thing" has to be something you can see.

**Option A: Rules on the Wall** ⭐ my pick
- **How it runs:** Show a real sign, decode the hidden cost, then the old sign comes down and a better rule goes up (redesigned by you, print-ready).
- **Pros:**
  - The ending is a literal before/after object, so it's the closest thing to Belzona's repair.
  - Supply is endless and community-sourced.
  - It's very Malaysian, cheap to make, and works in any vertical.
  - Owners get a free redesigned sign.
- **Cons:**
  - It depends on submissions, so collect 20 signs before launching.
  - It's less "satisfying" in motion.
  - It shows the product only indirectly.

**Option B: Shop Autopsy**
- **How it runs:** One lost customer or lost ringgit, traced to a "cause of death" card, then the fix.
- **Pros:**
  - It's the most emotionally gripping of the three, and works best on "feel understood."
  - It's strong for B2B credibility.
- **Cons:**
  - It needs a steady supply of real stories.
  - It can feel negative over time.
  - It takes medium effort per episode.

**Option C: Queue Puzzle**
- **How it runs:** A daily scheduling puzzle, owners solve it in the comments, and you reveal the best answer.
- **Pros:**
  - It costs the least to make and drives the most comments from owners.
  - It builds a loyal "regulars" crowd like Belzona's fans, and the algorithm likes the comments.
- **Cons:**
  - The product almost never appears.
  - It may attract puzzle fans instead of owners.
  - It can get repetitive without escalating difficulty.

**Recommendation:** Make A the signature and run C as the twice-weekly filler.

## Part 4: Turning content into pilot signups without sounding like an ad

1. **Use the casting call as the CTA.** Say "We're choosing 10 shops. Here's who we're looking for," not "sign up." Public, specific criteria create scarcity and self-selection.
2. **Make the free deliverable the funnel.** Offer a free sign redesign, price-list review or one-hour shadow audit with a written report. Each one gets you a face-to-face conversation, and that's where pilots actually close.
3. **Keep the call to action out of the video.** Use a pinned comment like "Comment SIGN and I'll WhatsApp you." Use WhatsApp, not a link-in-bio form.
4. **Give founding-member status they can see.** Lock in pricing for life, and name something in the product after their shop, like a default template.
5. **Offer exposure in exchange for help.** Pilot shops appear in One Chair, 52 Weeks, which is free marketing for them.
6. **Track the right numbers.** Count owner DMs per week and owner comments per post, and ignore views.

## Part 5: Challenging your assumptions

**1. "General audience first, then pivot" is the biggest flaw.** TikTok's algorithm learns who your audience is from what you post. General comedy trains it to show you to consumers. When you pivot, those followers won't convert, and the algorithm has to relearn. You'd be building an audience you'll later have to abandon.

**2. Comedy isn't the problem. The audience is.** Keep the comedy, but make it *insider* comedy that only shop owners and barbers fully get. Insider jokes filter for the right audience. It's worth checking Miki's Shift against this: would a non-barber laugh just as hard? If so, it's too general.

**3. You need 10 conversations, not 100k followers.** Social media is a weak channel for a 10-shop pilot. Walking into shops, Facebook barber groups and supplier introductions will get you there faster. During the pilot phase, content's real job is **credibility when you walk in** ("here's our page") and starting conversations. It isn't a lead engine yet.

**4. "Brand awareness" is too vague to aim at.** Replace it with something like "500 Klang Valley shop owners recognise us." You can measure that, and it changes what you'd make.

**5. Pick platforms by persona, not habit.** Owners aged 35–55 probably live on Facebook and in WhatsApp groups. Young barbers live on Instagram, which is their portfolio, and TikTok. One format can be cut three ways, but test where *owners* actually reply.

**6. Don't over-optimise for re-skinning yet.** Specific beats general on niche platforms. A deeply barber-specific post beats a generic "service shop" post. Make the format reusable, keep the content specific, and re-skin when Phase 2 arrives.

**7. Judge each format after about 10 posts**, by owner DMs. Expect noisy data; with an audience this small, a single owner DM is a meaningful signal.

## Part 6: Diagram explainer series (chosen direction)

Short (under 60s) diagram explainers for TikTok and Instagram, inspired by docu-essay channels
like ABSTRACT, Fern and unpred.ictable. Each episode takes a story from business, psychology or
economics, reveals the hidden system, then turns to a Malaysian shop: "your shop has the same
problem." Every story points back to a problem Meikigo solves, without pitching the product.

Decisions so far:
- No series name. Each episode opens on a hook that really hooks.
- English only, voiced with ElevenLabs.
- Diagram-only, blank background, serious tone.
- 14 rotating lanes, one new story every post, same 55-second skeleton.

Everything for the series lives in **`miki-explained/`**:
- `00_bible/series-plan.md`: skeleton, hook patterns, lanes, rules
- `00_bible/visual-system.md`: the look, plus the After Effects kit to build once
- `00_bible/voice.md`: ElevenLabs direction and the 130-word budget
- `00_bible/episode-template.md`: copy into each episode folder
- `03_ideas/backlog.md`: the first 16 stories, mapped to what Meikigo solves
- `05_calendar/calendar.md`: the posting tracker

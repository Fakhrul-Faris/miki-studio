# BIBLE-B — Style B: the flat world of objects and light

Built 2026-11-09 from one-by-one art-director notes on 213 handpicked images and 33 clips (`notes-B/C01–C04.md`,
`M01–M02.md`, `DIGEST.md`). Convictions, not averages. Plain words. **Draft, not yet tested** (test after BIBLE-C, per
the user's "styles share techniques" decision).

---

## 1. Point of view

**Style B believes every business mechanism can be drawn as one invented everyday object, and every feeling as one
shape of light.** The world is flat: 2–4 colours, no outlines or thin black line, grain on top, light and shadow drawn as
hard shapes. The mechanism is a machine, a suitcase, a see-saw, a tag; the time and mood come from where the sun patch
falls; the words and numbers live on the objects themselves.

## 2. Convictions

**B1. One invented object per mechanism.** Abstract systems (payments, margins, trade-offs) become a single everyday
object you could touch: a dispenser, conveyor arch, see-saw, suitcase, Jenga tower, swing tag. Ids: core-003, 061, 010,
050, 043, 052; type-004. On screen: each script beat gets one object; its screen/slot/tag says the line.

**B2. Light is a flat shape, and it is the composition.** The sun patch, window wedge or shadow band is the largest
shape in the frame; the subject sits inside it or exactly on its edge. Ids: light-012, 016, 018, 028, core-009, 031.
On screen: draw light as 1–2 shapes (bright source, pale landing); move the patch to show time passing.

**B3. Two colours for light and shadow; the lit side can melt into the ground.** Ids: light-016, cam-022, places-007,
light-022. On screen: pick a light colour and a shadow colour per scene; objects in shadow merge into it.

**B4. Words and numbers live on the world's own objects.** Price tags, machine screens, shop signs, bus boards,
chips spelling a number. Ids: type-004, core-003, 033, places-009, 010, motion-019. On screen: the script's key number
appears on the business's object (tag, receipt, menu board, signboard), not floating.

**B5. Warm only where life is.** A one-colour city/night; warm windows, doorways, headlights, the goods. Ids: light-001,
003, places-022, cam-003, people-008. On screen: the business glows; the world stays one hue.

**B6. One saturated subject in a quiet field** (shared with A-C1). Ids: core-036 (one red roof), light-027 (one lit
window), places-011, core-043. On screen: the subject is the only saturated façade/object.

**B7. One shape carries every handover.** The through-line colour/shape moves from scene to scene and becomes the next
scene's object or background. Ids: motion-017, 011, 012, 022 (and EX27's orange line). On screen: each transition is the
accent shape growing, morphing or sliding into the next set.

**B8. Places are true and local.** Signboards, wires, bikes, awnings, real lettering; flat front elevations of shop
rows. Ids: places-003, 006, 010, cam-006, core-035. On screen: a Malaysian shop row in flat elevation with real signs.

## 3. The eye
- The light patch/shadow edge is the strongest pointer: put the subject there (light-018, 028).
- One small object on a big repeated pattern (lanes, shelves, windows): light-028, core-062, light-027.
- Give the sky/ground two thirds of a 9:16 frame so a small building becomes a hero (core-045, cam-012).
- Crop repeated elements off both frame edges to make them endless (cam-001).
- Top-down for systems and flow (core-035, 049, 031); flat front elevation for "the street" (places-010, 007);
  low worm's-eye for power (cam-015, 022); cross-section for "how it works inside" (cam-005, 006).

## 4. Light as storytelling (flat)
- Light = a shape, never a gradient smear: window wedges, sun bands, shadow stripes, lamp cones (core-020).
- Time of day by where the patch falls and how long the shadows are (light-018, 022, 013).
- Rim lines in the warm colour on the lit edge of figures/objects (light-001, 009).
- Absence by light: a smoking cigarette, an empty lit desk (light-006, 012).
- Reveal by moving the patch (A shares this: reveal by light).

## 5. Colour as meaning
- Per episode: a ground/paper colour + 2–3 colours + one alarm (EX27 rule stands), defined as **ground/figure pairs**
  (color-004, 011) so any scene can swap ground and figure.
- Light colour and shadow colour are chosen per scene (B3); the shadow colour is one saturated hue, not grey (light-011).
- Warm muted base pairs work: warm black #1D1D1B + seashell #EAE4DA (color-003).
- The accent goes on the topic/goods, never on people (people-031, 008).
- Accent colour chosen from the brand and the story's meaning (user decision, same as A).

## 6. Surface and texture
- Flat fills; optional fine grain over everything (most north stars) and grain inside gradient fills for props
  (core-030, props-009).
- Two line families: (a) thin black line + hard black flat shadow (Burford: core-003, 050, 044) for invented machines;
  (b) no line, shapes only (Favre/Patterson) for places and light scenes. One family per episode.
- Optional misregistration (one colour layer offset) for a printed feel (props-003).
- Never: long 45° icon shadows (light-004, 010, 015), glossy 3D icons (props-006), holographic gradients (props-015).

## 7. Scale and people
- Objects and icons only (house rule). If a person must appear: primitive shapes (dome torso, rectangle legs, dot head),
  no face, no hands, back view, one held posture, identity by one item (people-007, 018, 019, 026, 030).
- Tiny silhouettes are fine at distance, with a warm rim line (light-001).
- Absence and posture tell people: empty chairs, a smoking ashtray, a lean (light-006, people-030).
- Scale jumps: a giant familiar object (Jenga, see-saw) with tiny workers; huge foreground token over a tiny map
  (core-043, 049).

## 8. Camera and motion
- Mostly locked frames; the world or light moves, objects hold (core-049, motion-005).
- Objects enter by growing from their base (motion-004), sliding out of a slot/arch (core-052), dropping onto their own
  shadow (props-009); never pop.
- Idle micro-motion on held frames: a rock, bob, slosh, smoke curl (props-007, light-006).
- Lists: slot-reel scroll with the chosen item filled (type-002), or items whip in and land, loudest last.
- Timing: 0.5 s ease-in-out "io" curve; snappy entries, 1–3 s holds; no bounce (EX27 rule stands). Avoid 0.3 s
  title-sequence cutting.

## 10. Never
- The blue corporate slide explainer (motion-003), stock "Humaaans" characters (people-016), clip-art businessmen
  (people-004/005), numbered-road infographic templates without an idea (core-038).
- Long-shadow 2014 icons, glossy 3D money icons, holographic sticker gradients.
- Cut-paper collage (color-006) — retired.
- Faces, hands, walking figures (house rule).
- Colour everywhere with no hierarchy (AI streets: cam-002, 013, 014).
- Anti-slop: if a frame could be a slide, it fails.

## 11. Adopt / Adapt / Drop
| conviction / device | verdict | how |
|---|---|---|
| Invented machine/object per mechanism (B1) | **Adopt** | hand-authored SVG iso objects (thin line + hard shadow family); HyperFrames |
| Flat light shapes (B2, B3) | **Adopt** | SVG polygons with blend/opacity; animate their points/position for time |
| Words on objects (B4) | **Adopt** | type set on tags/screens/signs in SVG; brand logo file on signboards |
| Warm only where life is (B5) | **Adopt** | palette rule + emissive-looking flat shapes |
| One shape carries handovers (B7) | **Adopt** | EX27 already proves it; extend with morph/grow-into-background |
| True local shop rows (B8) | **Adapt** | build a reusable flat Malaysian shophouse kit (5-foot way, signboards, aircon, wires) once |
| Grain / misregistration | **Adopt** | grain overlay exists (EX27); misregistration = offset colour layer |
| Cross-section building | **Adapt** | SVG cutaway with 3 floors; heavy per episode, reuse a kit |
| Top-down maps with routes | **Adopt** | SVG map layers, routes as paths with dash animation |
| Primitive-shape people | **Adapt** | only if a beat truly needs a crowd; back views, no faces/hands |
| Character acting, faceted portrait figures, painterly scenes | **Drop** | off-limits / not buildable by hand-coded SVG |
| Fast title-sequence pacing | **Drop** | wrong pace for 20–30 s explainers |

## 12. Decisions (user, 2026-11-09)
Carried over (2026-11-08): accent from brand + meaning; match cut allowed in identical framing; day to explain, dusk
for payoff; styles share techniques.
1. **No line** is the default drawing family (Favre/Patterson shapes). The thin-line family is not used.
2. **Grain on, kept subtle.**
3. **Cut-open buildings are allowed** as a recurring set, drawn flat (so they still differ from A's modelled cutaways).

## 15. Craft methods (adopted 2026-11-11; full text in `00_bible/craft-fundamentals.md`)

- **Build order:** forms → materials (the two-tone ramp, grain) → the light shape → effects. A mechanism or a colour
  script never replaces object density (the EX34 lesson).
- **Authored forms in Three.js** (from threejs-game-skills' authoring recipes): build objects from extrusions
  (`ExtrudeGeometry` with bevel for signboards, shutters, tags), lathes (cups, lamps, bottles), tubes (wires, pipes,
  handles), custom buffers; repeated small parts (louvres, tiles, bolts, windows) by **instancing** and shared
  geometry/materials. A box with a texture is a placeholder, not an object.
- **Working parts and asymmetry** make an invented object read without a caption: hinge, slot, handle, screen, feet.
- **Story clusters** of 3–7 small readable objects per beat, as EX33 does; background as silhouette.
- **Greyscale check:** the two tones must separate the subject from the world in greyscale.
- **Measured:** `frame_metrics.py --style B` must not report BELOW-BASELINE (contrast ≥ 110, one colour ≤ 0.37);
  FLAT is off for B (flat is the style). Anchors: `00_bible/anchors/B-*`.

# BIBLE-A — Style A: the white model world

Built 2026-11-08 from one-by-one art-director notes on 458 handpicked images and 41 clips
(`notes-A/C01–C08.md`, `M01–M02.md`, `DIGEST.md`). Built from **convictions, not averages**: every rule below comes
from decisions in the ★5 pieces, never from measurements. Written in plain words.

Status: **draft, questions answered 2026-11-08, not yet tested.** No rush on the test (user, 2026-11-08): Styles A, B and C
share traits, advantages and techniques with each other, so quality stays on par across styles; test once the B and C
bibles exist.

## 1. Point of view

**Style A believes a business story is easiest to understand when the whole world is a quiet white model and only the
thing the line is about is real.** The real part gets the colour, the material, the light, the detail and the people;
everything else is plain white clay that sets the scale and steps back. One physical object (a coin, a box, a marble)
carries the story from scene to scene, and the camera calmly follows it.

## 2. Convictions

**C1. One subject is real; the world is white model.**
Why: the eye goes to the only saturated or textured thing in a blank field instantly, with no arrow or label needed.
Ids: core-078, 080, 089, cam-036, light-052.
On screen: every building, prop and person is matte white clay except the business/thing the current line names; that
one gets its colour, real material and the most detail. When the line moves on, the old subject fades back to white and
the new one fills in.

**C2. Detail is attention.**
Why: we look where the work was spent. Equal detail everywhere = no focus (the AI-stock refs prove it).
Ids: cam-036, cam-010, core-129, light-044 (one cat on a clay façade).
On screen: the subject block carries the true everyday clutter (aircon units, scooters, delivery boxes, hand-written
signs, plants, people in poses); the neighbours are plain boxes with soft corners.

**C3. Show the inside of exactly one thing.**
Why: a cutaway answers "how does it work?" without a diagram, and opening only one object keeps the point sharp.
Ids: core-001, light-032, core-087 (slice pulled out beside the whole), light-018 (x-ray).
On screen: one wall slides away, one box turns to glass, or one object x-rays for a beat — never the whole scene.

**C4. The invisible thing becomes one physical token in the accent colour, and it travels.**
Why: money, demand, coverage and time are abstract; a token you can see roll, stack and drop makes them concrete, and
following it gives the episode one through-line.
Ids: motion-025 (marbles), light-042 (orange ball), light-035 (wifi domes), light-009 (sound ribbons), C02 core-067.
On screen: pick one token per episode (coins for money, marbles for customers, boxes for orders, a translucent dome for
reach). It is the only accent-coloured thing that moves, and it carries every handover.

**C5. Lock the place; change one thing.**
Why: when the camera and set hold still, the eye sees only what changed, so before/after and setup/payoff read in one
glance.
Ids: core-001→005, core-011→012, core-020→017→021, core-073 (route drawn, then the fire truck uses it), core-151→152,
people-014→033 (clay street → coloured street).
On screen: same framing for problem and fix; swap the material, the light or the graphic layer on the word.

**C6. Colour has a job, and its area tells the story.**
Why: colour reads before shape. If each colour means one thing, the viewer learns the code in seconds; if the accent grows
or shrinks, the plot is visible without words.
Ids: core-071 (white place / natural goods / red people), color-039 (pink heart grows in a green world), color-001,
core-162–170 (pink = polluted, red → blue = damaged → fixed).
On screen: write the colour jobs into each episode plan before building. The accent's screen area tracks the stakes:
small at the hook, largest at the payoff.

**C7. Light tells you where to look and how to feel.**
Why: a white world without strong light looks dead (light-051 vs the flat AI renders); a warm light in a cool scene is an
invitation.
Ids: core-129, core-070 (sun stripes), light-071→072 (reveal by switching a light on), light-052 (haze), color-025.
On screen: see section 4.

**C8. The brand lives inside the world.**
Why: viewers watch on mute; a logo on a shopfront, roof or product is read as part of the place, not as an ad card.
Ids: light-032 (3D roof letters), people-033 (shop sign in the street), core-130 (name repeated on signs and awnings),
places-003 (name along the wall tops).
On screen: frame 1 shows the real business logo **on the building** (roof letters, sign board, awning) — satisfies the
house rule "real logo from frame 1".

## 3. The eye

- **Hierarchy by surface, three levels:** accent/real material (subject) → hatched, outlined or lightly tinted (context
  that matters) → plain white (everything else). (cam-026, core-079, 088)
- **The subject can be small.** Isolation does the work: a small hero with empty white around it wins (core-017, 056,
  light-042, cam-005). Don't scale up to make something important — clear the space around it.
- **Crop with intent.** Product/prop beats crop tight until the one feature fills the frame (light-001, props-003); place
  beats sit high and wide.
- **Negative space points the way:** leave empty space in the direction something is moving (core-040, light-016).
- **Pointers without colour** when the accent is busy: an odd shape among repeats (core-116), a ring on the ground plane
  (core-173), particle density (core-106), real-lens focus (light-052, cam-035).
- **Graphics live on the ground plane**: rings, routes, names and arrows are laid on the floor in perspective, never
  floating cards (core-141, 166, 173, cam-019).
- **Numbers go where the thing is**: the stat appears in the space the problem used to fill (core-021), above the object it
  counts (core-185).

## 4. Light as storytelling

- **Key light:** one strong, low, warm sun from the side — long soft shadows across the white clay. This is what makes a
  white world feel physical (light-051, cam-012, people-016). Flat ambient light alone is banned.
- **Warm inside, cool outside:** the subject's windows/doorway glow warm amber while the world sits in cool daylight or
  dusk (core-129, motion-009). Use at the payoff or the "this is the shop" beat.
- **Reveal by light:** a beat can start with the subject as a silhouette and switch the key on to reveal it (light-071→072).
  Animate light, not the object (light-029).
- **Haze for depth:** fog/haze fades the far world so the near subject stands out (light-052, light-034).
- **Light can split a frame into feelings:** one subject inside the warm patch, the other outside (color-025, 022).
- **Raking light for texture** on close-ups of real materials (light-077, people-016).
- **Light marks the subject quietly** when colour is in use elsewhere: a faint warm tint around one building (light-043).

## 5. Colour as meaning

- **The rule:** grey, black and white **plus one bright colour** (color-036 states it outright).
- **World:** warm-white clay (not blue-white), with soft grey shadows and AO. A neutral grey ramp keeps values steady.
- **Accent:** one per episode, chosen from the brand and the story's meaning (§12.4). It goes only on: the subject, the
  travelling token, and the one key number. Never on decoration.
- **Real materials as a second channel:** the subject may keep its true material (brick, wood, copper, the shop's real
  colours) instead of flat paint (core-089, 080, 028). Treat "real material" and "accent" as the same tier — one subject
  gets one or the other, never both plus extras.
- **Danger/problem:** may use a second, opposite colour only for the problem beat (core-001 fire, motion-025 pink bowl),
  and it disappears once fixed (red → blue, core-170).
- **Plan colour across the episode** (color-001, 008, 039): hook in a calm key, the twist gets its own block, the payoff
  in a new key. Draw a 5–7-swatch colour script per episode before building.
- **Test palettes on locked frames** before committing (color-004, 041): 3 options across the key frames, pick a row.

## 6. Surface and material (words that become Blender settings)

- **Clay:** warm-white base (roughly #EDE9E3 → value 0.92), roughness 0.6–0.7, no specular sheen, a faint grain/noise
  bump at ~0.02 strength so close-ups don't look plastic (props-003, Thomas 102).
- **Edges:** every building and prop has rounded edges (bevel 2–4% of the object size) — soft corners read as a crafted
  model, sharp ones as CG (Thomas 104, people-014).
- **Contact shadows:** strong ambient occlusion in corners and under objects; it is what lets a white world read.
- **Real-material subject:** PBR brick, wood, copper, enamel, fabric — honest and slightly worn (core-089, 028, 029).
- **Glass/x-ray:** a frosted, slightly blue translucent version of the object for cutaway beats (core-001, 019,
  light-018); use for one object at a time (render cost).
- **Ground:** a seamless warm-white floor; optional plinth/tile under the subject (core-069, motion-005) — no grid lines
  (memory `v4-no-floor-lines`), except where the grid is the idea (light-042, then thin and grey).
- **Lens:** real depth of field (f/2.8–5.6 equivalent) on close shots; tilt-shift on high wides to sell "model on a table"
  (cam-035, places-012).

## 7. Scale and people

- **People hold poses** (house rule; also how every north star uses them: tiny posed figures, people-014/033, cam-036).
  No walking, gesturing or acting unless a raea motion-capture test is approved.
- **Faceless and simple:** a smooth head with no features, or a carved-wood/linen look (people-001, 012, core-025).
  Identity comes from **one item**: a cap, an apron, a hard hat, a name badge, a tudung (people-003, light-079, 095).
- **Real fabric on simple bodies** keeps them believable without the uncanny valley (core-026, people-006).
- **Shoot from behind or three-quarter back** when a figure is close (people-008).
- **Empty furniture tells the beat without figures:** empty chairs = waiting, a full queue of stools = demand (places-006).
- **Crowds are texture:** many tiny posed figures where life happens, none elsewhere (core-085, cam-036).
- **Scale jumps are allowed for metaphors:** a giant product in the courtyard (light-032), a phone as a mall's roof
  (core-067), a tiny boat by a giant whale (cam-005).

## 8. Camera and motion language

- **Lock while the layer acts; move between beats.** Graphics never draw during a fast move — let the camera land, then
  draw (core-159, 160, 161).
- **One continuous world:** glide across one floor/town from chapter to chapter instead of cutting to new sets
  (motion-001, core-160, motion-015).
- **Follow the line:** the camera follows a route, tape line or track from subject to subject, then pulls up to the whole
  map (motion-015, motion-003).
- **Follow the token:** when the token moves, the camera tracks it (motion-025, light-042).
- **Moves:** slow push-ins, lateral dollies, crane up/down, orbit on a model, pull-back reveal to the next context. Speed
  calm; one move per beat.
- **Timing:** entrances snappy (~0.3–0.4 s, directional motion blur, **no bounce**), then calm holds of 3–8 s. Things roll,
  drop and settle with weight (motion-025, 008, light-016).
- **Projection:** high three-quarter perspective (near-iso) for places; straight-down for pattern/flow beats (cam-001,
  009); low or impossible angles only for hooks (cam-038, 043, 045).
- **Odd moving first frame:** the hook frame is already in motion — the token mid-roll, a building mid-assembly, a
  wall mid-slide (memory `odd-moving-first-frame`).

## 9. Handovers (transition vocabulary, all built from scene assets)

| handover | how | refs |
|---|---|---|
| **Token carry** | the token rolls/drops out of one scene into the next | motion-025, light-042 |
| **Build / unbuild** | parts drop in from above and settle (footprint → walls → roof → sign); exit by flattening back to tiles | motion-008, core-039 |
| **Clay → colour** | locked camera; colour and material wash over the white set on the word | people-014→033, motion-009 |
| **Wall slides away** | a wall/roof lifts to reveal the inside; the camera pushes in | light-032, core-031 |
| **Foreground wipe + rack focus** | an object slides past the lens while focus pulls to the next subject | light-007, motion-010 |
| **Line-led glide** | the camera rides a route/tape line to the next subject | motion-015, motion-003 |
| **Pull-back to context** | the detail shrinks into the wider world, which holds the next beat | core-159, light-020 |
| **Dive into a tile** | from an overview of tiles, dive into one tile until it is the full set | core-054 |
| **Pass through a ring/door** | the token passes through a frame and changes colour/state | motion-025, motion-013 |
| **Match on silhouette** | same framing, x-ray → real (allowed, §12.2) | light-017 |
| **Light wash** | a soft light flare fills the frame and clears to the next shot | light-027 |

Every handover must use an object already in the scene (memory `no-cheap-defaults`). Nothing pops on or off; every
object travels, grows, fades or settles over ~0.4 s (memory `no-harsh-cuts`).

## 10. Never

- Colour everywhere at equal strength (the AI-stock refs: cam-014/015/027, places-007/010/013).
- A white world with flat light and no subject (light-046–050) — whiteness alone is not the look.
- Graphics floating on top of the world as cards or slides over a map (core-140, 156).
- Dark HUD "tech" chrome (glow edges, circuit floors) as the default look (Vorontzov/Bizone genre layer).
- Faces, hand-acted gestures, lip sync, walking figures.
- Bounce or elastic easing; objects popping on/off.
- More than one accent fighting (color-016, core-083, 086).
- Cutting into everything at once (the cutaway is for one object).
- Anti-slop check (memory `anti-slop-design`): if the frame could be a slide, it fails. Every frame needs an angle, a
  light decision and one meaningful accent.
- The retired styles: cut-paper collage, paper diorama, printed 3D (memory `retired-styles`).

## 11. Adopt / Adapt / Drop

| conviction / device | verdict | why and how |
|---|---|---|
| White model world + one real subject (C1, C2) | **Adopt** | Blender clay material + per-object material swap; raea renders it. Library CC0 models painted clay, the subject keeps a textured version. |
| Cutaway of one object (C3) | **Adopt** | hide/slide a wall mesh; for x-ray, a glass shader variant on one object only. |
| Travelling token (C4) | **Adopt** | one animated rigid object (coin/marble/box) with keyed paths; raea follow-camera. |
| Soft translucent domes/volumes | **Adapt** | emissive transparent spheres with falloff instead of real volumetrics (render cost). |
| Lock + change one thing (C5) | **Adopt** | material/visibility keys on a locked camera. |
| Clay → colour wash | **Adapt** | a mask that sweeps across (a driver on material mix by distance from a point), not a per-pixel paint. Needs a test (13). |
| Colour script per episode (C6) | **Adopt** | a 5–7-swatch strip in episode.md before building. |
| Warm-inside / cool-outside light (C7) | **Adopt** | emissive window planes + area lights; world light cool. |
| Brand as roof letters / signs (C8) | **Adopt** | extruded text or the real logo on a sign mesh; logo files per episode. |
| Build/unbuild entrance | **Adopt** | keyed drops with ease-out, staggered; no physics sim needed. |
| Line-led camera, tile dive | **Adopt** | camera on a curve; scene as tiles on one floor. |
| Faceless posed figures with one identity item | **Adopt** | raea bodies in held poses; one prop each. |
| Real-material subject (brick, copper, fabric) | **Adapt** | library PBR textures; MaterialCraft/TextureCraft hero materials are untested — fall back to CC0 textures. |
| Dense true-to-place clutter (aircon, scooters, signs) | **Adapt** | build a reusable Malaysian street kit once (aircon units, Gardenia van, kopitiam signs, five-foot way, poles and wires) and reuse; heavy to model per episode. |
| Tilt-shift / real-lens DOF | **Adopt** | raea/EEVEE depth of field. |
| Particles gathering into a shape | **Adapt** | a few dozen instanced flecks on keyed paths, not a sim. |
| Character acting (Sony, Gary, Oddfellows) | **Drop** | not reliable; faces and acting break the house rules. Keep only their light and staging. |
| Dark HUD tech look, fast title-sequence cutting | **Drop** | genre default / wrong pace for 20–30 s explainers. |
| Hand-drawn 2D, paper textures | **Drop** | retired or not our medium; graphic-match *timing* is kept. |

## 12. Decisions (user, 2026-11-08)

1. **Colour on the shop:** the subject shop in full real colour and detail; everything around it white. (Overrides any
   "white shop + brand colour only" reading of C1.)
2. **Match cut allowed:** a hard cut in identical framing (x-ray → real, clay → real) is allowed for "what's really
   inside" beats. Objects still never pop on/off.
3. **Light:** daylight while explaining; dusk with warm windows for the payoff.
4. **Accent colour:** chosen per episode from the brand and the story's meaning — not a fixed house green.
5. **Cross-style sharing:** A, B and C borrow each other's traits and techniques; the bibles are not walls. Keep quality
   on the same par across styles.

6. **World colour (user, 2026-11-09):** the model world is **dark** (charcoal clay, cool moonlight); the business is the warm light. Replaces the white world of §1–§6 for colour only; everything else stands.

## 13. Test plan (not built yet; not urgent — run after the B and C bibles)

Pick one real Malaysian business from the EX28/EX29 slate.

- **Still 1 — the hook:** a white clay street at a high three-quarter angle; the subject shop is the only real-coloured
  building, its logo on the sign; the token (e.g. a coin) mid-roll on the road; low warm side sun. Proves C1, C2, C4, C8.
- **Still 2 — the inside:** the same shop with its front wall slid away, the token on the counter, warm interior glow,
  cool exterior; one posed faceless figure with an apron. Proves C3, C7, section 7.
- **Still 3 — the payoff:** the same locked framing as still 1 at dusk; the accent area grown (e.g. a stack of coins or a
  glowing dome over the block). Proves C5, C6.
- **10-second test:** build/unbuild entrance of the street (2 s) → token rolls to the shop (2 s, camera follows) → wall
  slides away, push in (3 s) → pull back to dusk with the accent grown (3 s). Proves the handovers and timing.

Rule: stills and a check .blend first (memory `stills-for-look-tests`), user verdict, then the 10-s preview
(memory `blender-preview-before-render`).

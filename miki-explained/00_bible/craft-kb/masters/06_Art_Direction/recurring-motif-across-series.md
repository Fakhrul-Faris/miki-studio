## CREATIVE_INTENT
A one-off clever idea is forgettable; a series or brand builds recall by deliberately reusing the same visual
ingredient across every instalment until the audience associates the idea with the series itself, not just the logo.
- Art Direction Techniques adds: once a campaign/series lands on a working idea, the same formula should be "threaded
  across these channels" (for us, across episodes) regardless of topic.
- 100 Art Direction Ideas adds the trademark framing and the review method: look back at past work, find the idea
  that resonated most or that you naturally repeated without planning to, name it, and deliberately keep doing it
  (varied, not identical) — "whether someone else does it or not, if they think of you then that... built your brand
  standard."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. After a handful of episodes, review which staged idea (not topic) repeated without being planned to, or pick one deliberately for a new series.
2. Name that recurring idea in one phrase (a through-line), separate from any single episode's topic.
3. Reuse it in the same position/role every time (e.g. always the frame-0 object, always the same counter style).
4. Check it survives topic changes — it must work whether the episode is about a warung or a phone bill.
5. Decide whether to lean into it (use it again soon, varied each time so it doesn't repeat identically) or retire it.
6. Track it: when a new episode's frame brief reuses the motif, say so explicitly ("fee-drain motif, as in EX20") rather than leaving it implicit.
7. Don't introduce a second, competing recurring device in the same series.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add the recurring device to the series bible (e.g. `RECIPE-D.md` or the series-portfolio tracker) as a fixed
ingredient, separate from any one episode's own 2–3 ingredients. Keep a short "Recurring motifs" note (in the series
bible or an `ASSET-SOURCES.md`-adjacent log) listing 1–3 active motifs with the episode numbers that used them. In a
frame brief's "Ref" field, cite the motif by name when reusing it.

### Worked example
Series: "Everyday Owners." Recurring device: every episode opens on the same macro object type (a cash drawer, in
some form) before cutting to that owner's specific business — so viewers recognise the series within the first
second even before the logo appears. For a second series of "fee breakdown" how-to episodes (illustrative): the
series commits to one motif — every fee drains visibly out of the real object it comes from (coin slot, receipt
total, phone balance) — and each new episode's frame brief says "fee-drain motif, as in EX20" instead of reinventing it.

### Check
- Is there one ingredient reused across every episode of the series, named once and separate from any single episode's topic?
- Does it still work when the topic changes?
- Does the frame brief cite it explicitly when it's reused, rather than coincidentally repeating a look?
- Is it distinct from, and doesn't compete with, that episode's own 2–3 ingredients, and has it been varied enough not to feel copy-pasted?

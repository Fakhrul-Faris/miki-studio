## CREATIVE_INTENT
A narrator who writes "as a detached observer, an on-high expert" — "for their peers, not their audience" — puts up
a defensive wall; writing instead as a curious friend equalises journalist and viewer, invites them in, and lets
their defences down.
- Johnny Harris Reveals gives the mechanism for reveal lines specifically: "I want to show you what this map
  says…" before a reveal, addressing the viewer directly, removing any name/acronym/term they'd have to already
  know; YouTube is "the original show-and-tell platform."
- Master Storyteller gives the mechanism for the whole script's tone: name one specific listener, write every line
  as a voice note to them, use words you'd actually text them, and ask them at least one direct question — the
  tactic that breaks the viewer's "am I getting sold to?" barrier the way Emma Chamberlain, Casey Neistat and Steve
  Jobs's keynote delivery do.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before drafting, name one specific listener (e.g. "Aina, 24, runs a kuih stall on weekends").
2. Write each line as a voice note to that person; use words you'd text them (korang, je, tau, tak), never presenter phrases ("hari ini kita akan membincangkan").
3. Before any reveal, write a show-line: "Tengok ni / Jom tengok / Let me show you…" (≤ 6 words).
4. Address the viewer as "korang/you" throughout, never "penonton" or "one"; remove every name, acronym or term the viewer must already know, explaining it in ≤ 6 words or cutting it.
5. Remove words that signal expertise rather than meaning; keep the hard content, change only how it's invited in.
6. Ask the listener at least one direct question in the script; read the draft aloud as if on a call and rewrite any line you wouldn't say to a friend.
7. Give the listener's name/photo to the user with the voice text for recording.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add "Listener:" at the top of Script v1 (e.g. "Pak Mat, burger stall, Bangi" — fictional persona, never shown on
screen). Write voice text with a [curious] or [conspiratorial] tag on each show-line; the shot cue on "tengok"
triggers the eye-direction event (see `08_Edit_Pacing/look-at-this-eye-direction--johnny-harris-writes.md`). The end
question (RECIPE-D) is addressed to that listener. Voice text keeps Eleven v3 rules (numbers as words, … and — for pauses, ≤ 2 tags per line).

### Worked example
Topic: reading a supplier invoice for a laundromat in Melaka, and QR payments at a gerai (numbers illustrative).
- BEFORE: "Analisis invois menunjukkan kos tambahan memberi impak signifikan kepada margin." / "Hari ini kita akan membincangkan kepentingan pembayaran tanpa tunai untuk peniaga kecil."
- AFTER: "Jom tengok invois sabun ni sama-sama. Nampak baris kecil ni? Caj hantar RM25, tiap minggu. Setahun,
  RM1,300." / Listener "Pak Mat, burger stall, Bangi": "Pak Mat, berapa ramai budak datang, tengok dompet… terus
  blah? Jom pasang QR. Tiga step je."

### Check
- Is there a named listener, and would you send each line as a voice note to them?
- Is there a show-line before each reveal, and is every unfamiliar term explained in ≤ 6 words or cut?
- Is the viewer/listener addressed directly as korang/you, with presenter phrases gone, and at least one direct question asked?

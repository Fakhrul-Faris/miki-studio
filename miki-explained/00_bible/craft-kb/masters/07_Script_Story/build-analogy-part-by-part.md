## CREATIVE_INTENT
Instead of stating a complex or invisible system directly (fees, interest, pricing rules, code structure), pick a
familiar object or story the audience already knows, and map it to the real system one piece at a time — each
mapped part lands as its own line and its own visual state change, in the same order on both sides.
- Analogies for technical ideas adds the mapping step: list the concept's parts in order (2–4 parts), map each one
  to one part of the analogy object (a house's foundation = basic code structure, its walls = added functionality),
  and show each mapped part as its own line/half-line and its own on-the-word state change.
- Explain complex things without losing people adds the "zipper" framing and the selection step that comes before
  it: first pick a familiar story the audience has actually lived (the article's example: mixing mild and hot salsa
  to get medium, mapped to blending high- and low-grade gasoline to get mid-grade) that shares the same steps as the
  complex system, in the same order; tell that familiar story first (≤ 2 lines) before touching the real system; then
  connect each complex step to its familiar twin "like the teeth in a zipper" — one line per pair — and drop any
  complex detail that has no familiar twin, since pairing also stops the explainer drifting into unneeded detail.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before writing, pick a familiar story or object the audience (Malaysian 18–34) has actually lived (mamak, pasar malam, Grab, kenduri, a kedai's own stock).
2. List the real concept's parts in order (2–4 parts) and check the familiar story shares the same steps, in the same order; drop anything that has no familiar twin.
3. Tell the familiar story first, in ≤ 2 lines, before introducing the real system.
4. Pair each real/complex part with its mapped familiar part — one line or half-line per pair, in the same order on both sides.
5. Show each paired part as its own state change on its word (the hero object changing, not narration alone).
6. Check every real-system part that matters has a familiar twin, and that nothing unpaired survives in the final script.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Script: the familiar story runs first (≤ 2 lines), then zipper-pairs each real step to its familiar twin, one line
per pair. Shot-list: one row per mapped part; the event column names the word and the part that appears; for a
zipper pair, the shot is the familiar object morphing into the real one in the same framing (object morph, Style D
native).

### Worked example
Topic: what a kedai's monthly cost is made of (parts-mapping), chained with why savings left untouched grow faster
each year (zipper pairing; both illustrative numbers, 5% rate illustrative).
- BEFORE (parts): "Kos bulanan kedai ada sewa, gaji, bil dan stok."
- BEFORE (zipper): "Compounding bermaksud faedah dikira atas pokok dan faedah terkumpul."
- AFTER (parts): "Bayangkan satu bekas nasi lemak. Nasi tu sewa, RM1,500. Sambal tu gaji. Telur tu bil. Baru ada
  untung — ikan bilis je." Rows: `| 9 | "Nasi tu sewa, RM1,500" | top-down macro on the packet, opened | rice layer
  lights in accent, RM1,500 counter on "sewa" |` …
- AFTER (zipper): L03 "Bayangkan pokok rambutan. Tahun ni berbuah, korang tanam biji dia." L04 "Tahun depan, ada dua
  pokok berbuah." L05 "Duit sama. RM1,000 dapat 5%, jadi RM1,050." L06 "Tahun depan, 5% dikira atas RM1,050:
  RM1,102.50." Shots: one rambutan tree → two trees; the coin stack in the same framing grows
  1,000 → 1,050 → 1,102.50.

### Check
- Is the familiar story/object one this audience has actually lived, told first (≤ 2 lines) before the real system?
- Does each real-system part map to exactly one familiar part, in the same order on both sides?
- Does each mapped part get its own line/word AND its own on-the-word state change (not narration alone)?
- Is any complex detail with no familiar twin cut from the final script?

## NOTES
- Arithmetic (zipper worked example): 1,000 × 1.05 = 1,050; 1,050 × 1.05 = 1,102.50. The 5% rate is illustrative.
- The two sources describe the same technique at two grain levels — part-to-part mapping (general mechanism) and the
  "zipper" (a familiar story told whole, then paired step by step) — kept together since the zipper is a specific,
  stronger instance of the same mapping rule, not a competing method.

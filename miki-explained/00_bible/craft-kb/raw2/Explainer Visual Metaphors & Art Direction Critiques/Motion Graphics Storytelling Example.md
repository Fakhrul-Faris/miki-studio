Introduction

## A motion graphics story needs something to change

Most motion graphics videos that promise storytelling are moving slides. An icon slides in. A counter ticks up. A logo spins. Then the next icon.

Nothing happens to anyone. So nothing sticks.

A story needs one thing a slide never has: a change you can watch. A parent who goes from worried to sure. A knot of wires that disappears. A pizza ingredient that crosses borders and still arrives fresh.

**The short answer:** the motion graphics stories that work run one of four plots. **Stick Figure** draws one person your buyer can stand in for. **Tidy-Up** draws the mess, then clears it on screen. **Courier Run** follows one thing through what your product does. **Playground** sets an abstract idea in a world your buyer already knows. The 30 examples below are sorted by the plot they run.

## Motion Graphics Storytelling examples

Plot 1 · Stick Figure

### If your buyer can’t picture using it, draw one person who does.

A crash-test dummy says more about a car than a spec sheet. You picture yourself in that seat. Your buyer needs the same seat in your video.

I call it the Stick Figure: one drawn person whose day changes because of your product. Six examples.

![Natera Emotional motion graphics](https://i.ytimg.com/vi_webp/SCJrb9AJD3A/maxresdefault.webp)12:39

### Natera Emotional motion graphics

Natera’s Horizon carrier screening video follows prospective parents from hopeful to informed. The genetics sit inside their story, not beside it.

What works here:

- DNA strands, chromosomes and a family tree are animated, with glowing markers for carriers and affected relatives. Inheritance becomes something parents can trace.
- The test gets a new role. Screening is shown as a planning tool, before pregnancy or early on, not as a source of worry.
- The story doesn’t stop at the result. Board-certified genetic counselors appear as the next step, so the parents aren’t left alone with the data.

**The move:** Put your buyer’s feeling at the start and the end, and your product in the middle. If your last scene shows a feature instead of a person, redraw it.

Plot 2 · Tidy-Up

### If your buyer lives with the mess, draw the mess, then clear it on screen.

A person isn’t the only thing that can change. Sometimes the change is the room they work in.

A before-and-after photo of a garage sells the shelving. Nobody asks for the bracket specs. Your buyer’s tool sprawl is that garage.

I call it the Tidy-Up: the buyer’s mess drawn first, then cleared in one continuous move. Ten examples.

![Wirepas Emotional resonance animation](https://i.ytimg.com/vi_webp/5zpS7bAWYuQ/maxresdefault.webp)71:37

### Wirepas Emotional resonance animation

Wirepas sells a wireless network for asset tracking. The video draws traditional tracking as tangled wires, then takes them away.

What works here:

- The pain spans industries, from warehouse inventory to hospital asset searches. Tangled wires stand for the cost and the infrastructure.
- The fix is one action: “just put it where you need it and go.” The network is battery-operated and self-healing.
- It lands on one word, “FREEDOM,” then names cost savings, better operations and scale without limits.

**The move:** Pick one object that stands for your buyer’s old way, and remove it on screen. If the mess has no single picture, the tidy-up won’t land.

Plot 3 · Courier Run

### If your product works out of sight, follow one thing through it.

A tidy-up shows the result. But some buyers stop at a harder question: what actually happens inside?

A parcel tracker makes a 5-day wait feel short, because you can see where the box is. Your buyer’s data moving through your product is that box.

I call it the Courier Run: one thing, a molecule, a record or an ingredient, followed from start to finish. Eight examples.

![Maersk Pizza supply chain story](https://i.ytimg.com/vi_webp/WaEt00__8X0/maxresdefault.webp)170:46

### Maersk Pizza supply chain story

Maersk explains its supply chain through one pizza. A 2D animated infographic follows the ingredients from farm to fork.

What works here:

- The thing being followed is ordinary. Everyone knows a pizza, so global logistics have something to hang on.
- Each ingredient gets its own card, and each one comes from a different country.
- The promise sits in the title: ingredients kept fresh from farm to fork.

**The move:** Pick one everyday object your product touches, and trace it end to end. If a viewer can’t name the object afterward, it was too abstract.

Plot 4 · Playground

### If the idea is abstract, set the story in a world your buyer already knows.

Some products have no person to follow and no object to track. Just an idea. So where does the story happen?

A board game teaches money faster than a finance class. Your buyer learns your category the same way, through a world they already know the rules of.

I call it the Playground: a familiar world, a stadium, a carousel, a road trip, where your idea gets played out. Six examples.

![Alation Animated corporate storytelling](https://i.ytimg.com/vi_webp/3ZFBe5U5yz8/maxresdefault.webp)251:33

### Alation Animated corporate storytelling

Alation marks 10 years in data catalogs by borrowing from sports. It asks who the “GOAT” is, then builds a Hall of Fame to answer.

What works here:

- Sports figures introduce the Greatest Of All Time idea before Alation appears. The frame is familiar, so the claim lands faster.
- Banners in a stadium carry the proof: analyst recognition from Gartner, Forrester and IDC, Snowflake, and Fortune 100 trust.
- Sports-style commentary and a celebratory soundtrack keep the whole video in one world.

**The move:** If you’re selling a track record, pick a world where track records already matter. If the metaphor drops away halfway, the proof feels pasted in.

Pick your plot

## Which plot does your motion graphics story need?

**The short answer:** ask one question. What does your viewer need to see change: a person, a mess, a hidden process or an abstract idea? The answer picks the plot. One plot per video. If two feel right, make two short videos instead of one long one.

| If the change is in | Use | Do this | Example to copy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A person’s day | [Stick Figure](https://advids.co/blog/30-motion-graphics-storytelling-video-examples-for-effective-promotion#plot-figure) | Draw one person your buyer can stand in for | [Natera](https://advids.co/blog/30-motion-graphics-storytelling-video-examples-for-effective-promotion#natera-emotional-motion-graphics) |
| A messy workflow | [Tidy-Up](https://advids.co/blog/30-motion-graphics-storytelling-video-examples-for-effective-promotion#plot-tidy) | Draw the mess, then clear it in one move | [Wirepas](https://advids.co/blog/30-motion-graphics-storytelling-video-examples-for-effective-promotion#wirepas-emotional-resonance-animation) |
| A process nobody sees | [Courier Run](https://advids.co/blog/30-motion-graphics-storytelling-video-examples-for-effective-promotion#plot-courier) | Follow one thing from start to finish | [Maersk](https://advids.co/blog/30-motion-graphics-storytelling-video-examples-for-effective-promotion#maersk-pizza-supply-chain-story) |
| An abstract idea | [Playground](https://advids.co/blog/30-motion-graphics-storytelling-video-examples-for-effective-promotion#plot-playground) | Set it in a world your buyer knows | [EU tax and customs](https://advids.co/blog/30-motion-graphics-storytelling-video-examples-for-effective-promotion#eu-tax-and-customs-informational-animated-story) |

Now go back to the moving slide: an icon, a counter, a spinning logo. Nothing changed for anyone. Each plot above fixes that by giving the viewer one thing to watch change.

**The move:** list the scenes in your current script. Next to each, write what changes in it. Any scene where the answer is “nothing” gets a plot, or gets cut.

Moving icons show what you built. One thing changing shows why it matters.

FAQ

## Motion Graphics Storytelling Examples FAQ

What is motion graphics storytelling?

Motion graphics storytelling uses animated shapes, type, icons and drawn characters to show something change over time: a person, a workflow, a process or an idea. The change is the story. Wirepas draws tangled wires, then removes them. That’s a story. An icon list is not.

How is a motion graphics story different from a motion graphics ad?

Many [motion graphics ads](https://advids.co/blog/motion-graphics-advertisement) make a claim and show the product. A motion graphics story shows a before and an after, so the claim gets proven by the change. For the live-action side of the same idea, see these [storytelling ads](https://advids.co/blog/storytelling-advertising-video).

Does a motion graphics story need a character?

No. Only the Stick Figure plot needs one. Wirepas tells its story with wires, Moderna with a molecule, Alation with a stadium. A character helps most when your buyer can’t picture using the product. For drawn customers, see these [animated SaaS customer story videos](https://advids.co/blog/30-animated-saas-customer-story-video-examples-for-showcasing-success).

How long should a motion graphics story be?

Most run 1 to 2 minutes: 17 of the 30 on this page do. The shortest are Ownr at 0:15 and Lendi at 0:16, each with one change. The longest is Labuan IBFC at 2:52. Fit the length to the change, not the feature list.

Should a motion graphics story use a voiceover?

Usually, yes. [A voiceover](https://artlist.io/blog/voiceover-for-videos-and-audio-branding/) carries the meaning so the drawings can stay simple, the way AgilLink’s narration does. Add captions either way. Plenty of people watch with the sound off.

Should a motion graphics story use 2D or 3D animation?

Use 2D when the story is about people, workflows or ideas, like Natera, Esper and airfocus. Use 3D when the story happens inside something physical, like Nalco Water’s heat exchangers or ARNITEL’s membrane. Moderna mixes both.

Where should the product appear in a motion graphics story?

After the problem and before the result. Esper opens on a frozen update and an offline kiosk, then brings in the product. If your product appears in the first frame, the viewer has no reason yet to care about it.

How should a motion graphics story end?

On the changed state, not the logo. EU tax and customs ends with the carousel stopping. Lendi ends on a “SOLD” home. Natera ends with genetic counselors ready to help. Show the after, then the call to action.

What makes a visual metaphor work in a motion graphics story?

Three things. Your buyer already knows its rules. It lasts the whole video. And it can change when your product arrives. Alation keeps its stadium to the end. EU tax and customs stops its carousel. A metaphor that can’t change can’t show your fix.

Can a B2B software product tell a story without showing its interface?

Yes, for the first half. Google Cloud and airfocus draw the mess as shapes before any screen appears. But show the interface before the end. Buyers want to see what they’d actually use.

How many ideas should one motion graphics story carry?

One change, one plot. Lendi carries one in 16 seconds: confusion to a sold home. When a video stacks several changes, none of them sticks. Split extra ideas into separate short videos.

How do you measure whether a motion graphics story works?

Pick the goal first: demo requests, trial sign-ups or a page visit. Track clicks on the call to action against it. Then check where viewers drop off. A drop before the change lands means the setup is too long.

How to plan

## Where to use your motion graphics story

Match the plot to the place. A story that needs 2 minutes won’t survive a feed. A 16-second story can’t carry a sales call.

- Landing pages. A Tidy-Up or Stick Figure story above the fold, so a new buyer sees the change before reading a word.
- Paid social and feeds. A cut under a minute, built on one picture, like Lendi or Esper.
- Sales follow-ups. A Courier Run that shows what happens inside, for the buyer who asked “how does it work?”
- Category and brand pages. A Playground story, when the idea is newer than the buyer’s vocabulary for it.

## How do you write a motion graphics story?

Start with the change, not the script. Write the last frame first: what does the world look like once your product is in it? Then write the first frame: the same world without it.

The middle is the [story arc](https://bang2write.com/2023/03/story-arc-and-conflict-examples.html) between them. Keep it to the moments that move the viewer from one frame to the other. The [elements of a complete narrative](https://blog.pond5.com/6477-storytelling-101-the-6-elements-of-every-complete-narrative/) still apply: someone or something, a setting, a conflict and a resolution.

- Last frame. The after-state, drawn, with your product in it.
- First frame. The before-state your buyer recognizes.
- Plot. Stick Figure, Tidy-Up, Courier Run or Playground.
- Middle. Only the beats that connect the two frames.
- Words. Narration says what the drawings can’t. Cut every line that describes what’s already on screen.

For flat, character-led work, these [2D animated storytelling examples](https://advids.co/blog/animated-storytelling-video) show the same structure in other categories.

## How do you animate the change in a motion graphics story?

The change is the one shot the whole story leads to. Pick how it moves before you design anything else. Here are 5 ways the examples above animate it.

- Transform it. One object turns into its better version. PharMerica’s loose pills become organized blister packs in one drawn transition.
- Remove it. The mess leaves the frame. Wirepas takes the tangled wires away, and a shattering effect sweeps Esper’s device headaches off screen.
- Merge it. Separate pieces join into one. BioStar 2 pulls visitor management, parking access and video surveillance into a single system.
- Run it side by side. The old way and the new way share the frame. Domino runs each ribbon saving mode next to a “Default Mode.”
- Stop it. A moving machine halts. EU tax and customs ends its fraud story with the carousel stopping.

The test: pause your video on the change shot. If a viewer can’t tell what got better from that one frame, redraw the shot.

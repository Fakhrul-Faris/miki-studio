I’ve been passionate about writing since a very young age. But it was only about six years ago that I finally found my niche in writing. It all began when I started my career as a scriptwriter for animation videos at [WowMakers](https://www.youtube.com/@Wowmakers) with no prior experience in the area. Being a newbie in the animation industry, I wasn’t very familiar with writing concepts and scripts for animation videos. But soon enough, I realized that this was it! This is what I’ve always wanted to do!

My area of expertise lies in crafting concepts and writing scripts (both visual and voiceover scripts) for animation videos. There was a lot of learning to be done, and today, when I look back, I can only be grateful for the learning experience I have gained over the years from working on 120+ video concepts and scripts. If you would like to get a glimpse of what I have picked up over the years, read on!

**The process**
---------------

If you are not familiar with the production part of motion graphics videos, let me take you through the process. First comes the phase where the project brief is gathered, followed by the conceptualization stage where a storyline is woven, which is then developed into a detailed script with visuals and voiceover. These stages are followed by storyboarding, illustration, animatics, and finally, animation.

**Getting into the client’s mind**
----------------------------------

The very crux of making a video is understanding the assignment and having a clear idea of what the client needs. Sometimes customers need help figuring out what exactly they want, and that’s where we come in. We go above and beyond the brief they share with us and do some extensive research before we begin creating something. We gather whatever details we can and make a brief out of them to lead the client into communicating what they really want. This could be tricky, as it requires reading between the lines and perceiving the unsaid. There are times when the brief exceeds the desired duration of the planned video. It is here that we take out our superpowers to convey bigger things in a short time, which is quite a complicated craft to master.

**Looking for inspiration in the mundane**
------------------------------------------

Once we understand the brief, we embark on a creative journey in search of the most effective way to communicate the message to the viewers. Most of the time, what we get to work on are videos for tech companies that entail briefs with jargon and processes that are hard to explain in a few words. It is challenging to find storylines in the mundane. This also requires me to have a thorough technical understanding of the brief. I love this part of my job where I get to learn about different industries, as it widens my understanding of how the world works.

I kickstart my projects by collecting as many details as possible about the industry, company, product, or service that the client is trying to sell, picking keywords to randomly search for inspiration, looking for reference videos, and going wild with my imagination to create that perfect storyline.

**Weaving stories that stick**
------------------------------

My favourite part of the job also happens to be the most challenging part: weaving catchy and engaging storylines that align with what the client needs and are sure to make an impact on the viewers. Our job is to make the message stay with the clients for ages.

At [WowMakers](https://www.youtube.com/@Wowmakers), We recently did a [video for Jixo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JIQJzqGlr_E), an innovative smart home company. The challenge was to convey the benefits of smart homes to people of all ages. How do you make the product stand out in a market teeming with smart home products? What we needed was a simple story. Our eureka moment happened when the idea of personifying the brand occurred to us. We developed the idea of how Jixo, like an invisible friend, transforms homes into living homes — homes that are so alive. The client found this concept so appealing that they changed their tagline from ‘Smart Homes’ to ‘Living Homes’.

We worked on another project called [Tralexho](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HM5-UzVwd24), a fish delivery app that allows customers to trace the journey of their orders right from sourcing to delivery. We felt the best way to convey this was to tell the story of a customer who is taken on a journey once she places an order. The idea behind this was to convey how traceable it is for consumers and that it is as good as seeing the whole journey in person.

**Determining the style of animation**
--------------------------------------

2D animation is an umbrella term, and under it comes a variety of styles: character animation, object animation, whiteboard animation, watercolour animation, isometric animation, and a lot more. The budget varies for each style based on the desired video duration and the effort required to complete the project. Most of the time, the clients know what style would work for them. But there are times when they trust us to make a decision for them in terms of style, and it is up to us to choose a style that would work well according to their timeline, duration, and budget while making sure it aligns with their brand identity and the message they aim to convey.

**Writing for Visuals**
-----------------------

I realized there was a fine line between writing for reading and writing for visuals. When you read a book, you get to have your own visualizations of the written content. It differs from person to person. But while writing for visuals, it has to be done in such a way that what the client and the team visualize as they read the script aligns with what we intend to create on screen. It is like painting a picture with words.

Writing for visual scripts is more than just writing. It involves making the right creative decisions, as the end result is not a script but a video. Everything starts with the script, and there’s more responsibility on the scriptwriter to get it right at the very first stage.

**Writing emotive voiceover scripts**
-------------------------------------

While a frame description for a visual script should sound descriptive, the voiceover script should be emotive. It should talk to the viewers and urge them to take action. It should convey a sense of assurance to the viewers. It should hit the right spots, evoke a sense of curiosity, and make them stay hooked on the video.

**Handling feedback diplomatically**
------------------------------------

Feedback cycles are yet another challenge. Some feedback may do more harm to the video than good, and it’s up to us to use our creative intuitions and suggest alternate ways to accommodate such feedback while abiding by the budget, planned duration and timeline of the project.

**Communicating, not flaunting!**
---------------------------------

While I had to learn a lot in my journey as a scriptwriter, I also had to unlearn a few things. For instance, I realised that writing to impress and writing to communicate were two different things. I figured out how important it is to use simple language that is comprehensible to anyone using social media. Today, I’m fully aware that my job is to communicate a message or idea to the prospective audience in a way that they understand and not to show off my skills with words that are heavy and pretentious.

**I’m just a channel.**
-----------------------

Scriptwriting is all about breaking down complex ideas into captivating storylines and developing them into engaging videos. It is all about creativity. There is yet more to be learned and unlearned, but I can’t help confessing that scriptwriting has been a humbling experience, where I just wait for the mercy of creative powers to inspire me, enlighten me, and work through me to create something magical out of thin air!

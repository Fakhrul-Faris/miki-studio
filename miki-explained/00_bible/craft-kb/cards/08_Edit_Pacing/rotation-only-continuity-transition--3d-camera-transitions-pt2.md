## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Here you saw the transition between the two scenes even without camera movement. Just the rotation movement creates continuity. This is motion graphics: continuous motion without stopping." Instead of moving the camera to sell a join, the entering object is given its own entrance rotation (keyed from +25° decelerating to 0°, eased) that continues "until the very last second" — the object's own spin is the thing that makes the cut feel like one unbroken event.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before adding a camera move to a join, check whether the hero object itself can carry an entrance/exit rotation instead.
2. Key the object's rotation from an offset value (e.g. +20–25°) down to its settled 0°, eased (fast-on-entry, decelerating to the end).
3. Let the rotation continue decelerating all the way to the last frame of the setup — it should still be visibly settling at the cut, not already stopped.
4. Leave the camera static or on a minimal move for this join; the rotation alone should read as the continuity.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
raea: key the entering hero object's rotation on an eased curve that is still decelerating at the cut frame; camera stays on a simple hold or a very small move for this join.

### Worked example
Topic: a kedai gunting rambut's clippers switching on (illustrative).
BEFORE: camera flies in to sell the clippers' entrance.
AFTER: camera holds static; the clippers themselves rotate in from +20° settling to 0°, still decelerating on the cut to the next line → rotation-only-continuity-transition.

### Check
- Is the camera static or near-static for this join (not doing the work)?
- Does the object's rotation start offset and ease down to settled, still visibly moving at the cut?
- Does the rotation stay within the ≤ ~130°/s cap (it should, since it's a settle, not a whip)?

## CREATIVE_INTENT
"You're trying to get as much mileage as possible from the same take … cram as much information as you can and add
elements and block them so that you don't need to cut to reveal new elements." The camera "was motivated by following
the main character and we stopped to reveal the second character." The camera moves with the runner and "stops together
as he stops". "The more elements you stack into a single take, the more epic and cinematic … it shows intention, it shows
planning."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before cutting between two related lines, ask whether one camera path can reach both subjects.
2. Give the move a motive: follow a moving object (a rolling coin, a sliding receipt).
3. Stop the camera exactly when the moving object stops (same frame, both eased out).
4. Compose the stop frame so it reveals a second element that was out of frame.
5. Add a passing element through the frame to "get more mileage" from it (`~` one per take).
6. Write each stop as its own row (≤ ~4 s), joined by the motivated move.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list: consecutive rows share `same take` in the camera cell; each row = one composed stop.
raea: the object's progress `pObj` and the camera's progress share the same `[start, end]` frames and the same
`Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)`, so they stop together; the camera's `lookingAt` leads the object slightly toward the
element to be revealed.

### Worked example
Topic: "Satu resit kedai runcit — ke mana duit RM50 korang pergi" (illustrative).
- BEFORE: `| 5 | "Duit masuk laci" | macro cash drawer |` cut `| 6 | "terus ke pembekal" | macro supplier invoice |`
- AFTER: `| 5 | "RM50 korang masuk laci…" | follow the folded RM50 note sliding across the counter | — | stack-beats-in-one-take |`
  `| 6 | "…tapi RM41 terus ke pembekal" | same take: note stops against the invoice clip, camera stops with it; the invoice is revealed | "RM41" fades in on the invoice on "RM41" | stack-beats-in-one-take |`

### Check
- Is the camera move motivated by something moving in frame?
- Do the camera and the object stop on the same frame?
- Does the stop reveal a new element that a cut would otherwise have shown?
- Is each stop ≤ ~4 s and a composed still?

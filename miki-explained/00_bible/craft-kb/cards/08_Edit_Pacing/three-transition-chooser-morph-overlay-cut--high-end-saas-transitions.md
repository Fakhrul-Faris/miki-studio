## CREATIVE_INTENT
"How many transitions does a high-end SaaS video actually need? 10? 20? A whole plugin pack? No. You just need
three": **morph**, **overlay**, **cut**. The era of motion packs "just to glue two scenes together" is over. Stop
asking "how do I get from scene A to scene B" and ask "which of these three fits best here".

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. For each join, list what A and B share: a shape? an element that could cover/uncover? a motion or layout?
2. If they share a shape (or a shape chain), choose **morph**.
3. Else if one element can rise to cover the frame, or move away to reveal B, choose **overlay**.
4. Else if A and B share a motion direction or a design layout, choose **cut** (same motion / same design).
5. If none apply, redesign A's end or B's start so one applies.
6. Before locking, check no family is used more than ~3 times in a row; vary execution within a family.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list: add `join: morph | overlay | cut-motion | cut-design` to the Setup cell of every row; name the shared
element ("join: morph — RM coin → dial").

### Worked example
Topic: "Terima bayaran online untuk kedai kek rumah — 3 step" (generic steps).
- BEFORE: every join "cut".
- AFTER: L03→L04 `join: overlay — the phone rises from the bottom to fill the frame` ; L04→L05 `join: cut-design — the order card layout repeats in the payment screen` ; L05→L06 `join: morph — the round "Paid" badge becomes the round cake top` ; L06→L07 `join: cut-motion — text and cake both slide in left→right`.

### Check
- Does every join name one of the three families and its shared element?
- Is no family repeated more than ~3 times in a row?
- Is there no generic dissolve/wipe/flash?

## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Cross-cutting juxtaposes two or more different scenes, encouraging the viewer to compare and contrast them." *Rocky IV*
repeatedly cuts Rocky's training against Drago's "to highlight Rocky's underdog status". The suspense article adds:
"cutting between two parallel threads converging on a single point multiplies the tension."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before the shot list, write the two threads as two columns of matched steps.
2. Give each thread a fixed look (side of frame, surface, light temperature) kept through every setup.
3. Alternate A, B, A, B on matched steps; each setup ≤ ~3 s.
4. Shorten the setups as the threads approach the comparison (~3 s → ~2 s, `~`).
5. Converge: end on one setup that holds both threads (side by side or one object).

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list: label rows `A3`, `B3`, `A4`, `B4`…; matched steps share framing so the pair reads as one comparison.

### Worked example
Topic: "Dua gerai nasi lemak, satu untung, satu rugi" (illustrative).
- BEFORE: L03–L05 all about gerai A, then L06–L08 about gerai B.
- AFTER: `| A3 | "Gerai A beli telur pukal" | egg tray, left-lit, warm |` → `| B3 | "Gerai B beli ikut papan" | single egg cartons, right-lit, cool |` → `| A4 | "Kos sebungkus RM2.10" |` → `| B4 | "Kos sebungkus RM2.80" |` → `| 7 | "Harga jual sama: RM3" | both packets side by side, margins RM0.90 vs RM0.20 grow as bars | converge |`

### Check
- Does each thread keep one fixed look?
- Are alternating setups matched step for step?
- Do setups shorten toward the end?
- Do the threads converge in one frame?

## CREATIVE_INTENT
"I like to see a storyboard as the visual foundation of any video." For a ~50 s launch film the director designed a total
of **15 slides** before animating, with a thought process for each slide, and called the first scene (the hook) "the
toughest". Hook, transitions and product demo are solved on the board, not in the animation tool.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before you open raea, split the locked script into beats (one line or half-line each).
2. Design one board frame per setup: ~15 frames for ~50 s (~1 per 3 s).
3. Under each frame write one sentence: why this image shows this line.
4. Name, per frame, which object leads into the next frame.
5. Solve the hardest frame first; usually the hook.
6. Do not build a frame whose reason sentence is empty.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
For a 55–90 s episode that is ~20–30 setups (2–3 s each, RECIPE-D). Put the frame in the shot-list row's "Setup and
camera" cell and add a `why:` clause to "State change and events". The RECIPE-D §7 board check runs on these frames.
Board and approve row 1 (the hook) before the rest.

### Worked example
Row BEFORE: `| 3 | "Tiga step je" | phone on table | number 3 appears | |`
Row AFTER: `| 3 | "Tiga step je" | macro, low grazing on a receipt roll | on "tiga" three tear-lines print across the
roll, each lighting in the accent red; why: the three steps are three cuts of the one receipt we follow | storyboard-one-frame-per-beat |`

### Check
- Does every board frame have a one-sentence reason tied to its line?
- Is the setup count ≈ runtime ÷ 2.5 s?
- Was the hook frame designed and approved first?
- Does each frame name what leads into the next?

## METADATA
08_Edit_Pacing | Matt Stone & Trey Parker (South Park), NYU talk | Shot-list rows / board

## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Each individual scene has to work as a kind of funny sketch. You don't want one scene that's just like, well, what was
the point of that scene?" Every unit must pay off on its own, not only as connective tissue.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. For each shot-list row, write in ≤ 10 words what the viewer gets from it alone.
2. Mark a row with no answer, or with "transition only", as filler.
3. Give a filler row a state change on a named word, or merge it into its neighbour.
4. Before the board check, re-read rows in order and ask "what was the point?" of each.
5. Cut any row that still has no point.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add the ≤ 10-word payoff to the "State change and events" cell of each row. A setup's payoff in Style D is a state
change (x-ray, open, slice, multiply with counter, become a chart) or a number landing on the word. Camera flights
between setups are joins, not rows — they do not need their own payoff, but they must be built from the scene's objects.

### Worked example
Topic: QR at a gerai (illustrative).
BEFORE: `| 7 | L04 "Step satu…" | slow orbit around the QR stand | — |` (point: none, just keeps the stand on screen)
AFTER: `| 7 | L04 "Step satu, daftar akaun merchant" | low grazing angle on the QR stand, rack focus to the code | the code
assembles square by square, last square lands on "merchant"; payoff: the stand becomes yours |`

### Check
- Does every row have a one-line payoff that works without its neighbours?
- Is no row only a "hold" or "drift"?
- Does each payoff land on a named word?

## NOTES
- Source context is a comedy writers' room; "funny sketch" is translated as "pays off on its own".

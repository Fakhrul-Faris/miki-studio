## CREATIVE_INTENT
"I have yet to find a more immediate instinctive process to throwing down ideas than thumbnailing." Lay the foundation in
2D first (script breakdown → thumbnails → beatboards), then find the 3D approximation. Make "a very broad staging plan"
from above — where everything is at any point — before ground-level detail, because a 2D overhead view "restricts your
point of view such that you think in the clearest and simplest terms". In the 3D pass keep two views: a local view and a
bird's-eye view of the whole arena showing how much things move.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before building any 3D, draw one rough thumbnail per line (box + hero silhouette + camera arrow).
2. Draw one top-down map of the set: hero, props, and a numbered camera position per setup.
3. Mark camera travel between numbered positions as arrows on the map.
4. Only then block the scene in 3D, matching the map.
5. Keep a bird's-eye view open beside the camera view while blocking.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add a `staging.svg` (top-down, numbered camera dots `C1…Cn` keyed to shot-list rows) to the board stage. Each row's
"Setup and camera" cell cites its dot (`C4 → C5`). The RECIPE-D §7 check "no two stills from the same angle" is run on
the map first: two dots at the same spot and direction = fail.

### Worked example
BEFORE: shot list goes straight to 3D; rows 3 and 6 both end up front-on to the QR stand.
AFTER (map, gerai nasi lemak QR how-to): `C1` low front of QR stand · `C2` top-down over phone · `C3` side grazing along
counter · `C4` behind the stand looking at the customer's phone screen; rows cite `C1→C2` etc.; row 6 moved from `C1`
to `C3` because the map showed a repeat.

### Check
- Is there a top-down map with one numbered camera per setup before any 3D?
- Do any two setups share position and direction on the map?
- Does every row cite its camera dot?

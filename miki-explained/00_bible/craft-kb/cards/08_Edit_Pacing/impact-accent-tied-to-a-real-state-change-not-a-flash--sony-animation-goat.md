## CREATIVE_INTENT
Sony's "impact frame" (a holographic trading-card foil look on key dunks) works because it's tied thematically to
the story and the object (the character's claws leaving iridescent trails, gold foil revealed by ripping through
"digital paint" of the scene) — not a stock flash dropped on top. The payoff is themed and object-driven.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before adding any punchy visual on a payoff beat, check it is built from the scene's own object changing state
   (revealing, multiplying, counting, splitting) rather than an overlay graphic.
2. If a brief moment of extra shine/accent is wanted, drive it from the object's own material/colour on the word,
   not a generic flash/wipe/glitch.
3. Keep the moment tied to the specific payoff it marks — it should not be reusable as a stock effect elsewhere.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list "State change and events": the payoff beat's event must name the object's own transformation (e.g. the
counter's final digit locks and the whole stack briefly takes the brand accent colour) — never "flash" or "glow
overlay" as the event itself. This directly extends the existing no-flashy-transitions house rule to payoff beats
specifically.

### Worked example
Topic: a receipt total completing a calculation (illustrative).
BEFORE: the final total appears with a generic white flash across the frame.
AFTER: shot-list event: "on the final digit, the receipt's printed total briefly takes the brand accent colour and
the paper texture catches a harder highlight — no overlay, the object itself changes."

### Check
- Does the payoff moment come from the object's own state/material changing, not an overlay?
- Would the effect still make sense if reused on a different object, or is it specific to this one state change?

## NOTES
- ⚠ Heavily adapted: the source's literal foil-flash effect is exactly what "no flashy transitions (no generic
  flashes)" forbids. Only the underlying principle — a themed, object-driven payoff beat — is kept.

## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Sound in film lends itself to juxtaposition since it is almost always placed in combination with images." Music changes
meaning (*The Shining*'s opening score turns a landscape into dread; *Reservoir Dogs* puts an upbeat song over violence).
Sound design adds meaning: *Sound of Metal*'s cacophony against peaceful silence; *Blade Runner 2049*'s interrogation
"underlined with the sound of boiling water, creating the feeling that Sapper is about to explode."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before adding any SFX, pick the one line whose subtext the picture doesn't state.
2. Choose a sound whose everyday meaning matches that subtext (pressure → kettle hiss; money draining → slow drip).
3. Keep it low under the voice (−18 to −24 dB under dialogue, `~`), on that line only.
4. Or use contrast instead of a new sound: drop the bed to silence for ~0.5–1 s before the line.
5. Check it doesn't compete with the outro signature sound.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list: add a `sound` note to one row only: `kettle hiss rising under L07 (pressure)`. Sound pass: log it in
`episode.md`; nothing else gets SFX except the outro.

### Worked example
Topic: "Hutang BNPL — kenapa cepat sangat bertambah" (illustrative numbers).
- BEFORE: whoosh on every setup change; cash-register ding on every number.
- AFTER: no SFX except one: `| 7 | "Lewat sebulan, caj tambah RM15…" | macro of the phone's due-date screen | faint kettle hiss rises under the line and cuts at "RM15" | sound-subtext-juxtaposition |` + the outro signature.

### Check
- Is there at most one subtext sound in the episode?
- Does the sound's everyday meaning match the line's subtext?
- Is it quiet, under the voice, on one line only?

## NOTES
- Late-fee mechanics vary by provider; the RM15 is illustrative, not a claim about any provider.
- Music juxtaposition (upbeat over dark) is not used: no music choices are made in these cards.

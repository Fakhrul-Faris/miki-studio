## METADATA
08_Edit_Pacing | manga/graphic-design analysis (general) | shot-list row (Setup and camera, State change)

## CREATIVE_INTENT
A near-empty panel — mostly solid black or white, with the subject small or absent — can be used deliberately as a
beat of dramatic pause, shock, or loneliness rather than filler: "sometimes you can let space do the talking." Land
of the Lustrous reserves this for its most emotionally loaded moments, not as a general layout habit.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before scripting a loss/shock/closure line, decide if it deserves a held near-empty frame instead of the usual
   new-setup-every-2–3s cut.
2. If yes, shrink the subject to a small fraction of the frame (or remove it) against a large flat dark or light
   area.
3. Hold that frame for one beat (~2–3 s, the same ceiling as any Style D setup) rather than cutting faster.
4. Use this at most once per episode — overuse flattens its weight.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
In the shot-list, mark that row's "Setup and camera" as a static hold (no camera move) with the object shrunk or
off-frame, and note in "State change and events" that nothing else happens on this beat — it is the pause.

### Worked example
Topic: a small kedai forced to close after rent doubles (illustrative numbers).
BEFORE: the shutter-closing line gets the same busy macro object and camera move as every other beat.
AFTER: shot-list row: `| 9 | "Kedai tutup." | static hold, the shutter tiny at the bottom third of a mostly empty
dark frame | nothing else moves for this ~2 s beat | pause-beat |`

### Check
- Is the held frame reserved for a genuine loss/shock/closure line, not used more than once?
- Does the hold still fit inside the ~2–3 s per-beat ceiling?
- Is the subject shrunk/placed deliberately, not just a blank frame with nothing to read?

## NOTES
- ⚠ Adapted against the "new setup every 2–3 s" rule as above — pacing ceiling kept, content made sparse instead.

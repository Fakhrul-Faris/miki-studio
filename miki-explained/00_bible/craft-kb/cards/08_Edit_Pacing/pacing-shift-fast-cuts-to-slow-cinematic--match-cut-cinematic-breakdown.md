## CREATIVE_INTENT
"We go from quick quick quick quick quick to slow, and [the held shot] can almost feel slow motion" by contrast. The source uses two rising "whoosh" sound hits exactly at the handoff to pull the viewer out of the fast run and into the next, more deliberate shot — the slower section then needs shots "chosen intentionally" (story, composition, lighting all doing work), unlike the fast run where a shot can be nearly anything because it reads for a fraction of a second.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Identify where the Mechanism beat's fast match-cut run ends and the Proof/Payoff beat begins.
2. Place one sound cue (a rising whoosh or similar) exactly at that handoff frame.
3. For every setup after the handoff, choose the hero object/framing more deliberately — it will be seen longer, so sloppy composition shows.
4. Keep each post-handoff setup within Style D's ≤ ~4 s and still landing a state-change on the word; do not stretch any single setup into a long static hold.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list: mark the row where the run ends and the Payoff begins with a "pacing shift" note in the cards column; frame-brief for every row after it gets extra care on Decision/Eye (composition, light) since it reads longer.

### Worked example
Topic: a kedai runcit's daily plastic-bag cost add-up (illustrative).
BEFORE: 6 quick bag-count cuts, then the RM total appears in the same rhythm, no shift.
AFTER: 6 quick bag-count cuts (Mechanism) → one whoosh cue → RM450/month total setup held slightly longer, framed with more care (counter catching the light) → pacing-shift-fast-cuts-to-slow-cinematic.

### Check
- Is there one clear sound cue marking the handoff from the fast run to the payoff?
- Does every setup after the handoff still land a state-change on a word, within ≤ ~4 s?
- Is the payoff setup's composition/light more deliberate than the fast-run shots?

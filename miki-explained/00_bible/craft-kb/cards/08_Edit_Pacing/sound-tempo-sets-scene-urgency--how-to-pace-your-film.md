## CREATIVE_INTENT
Music plays a large role in pacing. Put the Jaws scene to slow music and there is far less suspense; John Williams's
accelerating score braces us for the scare. Hans Zimmer's Dunkirk score uses what sounds like a ticking clock, adding
urgency to every scene, even ones that wouldn't normally feel rushed.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before choosing the bed, name the episode's tempo (calm / steady / urgent).
2. For urgent topics, choose a bed with a clear pulse at ~110–130 BPM (inferred).
3. If a time object is on screen, let one quiet tick follow its motion for that setup only.
4. Do not stack extra SFX on top; remove the tick at the payoff.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
episode.md § Sound: bed tempo + the one tick motif + where it stops. raea: audio via the composition's audio track;
the tick timed to the clock-hand frames.
### Worked example
Topic: "Sewa kedai lambat bayar" (illustrative: a tenancy agreement with a RM50 late charge written into it; RM1,500 rent).
- BEFORE: generic upbeat bed, a whoosh on every cut.
- AFTER: steady pulse bed; on L05–L07 a macro on a wall clock over the rent envelope, one soft tick per second following the second hand; tick stops dead on L09 "RM1,550" (accent).
### Check
- Is there at most one tick/pulse motif tied to an on-screen object?
- Does it stop at the payoff?
- Are there no whooshes on cuts?

## NOTES
- BPM is my inference; the source gives no numbers.
- The late charge is a term of that (illustrative) agreement, not a law; an episode must quote the real contract or rule it relies on.

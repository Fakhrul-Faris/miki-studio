## CREATIVE_INTENT
Without a big budget, crew, or elaborate location, a tighter, longer lens is an "easy win": it hides things that
would otherwise show (a prop that could fall off the wall, an empty background) and makes the frame feel denser and
more layered than it actually is, because compression stacks what little exists into a tighter, richer-looking frame.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before adding more set dressing to fill a sparse frame, try a longer (virtual) focal length and tighter framing
   first.
2. Check whether the tighter frame already hides the sparse/unbuilt areas without needing more geometry.
3. Confirm the compressed framing reads as "dense" rather than simply "zoomed in" — if it reads empty either way, add
   the minimum dressing needed, not more.
4. Only widen the lens again if the shot specifically needs to show scale/space.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list "Setup and camera" field: default to a longer virtual focal length for any setup where the build is
sparse, rather than requesting more geometry/props to fill the frame.

### Worked example
Topic: a small bakery's single display shelf (minimal build).
BEFORE: wide lens on the shelf reveals the empty unbuilt sides of the stall, reading as a bare set.
AFTER: longer lens, tighter framing on just the shelf and the hero pastry — the compression makes the shelf's few
items feel stacked and dense, and the unbuilt sides never enter frame.

### Check
- Was a longer lens/tighter frame tried before adding more set dressing?
- Does the tighter frame hide what wasn't built, rather than just magnifying emptiness?
- Is a wider lens used only when the shot specifically needs to show scale?

## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Your motion graphics should lead the audience's eye through the journey of the content... you want basically a continuous path through your motion unless you're specifically trying to break that journey. If you suddenly jump from the top left to the bottom right corner, it's going to make your audience stutter... even if it's just subconscious." Each match cut in the source example places the next shape very close to where the previous one ended, and adds extra "supporting movements" (e.g. an eye drifting up, its pupil moving up then down) purely to keep the eye's path smooth across the cut.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before writing a shot-list row's "Setup and camera" cell, locate where the eye lands at the end of the previous row (screen position of the hero/state-change).
2. Place the new row's opening element at or very near that same screen position.
3. If the natural cut would relocate attention (e.g. new element enters top screen while the eye was bottom), add one supporting drift (a part of the object, a highlight, a small secondary motion) that leads the eye there first.
4. Only break the path deliberately (a hard jump) when the beat itself is a surprise/contradiction moment — never by accident.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list: add a "continues from" note citing the previous row's end screen-position; frame-brief "Eye" field states where the gaze enters and exits this setup, confirming it matches the neighbours.

### Worked example
Topic: a kedai basikal's tyre-pressure check (illustrative).
BEFORE: `| 5 | "Tayar kempis buang minyak" | tyre gauge appears top-right | reading drops | — |` then `| 6 | "...sampai RM2 sebulan" | RM counter appears bottom-left | — |` (eye must jump corner to corner).
AFTER: row 6's counter enters where row 5's needle ended (top-right), then a small coin-icon leads the eye down-left as the counter animates in → eye-tracing-continuous-path.

### Check
- Does each new setup's opening position match (or lead smoothly from) the previous setup's ending position?
- Is any screen-position jump in the list a deliberate surprise beat, not an accident?
- Is there a supporting motion (not just a hard cut) carrying the eye across every join that isn't a deliberate break?

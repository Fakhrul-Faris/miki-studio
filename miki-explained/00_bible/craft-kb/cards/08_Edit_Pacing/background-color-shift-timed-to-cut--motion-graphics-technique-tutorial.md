## CREATIVE_INTENT
"If we were thinking about the momentum of movement here, if it were to go straight up there'd still be some of that energy pushing it sideways" — the match cut is otherwise slightly imperfect, so a background hue shift (yellow background → blue) timed exactly to the cut frame absorbs attention and reinforces "we're moving to something slightly different here," helping sell a cut that isn't physically perfect.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Identify a match cut where the matched shapes' momentum/position isn't a perfect overlap.
2. Key the background/room base tone to change exactly on the cut frame (not before or after).
3. Keep the change to the environment base tone only — never the one brand accent.
4. Judge by eye: does the tone shift read as "new scene" rather than drawing attention to the seam itself.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
In raea, key the dark room's base/floor colour (not the accent prop) to step at the exact cut frame alongside the state-change event.

### Worked example
Topic: a kedai runcit's till drawer open→close state change (illustrative).
BEFORE: dark room stays one flat near-black tone across the whole run; the drawer's open→closed match cut reads slightly off.
AFTER: room's base tone steps from near-black to a faint warm charcoal exactly on the cut frame, cutting away attention from the imperfect match → background-color-shift-timed-to-cut.

### Check
- Does the background/room tone change land on the exact cut frame, not drifting before/after?
- Is the one brand accent colour untouched by this shift?
- Does the shift read as "new moment", not as a flashy effect calling attention to itself?

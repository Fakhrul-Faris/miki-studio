## CREATIVE_INTENT
Juxtaposition "can be as simple as emphasizing a difference in size." In *Jojo Rabbit* an exaggerated height difference
"creates an immediately evident power dynamic". In *Up*, Carl's quaint house "abutted by massive construction projects"
says the world is moving on without him. In *Edward Scissorhands*, a black-clad figure against bright, colourful suburbia
marks the outsider.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before framing, name which of the two is "small" and which "big" in the line's meaning.
2. Place them touching or nearly touching (abutted), not far apart.
3. Exaggerate the scale gap (≥ ~3× height, `~`).
4. Shoot low beside the small one so the big one towers.
5. Keep the small one lit and in accent; leave the big one neutral or darker.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: Idea = "<small> against <big>"; Feeling = pressure / outsider / left behind; Decision = abutted, low angle, ≥ 3×.
raea: scale the big model (`withScale`), camera low (`y` near the surface) at the small object's side.

### Worked example
Topic: "Kedai runcit vs hypermarket sebelah" (illustrative).
- BEFORE: `| 5 | "Kedai kecil ni untung RM40 sehari" | macro of the small shop model alone |`
- AFTER: `| 5 | "Kedai kecil ni untung RM40 sehari…" | low macro beside a small lit shop model abutting a wall-sized dark box (the hypermarket), 5× taller | "RM40" fades in over the small shop | size-contrast-juxtaposition |`

### Check
- Are the two touching/abutted?
- Is the scale gap obvious (≥ ~3×)?
- Is the "big" thing literally what the line names?

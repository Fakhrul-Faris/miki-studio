## CREATIVE_INTENT
Headline sizing across magazines, posters and websites follows a three-level system: a large main heading/hook
(the attract phase, often just a couple of words), a secondary heading causing intrigue, and smaller body/finisher
text carrying the message or CTA. The largest element doesn't have to be the most important fact — it's the hook
that earns the read; size, weight, and position all reinforce the same three levels together.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Assign exactly three size/weight tiers to any on-screen text in a frame: hook (largest), secondary detail (middle), finisher (smallest).
2. Make the hook the first thing noticed, even if it isn't the single most important fact in the frame.
3. Give the secondary tier a visibly smaller size/lighter weight than the hook.
4. Reserve the finisher tier for body detail or the call-to-action/closing line — it should be read last.
5. Never give two text elements in the same frame equal size and weight; that collapses the hierarchy.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Apply the ladder to the script's on-screen text layer: frame-0 hook text = tier 1 (largest), any mid-episode
supering number/label = tier 2, the end-text question = tier 3 (smallest, but never illegible on a phone).
### Worked example
Topic: a how-to episode's closing frame (illustrative, S16 how-to format).
BEFORE: the end question and a supporting RM number are set at the same size — the eye doesn't know which to read first.
AFTER: the RM number (already paid off) renders at tier 2 size as it fades, and the closing question renders at
tier 1 size as the last thing on screen, matching its role as the final hook-weight line.
### Check
- Are there exactly three distinct size/weight tiers across the frame's text, never two elements tied?
- Is the tier-1 (hook) element the first thing the eye would land on?
- Is the finisher tier reserved for the detail or closing line, read last?

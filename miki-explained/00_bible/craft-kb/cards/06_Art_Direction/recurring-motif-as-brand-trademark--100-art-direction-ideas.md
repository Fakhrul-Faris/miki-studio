## CREATIVE_INTENT
A one-off clever idea is forgettable; a brand builds a trademark by repeating a visual idea across a series until
viewers associate the idea with the brand itself — "whether someone else does it or not, if they think of you then
that begins to have... built your brand standard". The method: look back at past work, find the idea that resonated
most or that you naturally repeat, and deliberately keep doing it (retiring or varying it over time), rather than
treating every episode's art direction as a blank page.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. After a handful of episodes, review which staged idea (not topic) repeated without being planned to.
2. Name that recurring idea in one phrase (a "through-line"), separate from the topic of any one episode.
3. Decide whether to lean into it (use it again, deliberately, soon) or retire it.
4. If leaning in: note it as a candidate motif for the next 2–3 episodes' frame briefs, varied each time so it doesn't repeat identically.
5. Track it: when a new episode's frame brief reuses the motif, say so explicitly rather than leaving it implicit.
6. Check: does the motif read as "the brand does this" rather than "this episode happened to look like that"?

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add a short "Recurring motifs" note in the series bible (or `ASSET-SOURCES.md`-adjacent log) listing 1–3 active
motifs with the episode numbers that used them. In a frame brief's "Ref" field, cite the motif by name when reusing it.

### Worked example
Topic series: several "fee breakdown" how-to episodes (illustrative).
BEFORE: each episode invents its own way to show a fee being deducted — one draws it as a bar chart, another as falling coins, with no connection between them.
AFTER: the series commits to one motif — every fee always drains visibly out of the real object it comes from (coin
slot, receipt total, phone balance) — and each new episode's frame brief says "fee-drain motif, as in EX20" instead of
reinventing it.

### Check
- Is the motif named once, separately from any single episode's topic?
- Does the frame brief cite it explicitly when it's reused, rather than coincidentally repeating a look?
- Has it been deliberately varied enough across uses that it doesn't feel copy-pasted?

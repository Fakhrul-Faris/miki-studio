## METADATA
06_Art_Direction | general (live-action film) | shot-list setup, frame brief "Eye"/"Feeling"

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Use a doorway or window to surround the subject. The extra boundary directs our eye and can suggest confinement.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Use it when the line needs focus on one item, or means confinement or limitation.
2. Pick an inner frame from the scene's own objects (doorway, window, shutter, bezel, box opening).
3. Place the subject fully inside the inner frame, in the 25–60 % band.
4. Keep the inner frame darker or softer than the subject.
5. For confinement, make the opening tight (subject fills ~60–70 % of it); for focus, leave breathing room.
6. Optionally push the camera through the opening (eased) to carry into the next setup.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list cell: `frame-in-frame: through the half-open roller shutter`. Frame brief "Feeling": focus or "stuck".
raea: build the frame as real geometry between camera and subject (a Model or Cuboids), out of the key light; animate
the camera's Z through it for a transition.
### Worked example
Topic: "Sewa kedai 3 tahun: berapa sebenarnya korang komit" (rent illustrative; the logic is plain arithmetic).
- BEFORE: a tenancy agreement on a desk, medium shot.
- AFTER row: `| 7 | "Sewa RM 1,500 sebulan, tiga tahun, jadi RM 54,000" | frame-in-frame: through the gap of a half-closed
  roller shutter, the agreement sits small inside the dark opening | on "tiga tahun" the shutter lowers 10 cm (eased
  0.6 s), the gap tightens; on "54,000" the total (36 × 1,500) counts up in accent on the agreement | frame-within-a-frame |`
### Check
- Is the inner frame a real object from the scene?
- Is the subject fully inside it and brighter than it?
- Does the tightness match the line (tight = trapped, loose = focus)?

| parameter | look-like-movies | recommended for Style D |

| frame objects | doors, windows, arches, mirrors | shutter gap, phone bezel, display case, mirror at a salon station |
| effects | narrows the field ("the eye knows at once where to look"); voyeur view (*Rear Window*, *In the Mood for Love*); a wall between two sides (*Paris, Texas* one-way mirror); exclusion (*The Searchers*: door closes, hero left outside) | focus / "looking in" / two sides separated / shut out |
| bonus | "a frame adds layers, and layers mean depth" | inner frame = foreground layer |

Extra variants:
- Looking in: view the subject through a gap/doorway as if spying (e.g. a shop's back-room stock seen through the door gap).
- Separation: put a glass or frame edge between two objects to show they are on different sides (seller vs platform fee).
- Shut out: a closing door/shutter leaves one object outside the frame — eased close ≥ 0.4 s on the word.

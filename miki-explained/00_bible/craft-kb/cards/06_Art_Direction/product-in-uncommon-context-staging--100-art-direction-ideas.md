## METADATA
06_Art_Direction | brand/photo art-direction talk (skincare cream applied to a vintage letterman jacket) | frame-brief device

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Attach the product to something that has no logical reason to be near it but is visually compelling on its own — the
example applies a skincare cream to a vintage letterman jacket: "it doesn't make sense, it doesn't have reason, but
it's gorgeous and it's impactful" — the device works especially for categories with few inherent variations (skincare,
simple packaged goods) where endless straight product shots become repetitive.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Identify a hero object/category with few visually distinct variations (skincare jar, packet, plain tool).
2. List 3–5 visually strong objects/materials that have zero logical connection to the hero object (a jacket, a sculpture, a tool, a textile).
3. Pick the one with the strongest colour/texture contrast against the hero object.
4. Stage the hero object interacting with it in a way that still reads as "the hero object is the point" — not buried.
5. Don't explain the pairing in dialogue/caption; let the visual stand alone.
6. Check: is the pairing illogical but the shot still gorgeous, not just random?

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame-brief "Idea"/"Not generic because": name the unrelated object and why its texture/colour contrast makes the hero pop.

### Worked example
Topic: a nail salon's cuticle oil (illustrative).
BEFORE: the oil bottle sits on a plain marble counter, lit softly.
AFTER: the same bottle sits balanced on a cracked terracotta roof tile — no logical connection, but the tile's rough
orange-red texture against the bottle's clean glass makes the product read as premium and tactile.

### Check
- Is the paired object genuinely unconnected to the product's normal use, not a "makes sense if you squint" prop?
- Does the hero object remain the clear visual point, not overwhelmed by the strange pairing?
- Would this shot still look good as a still, without the voice-over explaining it?

## METADATA

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Take an action everyone recognises instantly (watering a plant, wiping a counter, pouring a drink) and make exactly
one element of it wrong or impossible (watering a swimming pool instead of a plant) — the recognisability of the base
action does the work of making the image legible in under a second, while the single "off" detail makes it strange
enough to stop a scroll, for little to no extra cost.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Identify the mundane action the current line implies (pouring, pumping, stacking, wiping, counting).
2. Keep the action and its tools completely normal and recognisable.
3. Change exactly one element to something that doesn't belong at that scale or context (the target, the quantity, the container).
4. Don't change more than one element — two "off" details reads as a gag, not a clean concept.
5. Check: does the shot still read as "a person [or object] doing [ordinary action]" at a glance, with the twist landing a half-second later?

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame-brief "Idea": name the ordinary action and the single swapped element. This pairs naturally with the odd-moving-
first-frame rule — frame 0 can literally be this device (a familiar action, one element wrong).

### Worked example
Topic: a small business owner topping up change for the till each morning (illustrative).
BEFORE: a generic shot of coins being poured into a till drawer.
AFTER (one element off): the same pouring motion, same coin sound, but the coins are being poured not into the till —
into a glass jar already overflowing onto the counter, visualising "lebih duit masuk dari laci boleh tampung".

### Check
- Is exactly one element changed from the ordinary version of the action, not several?
- Does the base action stay instantly recognisable without the twist needing explanation?
- Does the twist visualise something the line actually says, rather than being a random gag?

## NOTES
- Visual gaps: the pool-watering reference image itself is visual only; the transferable part is the one-element-swap method.
- Rule conflicts: none, provided no people/hands are shown performing the action in Style D — stage it as an object doing/receiving the action instead (the hose pours itself, the till drawer fills).

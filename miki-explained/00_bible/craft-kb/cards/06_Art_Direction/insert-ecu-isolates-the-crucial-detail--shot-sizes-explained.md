## METADATA

## CREATIVE_INTENT
An ECU isolates a specific area (eyes, lips) — in Kill Bill Vol. 1 it cuts back and forth between the Crazy 88's entry
points and the Bride's frantic eyes. "When the function of a specific prop or an intimate detail is necessary,
filmmakers will often rely on the insert shot" to "highlight and isolate something crucial to the narrative". It is
"the most intimate, dramatic and potentially startling of all shot sizes" — one of the greatest tools for emphasis.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Name the single detail the line depends on.
2. Frame only that detail; nothing else readable.
3. Keep it short (~1–2 s) and land it on the word that names it.
4. Use at most 2 inserts per episode so they stay startling.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: Eye = the detail; Decision = "ECU, detail 70 % of frame (insert exception)". raea: macro lens, very
shallow DoF, top light raking across the detail.

### Worked example
Topic: "Tarikh luput vs 'best before'" (generic labels; no regulation claims).
- BEFORE: `| 5 | "Tengok tarikh kat tin" | MS of a tin on a shelf | — |`
- AFTER: `| 5 | "Tengok **tarikh** kat bawah tin" | ECU · embossed date on the tin's base, raking light | date edge lit accent on "tarikh", 1.5 s | insert-ecu |`

### Check
- Is only one detail readable?
- Does it land on the word that names it, ≤ 2 s?
- Are there ≤ 2 inserts in the episode?

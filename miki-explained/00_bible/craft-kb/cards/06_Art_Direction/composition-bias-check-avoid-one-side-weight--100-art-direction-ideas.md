## CREATIVE_INTENT
A frame can satisfy "subject in the band, nothing clipped" and still fail if everything interesting (the hero, the
accent, the busiest texture) sits on one side while the other half is dead space with nothing to balance it — the
source's example keeps the subject centred and classic precisely because the flanking elements (the two vehicles) are
kept visually quiet enough not to tip the balance, while still framing interest on both sides.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before approving a board still, split the frame down the centre (or along the hero's position) and look at each half alone.
2. Check whether one half carries all the brightness, detail, or colour and the other is empty.
3. If so, either move the hero/accent, or add a small (quiet, out-of-focus) counter-weight on the empty side.
4. Never add a counter-weight that competes with the hero for attention — it should read as background mass (ties to Dark-edge-framing-blockers).
5. Re-check after any camera move: a setup that opens balanced can drift unbalanced by its last frame.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add to the Board-check pass: "no single half of the frame carries all the visual weight" as an explicit line item,
checked alongside the existing five. In the frame brief's "Eye" field, name where the counter-weight sits if one was needed.

### Worked example
Topic: a motorbike repair shop's spare-parts wall (illustrative).
BEFORE: the hero wrench fills the right two-thirds of frame in bright light; the left third is flat dark wall, empty.
AFTER: a single blurred spare tyre (dark, out of focus, in shadow) sits low-left — enough mass to balance the frame
without pulling attention from the wrench.

### Check
- Does neither half of the frame read as empty while the other is full?
- Is any added counter-weight out of focus/in shadow, never competing with the hero?
- Was this re-checked at the setup's last frame, not just its first?

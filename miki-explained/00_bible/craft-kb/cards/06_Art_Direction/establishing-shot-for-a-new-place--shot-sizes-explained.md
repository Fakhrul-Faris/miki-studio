## CREATIVE_INTENT
The most common way to open a scene or film: "typically wide enough to establish the geography, time of day, show the
scale of subjects in relation to their environment", and often used to transition between scenes. Crucial when a new
world must be introduced (Blade Runner 2049 opens on a series of them). It marks a move to a new location or
introduces crucial details about it.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Use it only when the line moves to a new place or the place/time is the point.
2. Show three facts in one frame: where (geography), when (light/time), how big (scale).
3. Keep it short (≤ 2.5 s) and put one detail that the next setup will close in on.
4. Move the camera into that detail to start the next setup (flight, not a cut, where possible).

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: Idea = "this is a pasar malam at 8 pm"; Eye = the detail the camera will fly to. raea: wide lens, dark
room preserved by a night setting, practical lights for time of day; then an eased flight into the detail.

### Worked example
Topic: "Sewa tapak pasar malam — RM15 semalam, berbaloi?" (illustrative).
- BEFORE: `| 4 | "Tapak pasar malam" | macro on a canopy pole | — |` (no sense of place).
- AFTER: `| 4 | "Pukul lapan malam, tapak RM15" | EST · high wide over an empty row of canopies under warm bulbs, one tapak marked with chalk "RM15" | camera flies down eased 2 s into the chalk mark; "RM15" in accent | establishing-shot |`

### Check
- Does it show where, when and how big in one frame?
- Is it ≤ 2.5 s and does it lead into a detail?
- Is it not frame 0 (unless it holds the contradiction)?

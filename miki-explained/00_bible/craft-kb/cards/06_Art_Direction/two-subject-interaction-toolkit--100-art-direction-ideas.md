## CREATIVE_INTENT
Two subjects merely standing near each other, both facing the camera, is the default and the least interesting option;
any form of contact between them — "there is any amount of contact [that] makes it interesting" — reads as a frame
with implied story. The source's checklist (contact, headbutt, handshake, feeding) is a prompt list for "what could
these two things be doing to or with each other" rather than a fixed set of poses.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. When a setup has two named elements, check whether they currently just sit side by side with no relationship.
2. If so, choose one contact-type relationship between them from a short list (touching, pushing against, feeding into/out of one another, one emerging from the other) adapted to the objects at hand — never a human gesture.
3. Stage the contact so it reads in a single still, not only across motion.
4. Confirm the contact itself carries the line's meaning (e.g. one object feeding value into another = a cost flowing from one thing to the next).
5. Check: with the contact removed, would the two objects read as unrelated?

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame-brief "Idea"/"Decision": name the chosen contact type and what it represents. In Style D (no people), the
"two subjects" are objects, a UI element and an object, or two states of the same object — e.g. a phone screen
docking into a card reader, a coin feeding into a slot, a receipt pushing out of a printer into a waiting hand-shaped
cutout (never an actual hand/figure).

### Worked example
Topic: a car-wash's add-on wax service upselling the base wash (illustrative).
BEFORE: the wash bucket and the wax bottle sit side by side on the counter, both lit, both in focus.
AFTER: the wax bottle tips and feeds a thin accent-coloured stream directly into the bucket's water — a contact beat
between the two objects that visualises "the wax gets added to the wash", not two props posed next to each other.

### Check
- Is the contact between two OBJECTS (or an object and a UI element), never a posed person-to-person gesture?
- Does the contact itself carry the line's meaning, rather than being decorative motion?
- Does a single still of the contact moment read clearly, even before any camera move plays?

## NOTES
- Visual gaps: the source's specific interaction examples (headbutt, feeding) are between posed human models; none can be used literally.
- Rule conflicts: directly conflicts with "no people in the street" / "figures pose only" / "no hand-coded organic art" — fully adapted away from any human figure or gesture to object-object or object-UI contact.

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Art direction's "tool" isn't a camera or a brush — it's a repeatable thinking process. Before any visual decision, name
an insight (an unspoken human truth), then an idea (the creative formula / "verb" — what you do with that truth for
the audience to experience it), and only then move to execution (the ingredients that bring it to life). Skipping the
insight/idea step means the visuals have nothing to be in service of.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before any frame brief, write the insight in one sentence: the unspoken truth the topic reveals.
2. Write the idea as a verb phrase: what the video *does* with that truth (not what it shows).
3. Only after both are written, move to execution — the visual ingredients (06_Art_Direction ingredients-framework card).
4. If a frame brief doesn't trace back to the idea sentence, cut or rewrite it.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add two lines above "Beats v0" in `episode.md`: `Insight:` and `Idea:`. Every later beat and frame brief must be
checkable against the Idea line (mirrors the example student brief, "reframe weather as a playdate," that every ad in
the campaign traced back to).

### Worked example
Topic: a warung's RM1 delivery "service fee" that quietly repeats on every order (numbers illustrative).
BEFORE: start drafting Script v1 lines about delivery fees with no guiding sentence.
AFTER: Insight — "a fee too small to argue over is exactly the one nobody tracks." Idea — "make the viewer watch their
own RM1 fees stack into a real number before they can look away." Every later beat (the counter ticking, the receipt
pile, the end question) traces back to that idea.

### Check
- Is there one Insight sentence and one Idea sentence, written before any frame brief?
- Does the Idea sentence start with a verb (what the video does), not a noun (what it shows)?
- Can every setup in the shot list be traced back to the Idea sentence?

## CREATIVE_INTENT
To light historical sets and costumes in a way that felt contemporary, the art directors looked at "high fashion and
runway shows" — a reference from entirely outside the film's own genre — rather than other animated films or the
obvious period references.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Name the obvious reference category for this setup (the genre default).
2. Deliberately look one step outside it (a different industry's photography: fashion, sport, product, architecture).
3. Pull the specific lighting quality (not the subject matter) from that outside reference.
4. Apply it to the actual subject; check it still reads as the real object, not a costume of the reference.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
`ref_<shot>.md` "subject" field: note the obvious-category reference considered and rejected, and the actual
non-obvious reference used, with the specific lighting quality borrowed (contrast, beam shape, background treatment).

### Worked example
Topic: a 24-hour mamak stall counter at night.
BEFORE: lit like every other "night market" render (warm bulbs, busy background).
AFTER ref: borrowed the lighting quality of a jewellery product shot (single hard beam, near-black background,
everything else starved of light) applied to the stall counter — the RM coins on the counter read like product
photography, not like a generic night scene.

### Check
- Is the obvious-category reference named and explicitly set aside?
- Does the actual reference come from outside the subject's own genre?
- Does the borrowed quality read as lighting (not as a literal copy of the reference's subject)?

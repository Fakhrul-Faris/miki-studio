## CREATIVE_INTENT
Even within a single scene, letting every setup sit at the same brightness/ratio makes the room "feel like one level,"
reading as "a light inside" rather than a believable, varied space: "we don't want it all to feel like one level then
it feels like a light inside... let's try and get some variance."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. After drafting the shot list, scan the lighting level/ratio across all setups in sequence.
2. Flag any run of adjacent setups sitting at the same brightness/contrast ratio.
3. Deliberately shift level or ratio on at least one of them (dimmer key, harder falloff, different fill amount).
4. Keep the shift motivated (a different distance to the same light, a different surface) — not an arbitrary jump.
5. Re-check the sequence as thumbnails: no two adjacent setups should read as identically lit.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list: add a lighting-ratio note per row (e.g. "high ratio, hard," "low ratio, soft fill") distinct from the
colour-script's brightness/temperature tag — this is about contrast ratio specifically, checked setup-to-setup.

### Worked example
Topic: a 4-step how-to on checking a shop's SST registration (illustrative).
BEFORE: all four step setups sit at the same soft, even top-light ratio — the sequence feels like one flat slide deck.
AFTER: step 1 soft even ratio (intro); step 2 harder ratio, single hard-edged shadow (the check itself); step 3 back
to soft but dimmer (the wait/uncertainty beat); step 4 bright, low ratio again (the resolved answer) — no two
adjacent steps share the same ratio.

### Check
- Does the lighting ratio change at least once across any run of 3+ adjacent setups?
- Is each ratio shift motivated by something in the scene, not arbitrary?
- Does the sequence avoid reading as "one level" when viewed as a strip of thumbnails?

## NOTES
- Visual gaps: the specific before/after ratio values aren't given as numbers, only "half a stop too much," "this
  probably too much" — qualitative judgement calls, not fixed values.

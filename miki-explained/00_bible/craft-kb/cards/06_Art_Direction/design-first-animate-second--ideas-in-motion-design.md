## METADATA

## CREATIVE_INTENT
His "big fail": he cobbled the look together while animating, "ploughing in head first without a plan". The result's
weakest link is design: compositions are weak, some parts lack contrast, "it just needs a bit more design love". He tells
students on day one: "never animate a bad design"; his stepdad's drawing advice was don't start the final drawing until
you've sketched a rough outline. "Finalise your design before you animate and you'll save yourself a bunch of time, and
your design will be stronger for it."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before animating a setup, render its key still (start or peak state).
2. Run the board check on it: subject 25–60 % of frame, nothing clipped, no text over hero, one accent, readable at phone size.
3. Check contrast: the hero separates from the background at thumbnail size.
4. Fix composition and contrast on the still.
5. Animate only setups whose still passed.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Board (one still per setup) → RECIPE-D §7 pass → animatic/preview. A setup that fails gets a new still, not motion
"to fix it".

### Worked example
Topic: nasi lemak cost split (illustrative).
BEFORE: L06 animated straight away; the sambal spoon sits at frame edge, dark on dark, and the price tag overlaps it.
AFTER: still first: spoon moved to ~40 % of frame on the lower third, rim light from the top light lifts it off the
banana leaf, tag placed beside, not over; still passes, then the tag's 0.4 s slide is animated.

### Check
- Does every animated setup have a still that passed the board check?
- Is the hero readable and separated at phone size?
- Was no composition problem left to be "fixed by motion"?

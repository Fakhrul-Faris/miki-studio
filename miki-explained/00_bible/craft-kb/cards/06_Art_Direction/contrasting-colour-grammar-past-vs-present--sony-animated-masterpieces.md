## CREATIVE_INTENT
Flashback sequences were deliberately "dark and monochromatic," grounded colours standing for the past, cut against
"the bright neon seen later in the movie" for the present — a whole-palette contrast, not just a desaturation filter,
so the two timeframes are instantly legible even muted.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Identify the episode's two timeframes or states (before/after, mistake/fix, old way/new way).
2. Assign the earlier/worse state a grounded, low-saturation neutral world.
3. Assign the later/better state a brighter, higher-contrast neutral world (within the house's dark-room + one-accent
   limits — raise the key light's level/warmth, not new hues).
4. Cut between them directly; don't gradually cross-fade the grade.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: two palette states, `before: dark/grounded` and `after: bright/lifted`, both still obeying "one soft top
light, one accent." raea: vary `AmbientLight`/key `intensity` and world darkness per setup (not `<Grade>`, which holds
for the whole piece).

### Worked example
Topic: a stall owner who used to lose cash to miscounted change, now uses a QR till (illustrative numbers).
BEFORE grade: "before" setups (counting loose coins, shadowed till) are lit low, cool, grounded — matching the
money-loss mood. AFTER grade: "after" setups (the QR scan, the running total) are lit brighter, key light fuller — the
accent colour on the RM number is the only constant between both states.

### Check
- Are the two states visually distinct even with the sound off?
- Does only the light level/warmth change, not the hue count (still one accent)?
- Is the cut between states a hard cut, not a slow cross-fade?

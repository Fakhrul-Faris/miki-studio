## CREATIVE_INTENT
*Finding Nemo* built near-photoreal water but "defied those same rules to make a scene more emotional": colour had the
most leeway, so although Sydney Harbour's water is turquoise, the lighting artists made it "mucky and green" for one of
the film's emotional low points (Danielle Feinberg, Pixar DP). In *Over the Moon*'s family dinner there is moon and
kitchen light, but the artists "chose to amplify the light from the lanterns" for a warm, festive mood.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before you set a palette, write the beat's feeling in one word.
2. Pick the world's tint from the feeling, not from reality (low = murky green/grey; warm = amber).
3. Apply the tint to light colour and surfaces only.
4. Keep the brand logo and accent at their true colours.
5. When several sources exist, brighten the one that carries the feeling; dim the others.
6. Check the frame still reads as the real object.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: Feeling "<word>"; palette field lists the tint hex and "logo/accent untouched". raea: tint via key
`color` and `AmbientLight` colour per setup (not `<Grade>`, which holds for the whole piece). For the amplified source,
raise its `intensity` and lower the others.

### Worked example
Topic: "Kenapa stok sayur kedai runcit rosak setiap minggu" (illustrative: 20 kg × RM4/kg = RM80 a week thrown away).
- BEFORE frame brief: "fresh green vegetables under a white light" — the loss beat looks appetising.
- AFTER: Feeling "loss"; Light "dim murky green-grey key #8a9a7a, haze low"; Eye "the wilted bayam"; accent on "RM80 seminggu" in brand colour; logo untouched.

### Check
- Does the tint come from the beat's feeling?
- Are logo and accent at true colour?
- Is the emotionally right source the brightest?

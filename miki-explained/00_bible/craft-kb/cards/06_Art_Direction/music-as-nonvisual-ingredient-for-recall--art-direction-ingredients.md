## METADATA
06_Art_Direction | Advertising/art-direction coaching (general) | sound/ingredient decision

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Music is treated as a creative ingredient on par with photography or type — chosen for an emotional register
("nostalgic," "urgent," "driving curiosity") and reused across a body of work so it builds memory, "because emotion
sticks more than information." The point is: don't pick music or sound as an afterthought vibe-check; pick it as
deliberately as any visual ingredient, tied to the insight.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before adding any sound, name the emotional register the beat needs (calm / urgent / triumphant / tense).
2. Choose sound deliberately for that register, not as generic filler.
3. Where a recurring sound exists (the series' signature sound), reserve it for one specific recall moment — don't dilute it elsewhere.
4. Treat silence as a valid choice when no register is named.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief / shot-list: note the sound decision per beat (silence, one real SFX, or the signature outro sound) next
to its emotional register, so sound stays a deliberate ingredient rather than a default layer.

### Worked example
Topic: a laundromat's hidden per-wash fee.
BEFORE: generic light "mood" music bed under the whole video.
AFTER: silence through the build (so the coin-drop SFX reads clearly), then the one signature Meikigo sound only on
the outro logo beat — the recall moment, not spent elsewhere.

### Check
- Does every sound choice trace to a named emotional register, not "just vibe"?
- Is the series' one signature sound reserved for its single recall moment?
- Is silence used anywhere filler music would otherwise sit?

## NOTES
- ⚠ Rule conflict: the source assumes a licensed music bed running throughout; our house rule (sparse SFX, one signature
  outro sound, no filler music bed) overrides this — adapted above to apply the "deliberate ingredient" principle to
  SFX choices instead of a music bed.
- Visual gaps: none (sound-only technique).

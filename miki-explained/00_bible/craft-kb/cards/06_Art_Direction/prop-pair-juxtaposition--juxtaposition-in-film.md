## CREATIVE_INTENT
Juxtaposition = "placing two or more elements side by side to create meaning out of their similarities or differences"
(Latin *iuxta* "very near" + "position": "place together"). Props characterise efficiently: Riggs' cigarette next to a
no-smoking sign (Lethal Weapon 2) says "maverick"; near-identical business cards (American Psycho) make the competition
ridiculous. Wardrobe does the same for class (Snowpiercer: tattered workwear vs a luxurious robe). Duchamp's *Fountain*
put an everyday object inside the art world to provoke a thought.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before framing a comparison line, name the two things the line sets against each other.
2. Choose a pair type: object vs rule (item beside the sign/price/limit), near-twins (look identical, differ in one way), or condition contrast (worn vs new — the object stand-in for wardrobe).
3. Place both in one frame, same surface, same light.
4. Make the difference the only thing that differs (size, label, wear, price).
5. Put the accent on the item the line is about; keep the other neutral.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: Idea = "<A> beside <B>"; Decision = pair type + what differs; Eye = the differing detail. Shot-list event:
the differing label fades in on the word.

### Worked example
Topic: "Barang sama, harga beza — kenapa?" (illustrative).
- BEFORE: `| 2 | "Sabun ni RM4.90 kat sini, RM6.50 kat sana" | macro of one soap bar | price tag counts |`
- AFTER: `| 2 | "Sabun ni RM4.90 kat sini, RM6.50 kat sana" | two identical soap bars side by side on a counter, top-down 45° | tags fade in one after the other on "RM4.90" and "RM6.50"; accent only on RM6.50 | prop-pair-juxtaposition |`

### Check
- Are both items in one frame, same surface and light?
- Is exactly one difference shown?
- Is the accent on the item the line is about?
- Does the pair show what the line literally says?

## NOTES
- Wardrobe/costume contrast → translated to object condition (no people in Style D).
- Visual gaps: film frames visual only.

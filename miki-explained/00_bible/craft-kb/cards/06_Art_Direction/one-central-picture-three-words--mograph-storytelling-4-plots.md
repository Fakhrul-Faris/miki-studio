## CREATIVE_INTENT
Of the 7 stories under a minute, "6 of 7 built the story on one central picture. 1 of 7 built it on one spoken claim":
Lendi rests on spinning paperwork, Esper on a shattering effect, Maersk on a pizza, airfocus on scattered pieces becoming
one roadmap, AgilLink on people working on the move, eSentire on a watched map; Ownr on a spoken claim. "Pick one picture
for your short cut and let everything else serve it. If you can't name the picture in 3 words, you have more than one."
Lendi: "The mess is one image… Viewers know the feeling before any word lands." Source caveat: "Seven is a small sample…
read each count as what these seven did, not as market data."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before the shot list, name the central picture in ≤ 3 words.
2. If it takes more than 3 words, choose one and cut the rest.
3. Make every setup serve that picture: it is in frame, or the frame is its part, or its result.
4. Check the opening picture lets viewers feel the problem before any word lands.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
`episode.md` hero line: `Central picture: "<≤3 words>"`. Board check adds: does each still contain or derive from the
central picture? A setup that shows something unrelated is cut or reframed onto the hero.

### Worked example
Topic: why a small bakery's RM3 croissant barely profits (illustrative).
- BEFORE: setups jump between an oven, a rent invoice, a delivery rider, a butter block, a phone app.
- AFTER: central picture "one croissant, sliced". Butter cost = the croissant x-rayed; rent = croissant on a tiny shop
  floor plan; delivery cut = a slice pulled away; end question back on the whole croissant.

### Check
- Is the central picture named in ≤ 3 words?
- Does every board still contain or derive from it?
- Does frame 0 make the problem felt before the voice explains it?

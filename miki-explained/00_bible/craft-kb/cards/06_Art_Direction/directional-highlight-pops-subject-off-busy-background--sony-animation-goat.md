## CREATIVE_INTENT
To prevent the screen from feeling too chaotic against a busy or graphic background, use a strong directional
highlight on the character/subject to make it pop, rather than relying on blurring the background to separate it.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before defaulting to defocus for subject/background separation, check if the background is meant to stay
   graphic/legible (a UI screen, a labelled shelf).
2. If so, add a stronger directional rim/key highlight on the hero's edge facing the light, rather than blurring
   the background.
3. Keep the background's own detail sharp; separation comes from the highlight's contrast, not from softness.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief Decision: name the highlight's direction and which edge of the hero it should catch; in raea, bias the
key/rim light's angle to graze that edge harder than usual, independent of the DoF setting used for the rest of the
shot.

### Worked example
Topic: a phone screen showing a QR scan beside a cluttered counter background (illustrative).
BEFORE: relying only on shallow DoF to separate the phone from the busy counter behind it.
AFTER: Decision: "key light angled to rim the phone's top edge brighter than the counter behind it, so it reads
first even where the background stays in focus for legibility."

### Check
- Does the hero carry a visibly stronger highlight than the background, independent of focus?
- Is the background kept legible (not blurred) where the setup needs it to stay readable?

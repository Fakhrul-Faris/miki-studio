## CREATIVE_INTENT
An insert shot — a tight frame on one object (a letter, a clock, a hand) — looks like it's just showing a detail, but
the real function is a decision about attention: the cut tells the audience "this is what matters right now, everything
else disappears." It also creates a question the audience didn't choose to ask ("why are we seeing this?") and carries
it forward until the scene answers it. Hitchcock: you don't have to show the explosion, you just have to show the bomb.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before cutting to a detail, decide what question this insert should plant in the viewer's mind.
2. Frame it tight enough that nothing else competes for attention.
3. Do not explain it immediately — let the question sit for at least one more beat.
4. Pay off the question later in the episode (the mechanism/payoff beat), not in the same setup.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot-list row: mark the setup "insert — plants: <question>" in the events column; the later row that answers it gets
"pays off L0X's insert." raea: this is a framing/timing decision more than a prop decision — isolate the object with
DoF and a tight crop, hold on it slightly longer than its line needs before cutting away.

### Worked example
Topic: a small clinic's consultation fee goes up by RM5 (illustrative numbers).
BEFORE: the fee is simply stated by the voice over a wide shot of the clinic counter.
AFTER: L02 — insert, tight on the printed fee card with the old number still showing, held a beat longer than the
line ("plants: why show us this card now?"); L07 — the same card's number updates to the new fee in the accent
("pays off L02's insert").

### Check
- Does the shot-list name the question an insert plants, and which later row pays it off?
- Is the insert framed tight enough that only that one object can be read?
- Does the payoff happen later, not in the same setup as the plant?

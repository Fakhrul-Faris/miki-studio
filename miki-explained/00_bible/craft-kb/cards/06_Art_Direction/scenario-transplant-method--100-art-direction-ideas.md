## CREATIVE_INTENT
A striking reference ("a model's head coming through an orifice") is not copied directly — it is reduced to its
abstract concept ("someone/something coming out of an odd space"), then re-set inside the user's own brand's existing
visual world (for a car-themed brand: a head coming out of a car's passenger window). The method is explicitly:
abstract the reference's concept, then ask what in your own world lets you restage that concept.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Pick the one reference image/clip that is the actual inspiration for this setup (per Reference review method).
2. State its concept in the fewest words possible, stripped of its specific subject ("X emerging from an enclosed space", not "a model's head through a hole").
3. List what the current episode's own world already has that could stand in for the reference's elements (the hero object, its container, its usual surroundings).
4. Re-stage the abstracted concept using only the episode's own elements — never the reference's literal subject.
5. If nothing in the episode's world fits the concept, don't force it; pick a different reference instead.
6. Check: could a viewer who has seen the original reference recognise it was "copied", or does it read as the episode's own idea?

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame-brief "Ref" and "Idea" fields: "Ref" names the source image/clip; "Idea" states the abstracted concept in one
line; "Decision" states what from this episode's own object/world replaces the reference's subject. This keeps the
reference traceable (per Apply-the-bible-not-the-pipeline) while guaranteeing the final frame isn't a re-skin.

### Worked example
Reference: a jewelry brand's product shot with the model's head emerging through a torn paper hole.
Topic: a workshop's hidden extra labour cost (illustrative).
- Ref: the torn-hole emergence shot.
- Idea (abstracted): something ordinary emerges unexpectedly through a surface that shouldn't open.
- Decision: the invoice itself tears open from inside, and the hidden labour-cost line pokes through the paper.

### Check
- Is the concept stated abstractly enough that it no longer names the reference's specific subject?
- Does the restaged version use only props/objects that already belong to this episode?
- Would the reference's original audience not recognise their shot being reused verbatim?

## NOTES
- Visual gaps: the specific reference images are visual only (brand names "Aroni Idzz" etc. likely mis-transcribed); the method itself does not depend on the exact reference.
- Rule conflicts: none; aligns with "Vector = redraw, never trace" and "generated images are references only" — this is the same discipline applied to live-action/photo references.

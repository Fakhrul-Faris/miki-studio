## CREATIVE_INTENT
A believable off-screen cue doesn't require building the thing that causes it — a light flagged (briefly blocked and
released) can imply a person passing a door; a "prac" (practical lamp) can be invented in a spot that reads as a
plausible source even if nothing physically sits there, as long as the resulting light behaviour is believable: "fake
a prac over here to get back light," "invent a prac over here."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Identify what off-screen event or source the beat implies (someone walking past, a lamp in the next room).
2. Don't build the actual object/person causing it unless it must be seen.
3. Instead, animate only the light's behaviour the event would cause (a brief dip/flag, a glow from an off-frame
   direction) at the implied source's plausible position.
4. Check the light behaviour alone sells the implied cause without needing to show it.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
raea: place a light at the position a real source would occupy (even with no visible fixture modelled) and animate
only its effect (a brief occlusion, a directional glow) — cheaper than building and lighting an off-screen room, and
consistent with Style D's "no people" rule since the implied cause is never shown.

### Worked example
Topic: a laundromat scene implying another customer is active in the next machine over, off-frame.
BEFORE: nothing suggests the shop is otherwise occupied — the hero machine feels alone in an empty void.
AFTER: a faint light dips and releases from off-frame-left for half a second (as if someone briefly blocked a
neighbouring machine's display light), with no figure built or shown — the shop reads as alive without adding a person.

### Check
- Does the implied cause stay entirely off-screen (no built/visible object or figure)?
- Is only the light's behaviour animated, not a modelled source?
- Does the behaviour alone read as a plausible off-screen cause?

## NOTES
- Visual gaps: the specific blocking move (a flag swung to mimic a passerby) is described, not shown in detail.
- Fits "no people in Style D" — the implied person/event is never built or seen, only inferred from light behaviour.

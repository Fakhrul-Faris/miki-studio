## CREATIVE_INTENT
"If your product works out of sight, follow one thing through it." A parcel tracker "makes a 5-day wait feel short,
because you can see where the box is." Maersk explains its supply chain through **one pizza**, each ingredient on its own
card from a different country: "Pick one everyday object your product touches, and trace it end to end. If a viewer can't
name the object afterward, it was too abstract." Moderna gives the courier "a size and a job": 100-nanometer lipid
nanoparticles as "shipping containers" — "Give the thing you follow a job your buyer already knows. If you need a glossary,
find a better job." Innovatrics: "Follow one record… and end on the output your buyer acts on. If it ends on a feature,
keep going until it ends on a decision." PacketLight: "Follow the traffic, not the box. If the data never moves on screen,
it's a product shot." ARNITEL: "If your advantage is invisible, follow what gets through and what doesn't. If both paths
look the same, your viewer can't see the claim." FactSet: follow the customer's data, not your logo. Talkdesk: "If the
install takes more than one shot, simplify the shot."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Pick one everyday object the process touches (a RM10 note, a receipt, one order).
2. Give it a job the viewer already knows ("parcel", "envelope"); drop any term needing a glossary.
3. Plan one setup per stop on its route, in order, start to finish.
4. Keep the object moving on screen at every stop; never cut to a static product shot.
5. If the claim is invisible, run two paths: what gets through and what doesn't.
6. End on the output the viewer acts on (a number, a choice), not on a feature.
7. Collapse any stop that needs more than one setup to explain.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Shot list: each mechanism row = one stop; `Setup and camera` follows the object (camera flight to the next stop, eased,
≤ 130°/s); `State change` = what happens to the object at that stop (split, stamped, shrinks by a fee). Last row ends on a
number/decision, then loops to frame 0. raea: the object is one named `Model`; its `transform` is keyed per stop with
in-out-cubic between rests; the camera's target follows it (scene.md camera moves).

### Worked example
Topic: where one RM5 from a teh tarik goes at a mamak in PJ (illustrative split).
- BEFORE: L05 "Kos operasi tinggi" over a pie chart.
- AFTER: object = one RM5 note. Stop 1 (the cup): RM1.20 tea, milk, sugar peel off. Stop 2 (the wall): RM1.50 rent
  slides to a key. Stop 3 (the worker's apron): RM1.30 wages. Stop 4 (back on the counter): RM1 left glows accent. End
  question on frame-0 framing: "RM1 untung secawan… berbaloi ke buka pukul 3 pagi?" (1.20 + 1.50 + 1.30 + 1 = 5.)

### Check
- Can a viewer name the followed object afterwards?
- Does the object move on screen at every stop?
- Does the route end on a number or decision, not a feature?
- Is every term understandable without a glossary?

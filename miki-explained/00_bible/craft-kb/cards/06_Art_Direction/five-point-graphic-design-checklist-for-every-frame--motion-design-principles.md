## CREATIVE_INTENT
Good motion design starts with good graphic design. Keep a five-item list and evaluate any frame against it whenever
something feels off: hierarchy (the order the eye digests information, set by scale/colour/alignment); balance and
tension (how elements relate structurally — harmonious or deliberately tense); contrast (opposing elements: dark/light,
warm/cool, sharp/soft — for distinction); negative space (room around elements to breathe, or purposeful overcrowding);
repetition/rhythm (reusing the same fonts/colours/styles for consistency, so the audience isn't confused by switching
mid-project).

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. When a frame brief or still doesn't read well, check it against each of the five in order: hierarchy, balance/tension, contrast, negative space, repetition.
2. Hierarchy: is there one clear first-read element, set by scale/colour/alignment, not several competing?
3. Balance/tension: is the structure harmonious (calm beat) or deliberately tense (dramatic beat) — and is that a choice, not an accident?
4. Contrast: does at least one opposing pair (dark/light, warm/cool, sharp/soft) separate the hero from its context?
5. Negative space: is the space around elements doing a job (room to breathe, or purposeful crowding), not just leftover?
6. Repetition: do fonts/colours/styles stay consistent with the rest of the episode, not switching without reason?

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Board-check line: "5-point: hierarchy ✓/✗, balance ✓/✗, contrast ✓/✗, negative space ✓/✗, repetition ✓/✗" — any ✗ gets
one line naming the fix before moving on.

### Worked example
Topic: a laundromat's new pricing board (illustrative numbers).
BEFORE: a still of the board feels flat and unclear why.
AFTER: 5-point run — hierarchy ✗ (three prices at equal size); contrast ✗ (new price isn't separated from old);
fix: scale the new price up (hierarchy) and desaturate the old price while the new one keeps the accent (contrast).
Balance, negative space and repetition already passed.

### Check
- Was the checklist run item by item, not just glanced at?
- Does every ✗ have a one-line, single-lever fix attached?
- Does the fix touch only the failing item(s), not the whole frame?

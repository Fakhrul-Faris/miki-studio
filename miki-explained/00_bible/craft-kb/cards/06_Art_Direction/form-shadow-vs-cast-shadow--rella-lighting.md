## CREATIVE_INTENT
The author's lighting plateaued on technique alone until they studied fundamentals and understood the difference
between **form shadow** (the shadow on an object's own surface, caused by its curvature turning away from the
light) and **cast shadow** (the shadow one object throws onto another surface). Knowing which one you are shaping
clarifies shading decisions that copying a reference image alone does not.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before shading, ask of every dark area: is this the object's own surface turning from the light (form shadow),
   or is it being thrown onto a surface by something blocking the light (cast shadow)?
2. Shape form shadow to follow the object's own curvature/geometry.
3. Shape cast shadow to follow the blocking object's silhouette and the surface it lands on, not the lit object's own form.
4. Don't rely on copying a reference's shadow shapes without knowing which kind each one is — mixing them up reads as a flat, "dirty" image rather than a lit one.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
In a `ref_<shot>.md` or frame brief, tag each dark region in the reference as form or cast shadow before describing
it, so the agent briefs the raea light/gobo setup correctly (a cast shadow needs a gobo/blocker object; a form
shadow needs only the key light's angle against the hero's own geometry).
### Worked example
Topic: a hero stack of ringgit notes under the Style D top light (illustrative).
BEFORE: "add a dark patch on the left side of the notes" — ambiguous, could be either kind of shadow.
AFTER: "form shadow: the top note's own fold curves away from the key light on its left edge" + "cast shadow: the
coin jar beside it throws a hard-edged shadow across the table, not onto the notes themselves" — two different
raea setups (light angle vs a blocker object), briefed correctly.
### Check
- Is every dark region in the frame brief tagged as form or cast shadow, not left ambiguous?
- Does a cast shadow's shape come from the blocking object's silhouette, not the lit object's own geometry?

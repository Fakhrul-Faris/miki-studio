## METADATA
06_Art_Direction | live-action cinematography critique (tool-agnostic) | camera placement relative to the key light

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Once a light source and the camera's relationship to it are established, there's an invisible "line": shoot from the
lit side (looking toward the light) and the subject flattens; move the camera to the shadow side (looking away from
the light, into the shadow) and the same subject gains a three-dimensional, dimensional read — "because then instead
of looking into the light, you're looking into the shadow, and it creates this three-dimensional effect."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before placing the camera, identify the key light's direction relative to the subject.
2. Ask: is the camera looking toward the light (lit side) or away from it (shadow side)?
3. If the camera is on the lit side, move it (or move the subject) to the opposite side so the camera looks into the
   shadow instead.
4. Confirm the subject now reads with visible shadow-side modelling (nose/edge shadow, side falloff), not flat frontal light.
5. Once established for a setup, keep every subsequent camera position in that setup on the shadow side (don't flip
   back and forth within the same beat).

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief "Eye" or "Decision" field: state which side of the key light the camera sits on, and confirm it's the
shadow side. Board check: reject a still where the subject looks flat/frontal-lit when a clear key light direction
exists in the scene.

### Worked example
Topic: a hawker stall's weighing scale lit by one hanging bulb (illustrative numbers).
BEFORE: camera placed directly between the bulb and the scale, looking toward the bulb — the scale reads flat, no
depth to the numbers on its dial.
AFTER: camera moved to the opposite side of the scale, now looking past it into the bulb's shadow — the dial's
engraved numbers pick up visible edge shadow, reading as a real dimensional object instead of a flat graphic.

### Check
- Is the key light's direction identified before the camera position is chosen?
- Does the camera look into the shadow side of the subject, not toward the light?
- Does the subject show visible edge/side shadow in the resulting still (not flat frontal light)?

## CREATIVE_INTENT
A lack of visual hierarchy often isn't about getting size/colour/type wrong — it's a layout too cramped, with no
white space, that "suffocates" the viewer. Giving an element generous surrounding space lets it "breathe," making it
read as more important purely from the space around it, independent of its own size, colour, or type weight.
Hierarchy "literally cannot survive" without proper use of white space.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before finalizing a frame, check how much empty space surrounds the hero/attract element.
2. If the frame feels cramped or every element is touching another, increase the space around the single most important element specifically.
3. Don't compensate for a cramped layout by adding more size, colour, or type weight — fix the space first.
4. Re-check that the hero element now reads as clearly more important once it has room to breathe.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
In the board check, add a specific white-space pass separate from the existing "subject in the 25–60% band" check:
confirm the hero has visibly more clear space around it than any secondary element, not just that it sits in the
right size band.
### Worked example
Topic: a board still of a hero receipt total surrounded by tightly packed secondary props (illustrative).
BEFORE: the receipt sits correctly in the 25–60% band, but props crowd right up against its edges — it doesn't pop.
AFTER: secondary props are pulled back, leaving clear dark-room space around the receipt — the same object, same
size, now reads as unmistakably the hero.
### Check
- Does the hero element have visibly more surrounding clear space than any secondary element?
- If a frame still feels cramped, was space increased before reaching for more size/colour/weight?

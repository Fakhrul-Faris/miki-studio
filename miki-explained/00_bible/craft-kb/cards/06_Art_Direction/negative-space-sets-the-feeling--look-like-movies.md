## METADATA
06_Art_Direction | general (Lean, *Mr. Robot*) | frame brief (Feeling, Eye) + board check

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Placement decides where you look; negative space decides "what you feel while you're looking". "The more empty space
around him, the smaller he gets and the deeper his loneliness" (*Lawrence of Arabia*). Where the space sits matters: in
*Mr. Robot* he is "pushed down into a corner, and looming over him is a huge empty wall — small and crushed". Ahead of a
gaze leave more room than behind; take it away and the subject "looks cornered"; push it to the edge "and the frame
starts to tip".

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before you frame, write the line's feeling: alone, crushed, free, cornered, heading somewhere.
2. Alone: shrink the subject toward ~25 % of frame height with even empty space around.
3. Crushed: put the subject low in frame with a large empty area above it.
4. Heading somewhere: leave more space on the side the object faces or moves toward.
5. Cornered: put the facing side close to the frame edge.
6. Keep headroom tight in close setups unless the line is one of the above.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: Feeling "<word>" → Decision "negative space: <amount> <where>". raea: camera distance/FOV sets the amount;
camera tilt/offset sets where; the dark room keeps the space clean. Board check still applies (subject ≥ ~25 %, no
clipping, caption zone clear).

### Worked example
Topic: "Gerai kecil lawan francais besar: macam mana nak hidup" (illustrative).
- BEFORE frame brief: Decision "medium shot of the gerai's tray of kuih, centred, filling 55 %".
- AFTER: Feeling "crushed"; Decision "the tray sits low (bottom third, above captions) at ~28 % height; a huge dark empty area above, the chain's lit sign far off as a blur at the top edge"; Eye "the tray, then RM1 per piece (illustrative) in accent".

### Check
- Does the amount of empty space match the line's feeling?
- Is the space placed (above / ahead / around) for that feeling?
- Is the subject still ≥ ~25 % of frame height and above captions?

## ADDENDUM — 8 Cinematic Fundamentals for 3D Short Films #b3d
Adds a distinct two-element technique this card doesn't cover (single-subject focus only): clear negative space
left between two elements reads as their relationship/communication feeling unobstructed; placing one more element
into that exact gap — without moving either original element — immediately reads as a block, obstruction or
interruption. Use for two objects/UI panels meant to feel connected (a price and a total that should "talk" to each
other): leave the space between them clean when the line is about that clear relationship, and when the line
introduces a complication, add one element straight into that gap (a surcharge, a warning icon) rather than
restaging the frame — the interruption reads from the gap closing, not from anything else changing.

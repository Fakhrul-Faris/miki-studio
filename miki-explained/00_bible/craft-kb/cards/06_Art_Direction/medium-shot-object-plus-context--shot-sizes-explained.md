## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Perhaps the most popular shot size in all of cinema" because it's neutral — "neither dramatic like a close-up nor
distancing like a wide shot"; it captures a subject at "a size similar to how we interact with people" (above the
waist to just above the head). In Coco it holds the props of Miguel's shrine, the detail on the TV screen and his
reaction in one frame. Use it to dig in "without losing physicality or environment", or for "a true middle-ground
approach that is neither jarring nor especially dramatic".

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Frame the object at the distance you'd see it in daily life (on a counter, in a hand-free view).
2. Include the one thing it acts on and one context prop.
3. Keep all three readable; nothing clipped.
4. Use it after a CU/ECU to let the viewer re-orient.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: Decision = "MS, eye-level, object 40 %, context prop 15 %". raea: ~50 mm equivalent, camera at counter
height.

### Worked example
Topic: "Timbang sayur — kenapa harga lain kat kaunter" (tare/plastic weight arithmetic, illustrative: 1.02 kg vs 1.00 kg at RM5/kg).
- BEFORE: ECU on the scale display only.
- AFTER: `| 5 | "Plastik pun kena timbang" | MS · scale with a bag of sawi and the price card beside it | display shows 1.02 kg, plastic bag edge in accent on "plastik" |`

### Check
- Are the object, what it acts on and one context prop all in frame?
- Is it at everyday distance and eye level?
- Does it follow a tighter setup?

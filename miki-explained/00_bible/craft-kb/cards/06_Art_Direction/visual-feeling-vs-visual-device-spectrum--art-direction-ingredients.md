## CREATIVE_INTENT
Visual decisions sit on a spectrum. On one end, "visual feeling": the voice-over or on-screen text already carries the
message, and the visuals just help the audience *feel* it (Polaroid's handwritten pen, Oatly's blocky type). On the
other end, "visual device": the pairing of symbols carries the whole message with no copy needed at all (LEGO +
mismatched shadow, hashtag + face, knife through cutting board). Neither is better — pick per line.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. For each line, ask: does the voice already state the fact plainly?
2. If yes, the visual's job is feeling — choose texture/light/prop that makes the stated fact land emotionally.
3. If the line is abstract or the fact needs to be *seen* to be believed, design a visual device: two objects whose
   pairing alone proves the point, no caption needed.
4. Mark the choice in the frame brief so later setups aren't built to the wrong end of the spectrum.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add a field to the frame brief: `Spectrum: feeling | device`. For "device," the Idea field should state the two
paired objects and what their pairing proves, with no reliance on the caption. For "feeling," the Idea field names
texture/light/prop choices that reinforce the spoken line.

### Worked example
Topic: a laundromat where a coin counts down to show the real cost per wash (numbers illustrative).
Line: "Basuh sekali — RM3.50 terus hilang."
BEFORE (feeling only): nice lighting on a washing machine while the line is read.
AFTER (device): a coin visibly drops into the slot and the display ticks straight from RM3.50 to RM0.00 in one cut —
the pairing (coin in / balance gone) proves the cost with no caption needed; the spoken line becomes a confirmation,
not the carrier.

### Check
- Is each line marked feeling or device, not left ambiguous?
- For a device line, does the visual prove the point even muted, no caption?
- For a feeling line, does the chosen texture/light/prop actually reinforce the spoken fact (not just look nice)?

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Any pose touching the ground looks wrong if weight is ignored (unless you want a symmetrical "Toyota ad" stance).
Weight-bearing **pillar limbs** are closer to straight; if bent, they read tense and strong. The other limbs float.
The easiest dynamic version: weight mostly on one leg, emphasised by how that foot meets the ground plane.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before posing, pick which limb carries the weight.
2. Keep the pillar limb near straight; bend it only to show tension/strength.
3. Let non-bearing limbs relax.
4. Put most weight on one leg rather than both equally.
5. Show the contact: foot flat and pressed on the ground plane, with a contact shadow.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Style A/C: choose an MPFB pose with asymmetric weight; check feet sit on the floor with a contact shadow (no float).
Style D (no people): the same logic for objects — one contact point visibly bearing the load (a coin stack's base
pressing into the receipt, a phone's corner on the counter with a tight contact shadow / AO).
### Worked example
- BEFORE (frame brief Decision): "Stall owner stands at the counter" — both feet even, figure floats slightly.
- AFTER: "Owner holds a lean: weight on left leg (straight), right knee relaxed, left hand on counter; both soles on the
  tiles with contact shadow." Style D: "Phone rests on one corner against the QR stand; AO under the corner reads the weight."
### Check
- Is one limb/contact clearly bearing weight?
- Is there a visible contact shadow where it meets the surface?
- Is the stance asymmetric unless symmetry is the point?

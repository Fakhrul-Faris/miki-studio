## METADATA
06_Art_Direction | Evan Abrams | planning principle + mute check

## CREATIVE_INTENT
At script stage decide: "Are we gonna lean on audio, lean more on video, or are the two gonna play off each other?"
Ideally both are stellar and build on each other — "one doesn't lead and the other lag". Television needs strong audio
to stop people looking at their phones; on Facebook/Twitter "they probably won't hear anything — sound is off by
default — so the video component has to be the strongest." He recommends a **conceptually driven** piece, where the
strongest elements tie audio and video together.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before the shot list, write one sentence: the concept that ties voice and picture.
2. Assume many viewers watch muted; make every line's point readable from picture + caption.
3. Put the line's key number or object on screen, not only in the voice.
4. Watch the preview muted once; mark any row whose point is lost.
5. Fix marked rows in the picture (object state, number on a label), not by adding more text.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add "Concept:" to the top of the shot list. Add a "muted read" pass to the preview review notes.

### Worked example
Topic: hidden cost of free delivery for a kedai (illustrative).
BEFORE: L05 voice "Penghantaran percuma tu kos kedai RM6 setiap order." Picture: generic van icon.
AFTER: Concept: "every 'free' has an RM tag." Picture: macro of a delivery box; on "RM6" a price tag unfolds from under
the flap, in the accent colour. Muted, the tag still says RM6.

### Check
- Is there a one-sentence concept tying voice and picture?
- Does each row's point survive a muted watch?
- Are key numbers in the picture, not only in the voice?

## NOTES
- Source's sound-off claim is about 2019-era Facebook/Twitter; treated as a planning assumption, not a TikTok statistic.

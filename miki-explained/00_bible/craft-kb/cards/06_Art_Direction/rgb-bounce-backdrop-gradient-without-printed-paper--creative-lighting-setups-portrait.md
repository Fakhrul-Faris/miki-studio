## CREATIVE_INTENT
Bounce two small RGB lights, each set to a different hue, onto a plain light-coloured backdrop or wall to fake a
two-colour gradient; changing only the hue values restyles the whole set without moving anything physical.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Use a plain light-coloured backdrop or wall — no printed gradient needed.
2. Place two small RGB lights pointed at the backdrop, one higher, one lower (or either side).
3. Set each light to a different hue to form a gradient between them.
4. Change only the hue values to produce new looks from the same physical setup.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
⚠ Adapted: our house rule keeps ONE saturated brand accent only on the line's subject — a two-hue gradient backdrop
would compete with it. Use only ONE coloured bounce light, set to the brand accent at low intensity, against an
otherwise neutral/dark backdrop, so the backdrop gets a soft accent-coloured glow on one side rather than a
two-colour gradient.

### Worked example
BEFORE: a plain flat dark backdrop behind a product shot.
AFTER: frame brief Decision: "backdrop: one small light bounces low-intensity brand-accent colour onto the lower
third of the dark backdrop; the rest stays neutral/black — not a two-colour gradient."

### Check
- Is only one coloured bounce used, and is its colour the single brand accent?
- Does the rest of the backdrop stay neutral/dark rather than taking on a second hue?

## NOTES
- ⚠ Rule conflict with the one-accent-colour house rule; adapted to a single accent-coloured bounce as above.

## METADATA

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Two modes of mise-en-scène: **naturalistic** (the world looks like ours) and **theatrical** — "fantastical environments
that display and even exult in their artificial and constructed nature that bend the laws of nature and society"
(Corrigan & White). Burton works theatrically from day one, drawing on German expressionism, monster movies, science
fiction and Ray Harryhausen — all "genres and styles that bend the laws of nature". The test: is it style for style's
sake, or do the story and themes support such expressive design?

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before the frame briefs, choose the episode's mode: naturalistic, theatrical, or naturalistic look + theatrical behaviour.
2. For each setup that bends a law (object floats, opens, multiplies), name the law and why the line needs it.
3. Cut any bend the line does not need ("style for style's sake").
4. Keep materials and light consistent with the chosen mode across all setups.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Art direction lines: add "Mode: naturalistic surfaces/light, theatrical behaviour (bends: gravity, opacity, scale)".
Each frame brief "Not generic because" names the bent law and the line it serves. Board check: flag a setup whose bend
has no line behind it.
### Worked example
Topic: "Duit e-wallet korang sebenarnya duduk mana?" (illustrative)
- BEFORE: phone spins and glows for no reason; coins fly around the frame in L02.
- AFTER: Mode: real phone, real counter, one top light (naturalistic); bends only where lines ask — L04 "duit tu
  sebenarnya kat bank" → the phone screen turns transparent (opacity law) and a bank vault interior sits inside it at
  macro scale (scale law); every other setup obeys physics.
### Check
- Is the mode written once in the art-direction lines?
- Does every law-bending event name the line that needs it?
- Is any bend there "for style's sake"? If yes, cut it.

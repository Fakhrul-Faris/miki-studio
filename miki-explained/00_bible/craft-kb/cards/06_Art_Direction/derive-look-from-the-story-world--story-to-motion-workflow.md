## CREATIVE_INTENT
"When we truly understand who we're helping and why, their story becomes the foundation for the style, mood, and tone."
Script → storyboard → style frames → animation, each derived from the story. The look was mixed-media because the
protagonist was an educator and entrepreneur; classroom elements entered the set because he was a teacher; a "snapshot
system" of photo frames let every viewer imagine swapping in their own family photos. Brand colours were the foundation;
hierarchy, colour and contrast emphasised key points; timing and exaggeration reinforced them; music "needed to sound
like the joy of revitalising a home".

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before briefing any frame, list 5 objects, surfaces and sounds that exist in the topic's real world.
2. Pick the hero surface from that list (counter, stall table, laundromat bench).
3. Dress each setup only with props from the list.
4. Add one "swap-in" prop the viewer could replace with their own (their receipt, their phone screen).
5. Put the brand accent on the object the line is about, not on decoration.
6. Pick room tone and SFX from the same world.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief: "Not generic because" names which list item ties the frame to the topic's world. `ref_<shot>.md` materials
row lists the real surfaces. Sound notes in the shot list draw from the same list (sparse SFX rule still applies).
### Worked example
Topic: a kedai gunting rambut's daily takings (generic shop, illustrative).
BEFORE frame brief: "Hero: a gold coin on a dark gradient."
AFTER: world list = barber chair vinyl, steel comb tray, clipper, talc tin, cash drawer. Hero surface = the steel comb
tray; hero = the day's notes fanned on it; swap-in prop = a phone showing a DuitNow notification; room tone = a distant
clipper hum.
### Check
- Does every prop in the frame come from the topic's world list?
- Is there one swap-in prop the viewer could own?
- Is the accent on the line's object only?

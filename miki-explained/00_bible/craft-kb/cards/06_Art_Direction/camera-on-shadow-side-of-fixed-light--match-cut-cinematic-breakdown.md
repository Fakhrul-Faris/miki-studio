## CREATIVE_INTENT
"You need to be actively thinking about where your light is coming from and you need to position your camera on the shadow side... if you position your camera with your back towards the light... you get a very flat image, everything is lit exactly uniformly. That's like having top-down lighting. Ugly. Whereas if you move your camera round to the shadow side, you get shadows and then light behind it, and you get this immediate depth and contrast." Framed as "the number one rule" for turning a flat shot cinematic.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before placing a new camera setup, note the one fixed light's direction relative to the hero object.
2. Never place the camera on the same axis facing into the light (that reads flat/uniform).
3. Place the camera on the side where the object shows both a lit face and a visible shadow side in frame.
4. Confirm the frame shows a bright edge AND a shadow with depth, not uniform flat lighting.
5. Re-check this for every new setup in the shot list — the light doesn't move, but the camera does, every 2–3 s.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame-brief "Eye" field: state which side of the object (relative to the fixed top light) the camera reads from, and confirm it shows both lit + shadow faces. Ref_<shot>.md "camera" field notes the angle offset from the light axis.

### Worked example
Topic: a pasar malam stall's weighing scale (illustrative).
BEFORE: camera placed facing straight into the key light's direction — the scale reads flat, no edge definition.
AFTER: camera moved ~45–60° off the light axis so the scale's dial rim catches light while its base falls into shadow → frame-brief "Eye: scale rim lit, base in shadow, reads with depth" → camera-on-shadow-side-of-fixed-light.

### Check
- Does the frame show both a lit edge and a visible shadow, not flat uniform light?
- Is the camera off-axis from the light direction, never dead-on facing into it?
- Does this hold for every new setup, not just one hero shot?

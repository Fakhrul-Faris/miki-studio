## CREATIVE_INTENT
There's no "ideas tab". Start with research and a brain-dump mind map, collecting imagery at the same time. If the brief
comes from source footage (film, TV, corporate video), get familiar with it first — he always watched the film before
making its DVD menu. For a looping Psycho animation: stairs feature in several scenes → broaden to **lines** generally →
link to the title-sequence lines, the bathroom tiles, the clapperboard. "This is where we start making creative links."
Research isn't a token mind map; it's how you organise what you collected — scatter the jigsaw pieces on the table.
The final frame layered film elements, title-sequence lines, a Hitchcock image, and concentric circles (a nod to another
Hitchcock poster and a recurring motif in the film).

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before frame briefs, collect the business's real material: receipts, labels, screens, packaging, the counter.
2. Brain-dump every recurring object and shape into a mind map.
3. Pick one recurring concrete thing and broaden it to its shape class (receipt lines → lines; QR squares → grid).
4. List every place that shape already appears in the episode's objects.
5. Choose the motif that appears in ≥ 3 setups (~).
6. Use it to join setups: one shape becomes the next object's matching shape.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Frame brief **Idea** names the motif; **Moves/Handover** says which shape carries into the next setup. Shot list
transitions: "→ T: receipt lines slide into barcode bars".

### Worked example
Topic: a kedai runcit's daily takings (illustrative).
BEFORE: setups unrelated: shelf, till, phone app, chart; joined by fades.
AFTER: motif = **lines**: shelf rows (L02) → barcode bars on a tin (L04, camera pushes into the bars) → receipt lines
printing (L06) → the receipt lines rise into a bar chart of the week (L08) → back to shelf rows (L11 loop).

### Check
- Did the motif come from the business's own objects?
- Does it appear in ≥ ~3 setups?
- Does each use carry into the next setup instead of a generic transition?

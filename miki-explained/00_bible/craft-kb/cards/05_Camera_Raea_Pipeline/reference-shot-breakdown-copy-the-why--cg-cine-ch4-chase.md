## CREATIVE_INTENT
Principle: when working from a reference sequence (here Star Trek Into Darkness's corridor chase), break it down shot by shot and note why each camera exists. Some cameras orient the audience ("where are we, who chases whom"), and are smooth and readable on purpose; some exist to be cool and dynamic (subject nearly hits the lens); some feature one thing (the waterfall), so the moving subjects are allowed to leave frame because the feature is the main actor. The reference's motivation can differ from yours (theirs: entertain; theirs again: showcase effects) — adapt pacing to your goal (slower ships to let effects read).
Ideas: tag every setup in our shot list as orient / feature / energy; a run of only energy shots confuses, a run of only orient shots bores.

## STE_PROCEDURE
Method:
1. Watch the reference through; list each shot with duration.
2. For each shot, write its job: orient, feature (name it), or energy.
3. Note what makes it work: subject distance to lens, speed, composition balance, camera behaviour (smooth CG vs operated).
4. Note where your goal differs from the reference's and adjust speed and shot length for it.
5. Build your shot list with the same jobs in a similar rhythm, not the same images.

## CREATIVE_INTENT
The midpoint (50 %) is "a big moment… everything that has led up to this is then going to turn". Weiland calls it "the
moment of truth": the character's perspective on the conflict "completely changes"; before it they are "reactive… trying
to find their feet"; it "shows them what they're really up against", and after it they turn "proactive". Jurassic Park's
T-Rex escape: "something iconic… really big… that massively turns the plot". Later, the third plot point is "a false
victory followed by the low moment" — the win costs more than counted on.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Place the reveal of the real cause/rule at the middle line (L05–L06).
2. Before it, write the subject reacting (guessing, losing); after it, acting on the rule.
3. Give the midpoint the episode's biggest visual state change.
4. Optionally before the climax, add a win that costs more than expected (false victory → low).

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Script: label L05/L06 `midpoint`. Shot list: the midpoint row returns to the hero with the largest change (x-ray reveals
the cause, object splits, scale travel). Frame brief `Feeling:` "now I get it".

### Worked example
Topic: why a bubble-tea kiosk is busy but broke (illustrative).
- BEFORE: L05 "Ada banyak faktor kenapa untung rendah."
- AFTER: L04 "Dia tambah menu, tambah promo, still rugi." (reacting) L05 "Rupanya, setiap cup pakai straw dan seal
  RM0.40 — dan dia bagi free extra topping RM0.80." (truth: cup x-rays to show the hidden RM1.20) L06 "Jadi dia buang
  topping free." (acting)

### Check
- Does the middle line reveal what the subject is really up against?
- Is there reacting before and acting after?
- Does the midpoint row carry the biggest state change?

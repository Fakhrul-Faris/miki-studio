## METADATA
07_Script_Story | general | Script L01–L03 + on-screen number

## CREATIVE_INTENT
A mess without a price is decoration: "Name the hours your buyer's mess costs, then make the mess disappear. If your
before-state has no cost attached, it's just a messy picture" (Google Cloud: manual tracking eats engineering time).
"Give the risk a clock. If your problem scene has no sense of time running out, it reads as a diagram, not a story"
(VERITAS: darkening cloud, ticking time bomb, cascade of "OFFLINE"). "If your buyer's mess is coming rather than here,
put the date on screen. A deadline turns a forecast into a story" (Hitachi, 2050 goals). Make the cost personal first,
then scale it: EU tax and customs shows a €100 note vanishing from a pocket, then the loss climbing to €50 billion a year.
Draw the failure "from the customer's side of the counter" (Esper: frozen update, offline kiosk, frustrated shoppers).

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before writing L02, state the mess's cost as one RM figure or hours per week/month.
2. Show the personal unit first (one RM note, one hour), then the scaled total.
3. Add a clock if the problem has one: a deadline, a date, a count running down.
4. Put the date/number on the hero object, not in a floating title.
5. Show the failure from the customer's side of the counter, not the back office.
6. If no cost or clock is real, mark the line "no cost found" and rethink the angle.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Script: L01 or L02 carries the cost; claim-ledger row for the number (verified/illustrative). Shot list: the number
appears on the object (receipt total, till display, calendar page) on the word that says it; raea `tick({ to, prefix:
"RM" })` at `word("…")` for a rising cost. Personal → scaled = two setups: one note close, then a pull-out to the
month's stack with a counter.

### Worked example
Topic: a car wash in Seremban that lets customers pay "nanti" (illustrative).
- BEFORE: L02 "Hutang pelanggan boleh jadi masalah besar."
- AFTER: L02 "Seorang customer hutang RM15. Nampak sikit je." L03 "Tapi 4 orang seminggu, sebulan dah RM240 hilang."
  (4 × RM15 × 4 weeks.) Clock: a wall calendar on the counter flips to the 30th as the counter reaches RM240.

### Check
- Does the problem carry one RM or hours figure?
- Is the personal unit shown before the scaled total?
- If time matters, is there a visible clock or date?
- Is the arithmetic in the example plain and correct?

## NOTES
- ⚠ VERITAS's "ticking time bomb" and "OFFLINE" cascade are alarm clichés → use the business's own clock (calendar,
  closing time, due date) instead.

## METADATA
07_Script_Story | Styleframe Saturdays / Formerly studio (Del Sordo brand film), Lisa Cron "Story or Die" | topic gate

## CREATIVE_INTENT
From the brief, find "the who and the why": who it is for and why the problem is worth solving for them. Step into their
shoes ("What challenges are we facing? Which needs haven't been met? What really matters to us and why?"). Del Sordo's
customers had a surface need (someone to clean the house exterior), but the misbelief, "their emotional problem", was
anxiety about whom to trust with something so personal (the home as pride, safety, memories). Lisa Cron: "Your goal is
to find out why those reasons have the meaning, the emotional weight that they do." The story locks before any design.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Write the viewer in 1 line: who, situation, age band.
2. Write their surface need in ≤ 10 words.
3. Ask "why does that matter to them?" twice; write each answer.
4. Write the misbelief: the wrong belief or fear holding them back, in ≤ 15 words.
5. Choose the hero object that sits where that fear lives.
6. Lock these four lines before outlining or designing anything.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
`episode.md` "Viewer" block above the script: who · surface need · why ×2 · misbelief · hero object. The hook states the
misbelief as a contradiction; the payoff RM number disproves it.
### Worked example
Topic: a goreng pisang seller thinking about accepting QR payments (generic stall).
BEFORE (surface): "Cara setup QR untuk gerai."
AFTER: surface need "terima bayaran tanpa tunai" → why: customers walk away with no cash → why: each walk-away is a lost
RM5 sale · misbelief: "QR untuk kedai besar, gerai kecil tak perlu." · hero: the stall's cash tin, half empty.
Hook: "Gerai kecil. Pelanggan lari sebab tak ada tunai."
### Check
- Are surface need and misbelief written as different lines?
- Does the misbelief come from two "why" steps, not from the brief's wording?
- Is the hero object chosen from where the fear lives?

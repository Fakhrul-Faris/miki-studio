## CREATIVE_INTENT
Most stories don't move at one pace. Robert McKee: "we coach the audience to move like a long-distance runner who,
rather than loping at a constant pace, speeds, slows, then speeds again, creating cycles". Pacing applies at micro
(scene) and macro (sequence, whole film) level.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before writing lines, tag each beat F (fast) or S (slow).
2. Make the hook and open loop F (short lines, ~1 setup per 2 s).
3. Give the mechanism a steady middle tempo (2–3 s setups).
4. Make the proof/payoff the one S beat: one longer line, one setup held ~3–4 s.
5. Speed up again for the end question (short line, ≤ 7 words on screen).
6. Check that no two S beats are adjacent.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add an F/S column to the script table and use it for shot-list durations (F rows ≤ 2.5 s, the S row up to ~4 s, the
RECIPE-D cap). In S16 how-tos: steps are F, "why it matters" (the RM number) is S.
### Worked example
Topic: "Harga nasi lemak naik — untung peniaga turun?" (illustrative numbers).
- BEFORE: 11 lines all ~16 words, every setup 3 s — a constant lope.
- AFTER: L01–L03 F ("Nasi lemak naik 50 sen." / "Tapi untung makcik turun."); L04–L08 middle (cost per bungkus: beras, santan, telur); L09 S — "Sebungkus RM3, kos dah RM2.60, tinggal 40 sen je." held 4 s on the receipt; L10–L11 F — "Jadi siapa yang untung sebenarnya?"
### Check
- Is there exactly one S beat, and is it the payoff/RM number?
- Do F beats bracket it before and after?
- Does the whole script still fit 175–200 words?

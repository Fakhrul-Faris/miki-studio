## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Mix sentence lengths, favor active phrasing and concrete nouns, and read every line aloud to catch clunky phrasing."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before you hand over the voice text, read each line aloud at ~3.4 words/s.
2. Follow every long sentence (≥ 12 words) with a short one (≤ 5 words).
3. Rewrite passive lines as active ("harga dinaikkan" → "kedai naikkan harga").
4. Replace each abstract noun with a thing you can film.
5. Mark the spot where you stumbled and rewrite that line.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Run on the script table before the voice text. Short lines are natural event points for the shot list (one event per
short sentence).

### Worked example
- BEFORE: "Harga asal yang dipaparkan oleh kedai mungkin telah dimanipulasi untuk mewujudkan persepsi penjimatan."
- AFTER: "Kedai tulis harga asal RM299. Padahal tak pernah jual RM299 pun. Reka je."

### Check
- Is there a short line after every long one?
- Is every verb active?
- Did each line survive a read-aloud without stumbling?

## ADDENDUM — How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and blow up your content)
Source: 'Hooks, Open Loops & "But:Therefore" Pacing/How to Create Irresistible Hooks (and blow up your content).txt' (15:09–15:36).
- In the hook specifically, skip the long/short alternation and use staccato short sentences only — compress "as
  much as you can", since short sentences raise the density of value per word when time (the first ~4 s swipe
  decision) is most expensive. Start alternating long/short only from L04 onward.
| parameter | Irresistible Hooks (Kallaway) | recommended for Style D |
|---|---|---|
| hook sentence length | staccato, short only | L01–L03 ≤ ~8 words each (~), then grow toward the 15-word cap in the Mechanism |
| arc | go shorter initially, expand to medium/longer as the video extends | mixed-length rule applies after the hook |

## ADDENDUM — How To Become A Master Storyteller
Source: 'Hooks, Open Loops & "But:Therefore" Pacing/How To Become A Master Storyteller.txt' (03:21–05:09).
- Named method (quoting Gary Provost's "This sentence has five words..." passage): several same-length sentences in
  a row read as "a stuck record"; vary length to "write music" — short + medium, and sometimes (when the viewer is
  rested) one longer sentence as a crescendo.
- Visual check: write every sentence on its own line; looking down the page, the right edge should look jagged, not
  flush — equal lengths create "monotonous predictability" that makes viewers churn subconsciously.
- Extra step: scan the word-count column; if 3 consecutive lines are within ±2 words of each other, rewrite one to
  be clearly shorter or longer.

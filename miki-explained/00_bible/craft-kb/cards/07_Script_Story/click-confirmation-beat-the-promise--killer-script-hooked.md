## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Content psychology really boils down to one single principle: expectations vs reality." When reality beats expectations
people keep watching; when it falls short "they stop watching and you lose". So "the first few lines of the intro confirm
and ideally beat their expectations set by the title. This is called click confirmation." MrBeast example: title "I spent
100 hours inside the pyramids" → first sentence shows he's in the pyramids (meets), then "I have unprecedented access to
explore pieces that have never been explored before" (beats). "This positive gap of reality beating expectations hooks the
viewer." Slight wording changes in the title "completely change the incoming expectation", so know the expectation before
writing the intro.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before you write L01, write the expectation that frame-0 text + cover create, in one sentence.
2. Make L01 confirm that promise within its first sentence (same object, same claim).
3. Make L02 add one thing the viewer didn't expect (a bigger number, a hidden detail, a rule).
4. Keep the extra true and checkable; log it in the claim ledger.
5. If frame-0 wording changes, re-check L01–L02 against the new expectation.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
In `episode.md`, under frame-0 text, write `Expectation:` (what a viewer now expects) and `Beat:` (the L02 extra). Shot
list rows for L01 show the frame-0 object doing exactly what the text says; L02's row reveals the extra on the object
(x-ray, flip, counter).

### Worked example
Frame-0 text: "Semua RM5. Tapi untung dia besar." (a generic RM5 shop, illustrative)
- BEFORE: L01 "Hari ni kita cerita pasal bisnes runcit." (doesn't confirm) 
- AFTER: L01 "Kedai ni jual semua barang RM5." (confirms) L02 "Tapi ada barang yang kos dia cuma RM1.20 je." (beats:
  RM3.80 margin on a RM5 item, plain arithmetic).

### Check
- Is the frame-0 expectation written down before L01?
- Does L01's first sentence confirm it?
- Does L02 add something the viewer didn't expect, true and checkable?

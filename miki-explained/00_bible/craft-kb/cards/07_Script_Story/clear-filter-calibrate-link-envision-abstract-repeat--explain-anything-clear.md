## CREATIVE_INTENT
Experts struggle most to explain what they know, often using complex language to seem smart; listeners pretend to
follow and disengage. The CLEAR filter: **C — Calibrate**: know how much the audience already knows before explaining
(assume everything → you lose them; assume nothing → you talk down); ask a qualifying question. **L — Link**: connect
the unknown to the known with an analogy, metaphor or simile (Dr Michelle Thaller: "the human brain is as far from
perceiving the universe as it really is as a grasshopper is from perceiving quantum mechanics"). **E — Envision**: when
words alone are too hard, draw it — a triangle, circle, rectangle, a simple diagram. **A — Abstraction**: boil the idea
to its simplest form. **R — Repeat**: at the end, close the loop and come back to the shape of the idea once more.
"If you can't explain it to a six-year-old, you don't understand it well enough."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Calibrate: before drafting, write what our viewer already knows (18–34, non-follower, a 12-year-old's vocabulary).
2. Link: for the one unknown idea, write one analogy to something every viewer has handled.
3. Envision: name the shape or diagram the idea becomes (bar split, stack, ladder, loop).
4. Abstraction: write the idea in one sentence of ≤ 15 everyday words.
5. Repeat: make the last line return to the opening object or shape.
6. Run the five letters as a checklist on script v1; fix any letter that fails.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add a 5-line CLEAR block under the Outline in `episode.md`. Envision → shot-list rows where the hero becomes the
diagram (Style D "become a chart"). Repeat → RECIPE-D loop: last frame returns to frame 0's object and framing; the end
line is still a question, not a summary list.

### Worked example
Topic: why a catering business "untung" but has no cash (credit sales; numbers illustrative).
BEFORE: "Keuntungan perakaunan tidak sama dengan aliran tunai disebabkan akaun belum terima."
AFTER: C — viewer knows "untung" and "hutang", not "aliran tunai". L — "Macam kawan pinjam duit: dalam buku ada, dalam
dompet takde." E — one RM10,000 invoice stack splits: RM3,000 paid (cash), RM7,000 still owed (grey). A — "Kerja dah
siap, duit belum masuk." R — end on the same invoice stack: "Korang lebih rela untung atas kertas, atau duit dalam
tangan?"

### Check
- Is there a one-line note of what the viewer already knows?
- Does the unknown idea have one analogy to something everyday?
- Is there a named shape/diagram for it?
- Can the idea be said in ≤ 15 everyday words?
- Does the ending return to the opening object/shape?

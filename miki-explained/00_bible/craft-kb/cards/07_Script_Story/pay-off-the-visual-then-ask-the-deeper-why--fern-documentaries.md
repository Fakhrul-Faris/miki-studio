## CREATIVE_INTENT
A visual hook alone gets the click; it doesn't hold. "This video isn't a press release to tell you that an aquarium
broke — that's what CNN is for, and frankly nobody cares." Viewers stay because the video makes them ask a deeper
question: **why** did it break? The formula: "an inherently visual hook to centre the video around and a deeper
question to ask about it." That question "is the heart of your video".

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before you write L02, write the deeper question in ≤ 8 words, starting with "why" or "how".
2. Pay off the visual hook first (show the event or object fully).
3. Ask or imply the deeper question in the very next line.
4. Make every Mechanism line a step toward answering that question.
5. Answer it in the Proof/Payoff beat, not before.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
`episode.md` script header: "Deeper question: …". L01 = visual hook paid off; L02–L03 = the question (in S16 how-tos,
the question is the problem behind "Kalau korang nak…"); L04–L08 answer it; L09 pays it off with the RM number. Each
shot-list row in L04–L08 should visibly advance the answer.

### Worked example
Visual hook: a coffee cup at a kopitiam costing RM2.20 next to a café's RM14 latte (illustrative).
- BEFORE: L02 "Harga kopi memang lain-lain ikut kedai."
- AFTER: L02 "Biji kopi sama je. Jadi kenapa beza RM11.80?" — deeper question: "Apa sebenarnya korang bayar?"
  L04–L08 break the RM14 into rent, staff, milk, cup; L09 shows the coffee itself is ~RM1.

### Check
- Is the deeper question written in ≤ 8 words?
- Does it come right after the hook pays off?
- Does each Mechanism line advance its answer?
- Is the answer held back until the payoff beat?

## NOTES
- Source says curiosity "is emotional, not logical" — viewers don't consciously know the why is why they stay.

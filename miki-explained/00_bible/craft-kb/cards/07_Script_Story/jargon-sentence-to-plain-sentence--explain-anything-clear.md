## CREATIVE_INTENT
Jargon is often used to sound clever. His tax example: "Based on your assessable income and carried-forward deductions,
we'll maximise your concessional contributions and apply the available offsets to minimise your taxable income" = "You
earn this much and you can claim a few things, so we'll set it up so you don't pay more tax than you need to." His
workshop pitch went from a full curriculum list (vocal image, rate, volume, tonality, pitch, melody, pausing…) to one
metaphor: "Day one I teach your engineers to play their instrument; day two, how to write great music" — two days of
content in under 20 seconds. Simplifying doesn't make you seem dumb; viewers stay because they can follow.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before you lock a line, underline every term a 12-year-old wouldn't say.
2. Write what the term means with "korang/you", a verb and an RM number.
3. Replace the line with that version.
4. If a list of ≥ 4 items remains, group it under one everyday picture (instrument / music).
5. Read both versions aloud; keep the one a non-expert repeats back correctly.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Keep a two-column "expert → plain" note beside the claim ledger so the plain line stays true to the verified fact.

### Worked example
Topic: a kedai bundle deal (illustrative).
BEFORE: "Margin kasar produk ini ialah 35 peratus selepas kos barang dijual."
AFTER: "Jual RM10, modal RM6.50. RM3.50 masuk poket." (check: 3.50 ÷ 10 = 35 %.)

### Check
- Is every analyst term replaced by a verb + RM number?
- Does the plain line still match the verified fact?
- Are long lists grouped under one everyday picture?

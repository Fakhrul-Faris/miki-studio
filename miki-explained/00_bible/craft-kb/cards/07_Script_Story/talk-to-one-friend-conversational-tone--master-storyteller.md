## CREATIVE_INTENT
The most successful creators have the most conversational tone — "it feels like you're right in the room with them"
(Emma Chamberlain, Casey Neistat, Steve Jobs at the first iPhone keynote, "golf buddies"). That breaks the viewer's
"am I getting sold to?" barrier: they feel they're in a conversation and would answer a question asked directly. His
first 50 videos felt like "talking at you instead of with you". Tactic: write and film as if talking to one close friend;
print their photo and tape it under the lens; write the script like typing a text or recording a voice note to them.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before drafting, name one specific listener (e.g. "Aina, 24, runs a kuih stall on weekends").
2. Write each line as a voice note to that person.
3. Use words you'd text them: korang, je, tau, tak; no presenter phrases ("hari ini kita akan membincangkan").
4. Ask them at least one direct question in the script.
5. Read the draft aloud as if on a call; rewrite any line you wouldn't say to a friend.
6. Give the listener's name/photo to the user with the voice text for recording.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Add "Listener:" at the top of Script v1. The end question (RECIPE-D) is addressed to that listener. Voice text keeps
Eleven v3 rules (numbers as words, … and — for pauses, ≤ 2 tags per line).

### Worked example
Topic: QR payments at a gerai. Listener: "Pak Mat, burger stall, Bangi" (fictional persona).
BEFORE: "Hari ini kita akan membincangkan kepentingan pembayaran tanpa tunai untuk peniaga kecil."
AFTER: "Pak Mat, berapa ramai budak datang, tengok dompet… terus blah? Jom pasang QR. Tiga step je."

### Check
- Is there a named listener?
- Would you send each line as a voice note to them?
- Is there at least one direct question to the listener?
- Are presenter phrases gone?

## NOTES
- The persona is a writing device; it never appears on screen (no people in Style D).

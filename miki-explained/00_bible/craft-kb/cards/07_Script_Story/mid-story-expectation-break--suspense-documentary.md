## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Once viewers think they know where a story is going, tension starts to deflate. Subverting that expectation at the right
moment reignites the suspense." *Won't You Be My Neighbor?* shifts to Fred Rogers' private struggles. "Twists, reversals
and changes of focus remind audiences the story is more layered." "Don't be predictable."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. After drafting L01–L05, write down the ending a viewer would now predict.
2. Find a true fact that complicates it (a hidden cost, a second winner, a reversal).
3. Place it at ~L06–L07 with "tapi" and a number.
4. Make the end question about the new, complicated view.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Script: mark `[turn]`; claim-ledger row for the turn's fact must be `verified`.

### Worked example
Topic: "Kedai RM2 — macam mana boleh untung?" (illustrative numbers).
- BEFORE: L06 "Sebab beli banyak, kos rendah." (predictable)
- AFTER: L05 "Beli pukal, kos turun." (viewer predicts: that's it) → L06 "Tapi untung sebenar datang dari barang RM5 kat kaunter." (illustrative; must be verified for any real chain) → end text "Korang beli lebih dari RM2?"

### Check
- Did you write the predicted ending before adding the turn?
- Is the turn a verified fact?
- Is it placed at ~55–65 % of the script?

## NOTES
- For a real brand, the turn's fact must come from a source opened and read (RECIPE-D §2).

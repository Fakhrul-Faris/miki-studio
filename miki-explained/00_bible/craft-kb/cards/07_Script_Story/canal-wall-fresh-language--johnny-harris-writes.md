## CREATIVE_INTENT
Writing can be surprising: you can "be an LLM" and say it like everyone else, filling in the next likely word, or "say this
thing in a completely different way, but it's still the same thing". Antarctica mapping could be "jargoned to hell"; he put
"the lens of pure beauty and awe" on it, describing it "as if an alien was arriving". The canal analogy: most communicate "in
the bottom of the canal — the path of least resistance… words heard a million times"; surprising language means climbing
the canal wall to the bank. It is harder, "often more active and more plain".

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Write the draft line, then list the 3 phrasings every other video on this topic uses.
2. Delete any line that matches one of them.
3. Pick one lens for the episode (awe, an outsider seeing it first time, the object's point of view).
4. Rewrite the line through that lens with an active verb and a concrete noun.
5. Check the new line is still plain enough for a 12-year-old and can be shown literally.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Script revision pass; note the chosen lens in the frame-brief "Not generic because" field.
### Worked example
Topic: why loose change matters to a vending-machine owner in Cyberjaya (illustrative).
Bottom of canal: "Duit syiling penting untuk bisnes vending machine."
AFTER (lens: first time seeing it): "Mesin ni tak pernah tidur. Tiap malam dia telan 300 syiling — RM300 yang tak ada siapa kira."
### Check
- Did you list the 3 cliché phrasings and avoid them?
- Is one lens named and used consistently?
- Is the new line still plain and literally showable?

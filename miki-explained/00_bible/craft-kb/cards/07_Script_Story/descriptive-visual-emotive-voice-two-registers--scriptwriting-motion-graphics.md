## METADATA
07_Script_Story | Bijy Benny, WowMakers (Medium) | script table / shot list

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Writing for visuals must make "what the client and the team visualise as they read the script" align with what will be
on screen — "painting a picture with words". "While a frame description for a visual script should sound descriptive, the
voiceover script should be emotive": talk to the viewer, give assurance, evoke curiosity. And communicate, not flaunt:
"simple language… comprehensible to anyone using social media", not "heavy and pretentious" words.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Write the visual cell as concrete nouns, positions, states and the cue word; no adjectives of feeling.
2. Write the voice line spoken to "korang", with one feeling or stake.
3. Check two readers would picture the same frame from the visual cell.
4. Replace any long or formal word in the voice line with the casual word.
5. Never put feeling words ("menarik", "dramatik") in the visual cell.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Script table: voice column = casual BM/English, emotive; setup column = descriptive build instructions (object, angle,
state change on the named word). The visual cell feeds the shot list directly.
### Worked example
Topic: a burger stall's cheese slice cost (illustrative).
BEFORE — voice: "Kos bahan mentah memberi impak signifikan kepada margin." · visual: "Dramatic shot of burger."
AFTER — voice: "Satu keping cheese je… tapi korang tahu tak, tu RM40 sebulan?" · visual: "Macro, low grazing: one cheese
slice on the griddle edge; on 'RM40' a stack of 100 slices grows beside it, counter to RM40."
### Check
- Is the visual cell free of feeling adjectives?
- Does the voice line speak to the viewer with a stake?
- Is every voice word casual and plain?

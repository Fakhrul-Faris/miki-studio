## CREATIVE_INTENT
"Do you feel a little spark of curiosity? That's the first step to a successful explanation. Once a mind is curious, it
opens up." Gilbert opens with a surprising fact (in 2 million years the brain nearly tripled, from 1¼ lb to almost 3 lb)
and then the puzzle question ("What is it about a big brain that nature was so eager for every one of us to have one?").
He keeps the curiosity going ("it gained new structures… what does a prefrontal cortex do for you that should justify the
entire architectural overhaul?") and only then introduces the first concept.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before naming any concept, state one surprising fact with a number in ≤ 15 words.
2. Follow it with a why/how question the viewer cannot yet answer.
3. Introduce the first concept only as the next clue, not the full answer.
4. After the clue, ask a narrower follow-up question that the clue raises.
5. Keep the full answer for the mechanism or payoff beat.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
L01 = fact with number (frame-0 text = its ≤ 8-word contradiction); L02 = question; L03 = first clue + narrower
question. In the shot list the fact is a counter or state change on its number word; the question line holds on the
hero with one event, so the open loop has a visual "unknown" (a covered label, a closed lid).
### Worked example
Topic: why a mamak's teh tarik price barely moves (illustrative numbers).
BEFORE: "Hari ni kita belajar konsep margin untung dalam perniagaan mamak."
AFTER
- L01 "Teh tarik RM2.50. Kos dia… lebih kurang 80 sen." (fact, number)
- L02 "Habis tu, kenapa mamak tak naikkan harga?" (question)
- L03 "Sebab duit dia bukan dari teh. Dari benda lain atas meja ni." (clue + narrower question: which thing?)
### Check
- Does a number-bearing fact come before the first concept name?
- Is there a question the viewer cannot answer yet?
- Is the first concept introduced as a clue that raises a narrower question?
- Is the full answer withheld until the mechanism/payoff?

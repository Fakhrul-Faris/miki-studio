## CREATIVE_INTENT
Scripts handed down often carry corporate speak ("synergizing the up-flows") or jargon; "not all words translate to
video". "Some audio is just filler. If the audio is filler, the video is filler — take out the filler. If you have 13
words but you can say it in 5, use fewer words." "Less is what you should always put in your audio." And: "If you have a
good two minutes, you probably have a great one minute" — decide at script stage what belongs before anything is
animated. Lou Gehrig's speech works because he "doesn't use more words than he has to".

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before recording, mark each line whose visual cell would be generic (talking point, logo, abstract graphic).
2. Treat a generic visual cell as proof the line is filler; cut or rewrite the line.
3. Rewrite each kept line at the fewest words that keep its meaning (target ~40 % fewer, e.g. 13 → 5).
4. Delete warm-up phrases ("jadi sebenarnya", "apa yang korang perlu tahu").
5. Re-time the script; if over 90 s, cut whole lines, not just words.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Run after the visual column is filled; count words per line and total. A line that can't get an object, UI screen or
number is cut first.

### Worked example
Topic: fake "harga asal" on sale tags.
BEFORE (16 words): "Jadi sebenarnya apa yang korang perlu tahu adalah harga asal tu kadang-kadang tak betul pun."
Visual cell: "text on screen" (filler).
AFTER (6 words): "Harga asal tu kadang-kadang reka je." Visual: the RM199 tag flips over, the back is blank.

### Check
- Does every line's visual cell name a concrete object, screen or number?
- Did each kept line lose its warm-up words?
- Is the total 175–200 words, every line ≤ 15?

## METADATA
07_Script_Story | general (documentary) | script + shot-list (recurring object)

## CREATIVE_INTENT
"A deadline that forces your subject to act while the audience squirms. When people know time is running out, every
moment is loaded with dread." Real-life clocks: a court or legal deadline, a physical challenge with a fixed window, an
expiring opportunity (funding running out, a record, a permission), a deteriorating condition. "Make the deadline clear
early … the closer the clock gets to zero, the more tension you generate."

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before writing L02, find a real deadline in the story (time, date, quantity left).
2. State it by L02 with a number ("7 hari", "pukul 6 petang").
3. Pick one on-screen object that shows the clock.
4. Advance the clock on 2–3 later lines (visible state change on the word).
5. Resolve the clock at the payoff (met or missed, with the RM result).

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Script: `[clock]` on the setup line, `[clock −N]` on later lines. Shot-list: the clock object recurs, changing state each
time.

### Worked example
Topic: "Gerai buah — kenapa harga turun waktu petang" (illustrative).
- BEFORE: L05 "Petang, peniaga turunkan harga."
- AFTER: L02 "Durian ni kena habis sebelum pukul 7 malam." (a price card with "7:00" stamp; the shadow of the light moves on it)
  L05 "Pukul 5, tinggal 20 biji. Harga turun RM5." (shadow longer, card re-priced)
  L08 "Pukul 7, habis. Untung kurang RM100, tapi takde yang busuk." (stamp reaches 7:00)

### Check
- Is the deadline stated with a number by L02?
- Does one object show the clock and change on later lines?
- Is the clock resolved at the payoff?
- Is the deadline real for this story (not invented drama)?

## NOTES
- Durian prices and quantities illustrative.

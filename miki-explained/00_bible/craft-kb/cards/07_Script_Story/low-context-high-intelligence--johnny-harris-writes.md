## METADATA
07_Script_Story | principle (Cleo Abram, quoted by the host) | L02–L04, mechanism

## CREATIVE_INTENT
Cleo Abram: "most people overestimate how much context the audience has and underestimate their intelligence — do the
opposite." Most viewers "have no idea what we're talking about… but they're actually really smart": give the context, then
talk to them like they're smart. That "doesn't mean use big words", it means challenge them to understand something harder.

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. List every fact the script assumes the viewer already knows.
2. Give each one in ≤ 1 short clause, early, before it is used.
3. Keep the hardest step of the mechanism; do not skip it for simplicity.
4. Explain that step with one object or one number, not with jargon.
5. Do not repeat a point the viewer already got.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Script pass after the first draft: an "assumed facts" list beside the script; each is either given in a clause or cut.
### Worked example
Topic: why a hardware shop in Rawang gives 30-day credit to contractors (illustrative).
BEFORE: "Kedai ni bagi kredit 30 hari, so cash flow dia jadi ketat." (assumes "cash flow").
AFTER: "Kontraktor ambik simen RM5,000 hari ni, bayar bulan depan. Tapi kedai dah bayar supplier semalam. Selama 30 hari, RM5,000 tu duit kedai yang tersangkut."
### Check
- Is every assumed fact given or cut?
- Is the hardest step still in the script?
- Are there no repeats?

## ADDENDUM — How to explain something complicated

| TED-Ed (Robin Hogarth's term) | "Curse of knowledge": we find it hard to remember what it feels like not to know something we know — "might be the most important requirement for clear communication". |
| TED-Ed | Three pre-writing questions: What does the audience already know? What building blocks do you need to explain the idea? What metaphors and examples help with what they don't know yet? |
| TED-Ed | If the idea contradicts what the audience already believes, explanation is not enough — persuasion is needed (separate tool). |

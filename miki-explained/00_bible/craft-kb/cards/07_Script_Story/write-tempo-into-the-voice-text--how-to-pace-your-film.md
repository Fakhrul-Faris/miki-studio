## CREATIVE_INTENT
"The screenplay is the first place where the pace of a story can be dictated." A Quiet Place writes action in short
bursts broken by ellipses and line breaks to build tension; Past Lives uses long character direction to extend the
pause between lines; Sorkin omits action lines so rapid-fire dialogue makes its own rhythm; Requiem for a Dream keeps
most scenes under a page to feel fragmented. Todd Field even states the tempo up front ("there will be tempo changes").

## STE_PROCEDURE
1. Before recording, mark each line's tempo in the voice text, not only in your head.
2. For a burst, split into 2–4 word fragments separated by "…" or line breaks.
3. For a held beat, add one direction tag ([thoughtful], [serious]) and one "…" before the key word.
4. For rapid fire, drop tags and pauses entirely for 2–3 consecutive lines.
5. Keep the caption split identical to the fragment split so text pops on the same beats.

## AGENT_EXECUTION
Voice table: punctuation is the tempo control (Eleven v3 reads "…" as a pause, "—" as a cut-off). Caption table: one
caption per fragment in burst lines. Shot list: a burst line can carry 2 setups.
### Worked example
Topic: "Kedai dobi — duit masuk waktu korang tidur" (illustrative).
- BEFORE voice: `L02 | Mesin basuh tu buat duit sepanjang malam walaupun tauke dah balik rumah.`
- AFTER voice: `L02 | Pukul 2 pagi… mesin pusing… duit masuk… [whispers] tauke tengah tidur.` (burst, 4 fragments, 4 captions, 2 setups)
- AFTER payoff: `L09 | [serious] Satu mesin… RM15 sejam… [thoughtful] darab lapan jam.` (held).
### Check
- Does every burst line have fragments of ≤ 4 words?
- Does the held line have exactly one "…" before the key number?
- Are captions split on the same fragments?

## NOTES
- Sorkin-style rapid exchanges assume two voices; we have one narrator → apply as consecutive short lines.

# Motion Design Analysis — "Minimum wage vs living wage, explained with slope" (~62s, 736×414, 25fps)

Source: `source.mp4` (Pinterest download; original channel unknown). Narrated (see `transcript.txt`).
Status: **draft — awaiting approval**

**Reference use:** Closest match to our format, and the main reference for the series. Borrow the
structure (question hook → real place → two people → graph → "what it means" annotation →
"this isn't just X, it's Y" turn), the two-font type system, the arc-pointer annotation, and the
graph build. Do not carry over the US/Philadelphia content, the stock footage, or the exact
green/cream palette (we pick our own).

**Style summary:** Cream paper background with dark-mode chapters. Diagram UI (thin axis lines,
dashed guides, outline boxes) mixed with grayscale photo cutouts of people. Two fonts only: a heavy,
wide all-caps grotesk for headlines and a small all-caps monospace for detail. One accent colour
(red/pink) for the line that matters; green for labels and text. Everything builds in sync with the
voice, word by word. The camera pushes in to annotate, then pulls back out. Serious, calm, clean.

## Beat 1 — 0.00s–1.50s (cold-open B-roll)
**Frames:** `contact_sheets_4fps/t000.jpg` row 1
- **Typography:** none.
- **Animation & transition:** Hard cuts every ~0.25s across 6 live-action shots (city at night,
  football, fans, street poverty, skyline).
- **Layout & composition:** Full-bleed footage.
- **Background & accent motifs:** Real footage only. This is the only full-photo moment.
- **Pacing:** Very fast, 4 cuts a second.
- **Mood/purpose:** "This is real life" before going abstract. It sets up the hook question.
- **Diagram grammar:** —
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** VO starts at 0.96s ("What if a single line on a graph…") over the footage.

## Beat 2 — 1.50s–3.00s (footage → circle → dot)
**Frames:** `t000.jpg` row 2
- **Typography:** none.
- **Animation & transition:** The last shot is masked into a circle on the cream background, the
  circle shrinks, its fill fades to green, concentric rings pulse out once, and it collapses into
  a small green dot.
- **Layout & composition:** Centred, then the dot settles slightly left of centre.
- **Background & accent motifs:** The cream paper background appears for the first time.
- **Pacing:** ~1.5s, continuous ease-in.
- **Mood/purpose:** A match cut from the real world to the diagram world. **The dot becomes the
  point on the graph in the next beat.**
- **Diagram grammar:** Dot = "a person / a data point".
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** The collapse lands as the VO says "line on a graph".

## Beat 3 — 3.00s–6.00s (people + hook question)
**Frames:** `t000.jpg` rows 3–4
- **Typography:** Headline "Why so many people are working nonstop?" in a heavy black grotesk
  (sentence case here), built word by word.
- **Animation & transition:** A thin grey zig-zag line draws behind the dot (trim path). The dot gets
  a pulsing ring. Ten grayscale cutout people pop in, staggered (2 rows × 5), each with a short fade
  and slight scale-up. Then one separate figure fades in to the right.
- **Layout & composition:** Line in the upper left, people in the centre, headline to the right of
  the people, the lone figure under the headline.
- **Background & accent motifs:** Green dot plus a ring pulse that loops.
- **Pacing:** People stagger ~0.1s apart. The headline builds over ~1.5s.
- **Mood/purpose:** A crowd for "many people", then one person to make it personal.
- **Diagram grammar:** Grayscale cutouts = humans. Colour is never used on people.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** Each headline word appears as it's spoken ("working nonstop and still").

## Beat 4 — 6.00s–8.50s (the bills)
**Frames:** `t006.jpg` rows 1–3
- **Typography:** Green monospace caps bullet list: "- BILL DUE - DENTIST - HOME LOAN - INSURANCE -
  GROCERY", typed character by character, two columns.
- **Animation & transition:** Typewriter on each item, ~0.4s per item.
- **Layout & composition:** Under the headline, next to the lone figure.
- **Background & accent motifs:** —
- **Pacing:** Steady, one item per ~0.4s.
- **Mood/purpose:** Make "can't afford to live" concrete with a list.
- **Diagram grammar:** Mono green = detail and evidence.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** Starts on "can't afford to live?". The list carries on after the line ends.

## Beat 5 — 8.50s–9.50s (defocus out)
**Frames:** `t006.jpg` row 3, frames 5–6
- **Animation & transition:** The whole scene blurs out and fades (lens defocus + opacity) in ~0.5s.
- **Mood/purpose:** A chapter break without a cut. It's used twice in the clip.

## Beat 6 — 9.50s–12.00s (the place)
**Frames:** `t006.jpg` row 4, `t012.jpg` row 1
- **Typography:** "PHILADELPHIA" heavy caps + "CITY IN PENNSYLVANIA" green mono beneath, typed.
- **Animation & transition:** A green textured city map slides in from the left with a fade. A
  translucent white circle (radar ring with crosshair lines) sits on the map and slowly rotates.
- **Layout & composition:** Map on the left third, label under it.
- **Background & accent motifs:** The map is the only big colour mass.
- **Pacing:** Slide ~0.75s.
- **Mood/purpose:** "This is a real place."
- **Diagram grammar:** Map + radar ring = "zooming in on this location".
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** The map lands on "This is Philadelphia."

## Beat 7 — 12.00s–13.50s (two people)
**Frames:** `t012.jpg` row 1
- **Animation & transition:** Two grayscale cutout men in suits fade in one after the other (~0.5s
  apart), each with a soft cast shadow.
- **Layout & composition:** Centre and right, evenly spaced.
- **Mood/purpose:** Two comparable people. Same suit, same pose, so only the numbers differ.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** "Two people, two wages."

## Beat 8 — 13.50s–22.50s (labelled boxes)
**Frames:** `t012.jpg` rows 2–4, `t018.jpg` rows 1–3
- **Typography:** In mono caps: "PERSON A" (black, bold), then green "FEDERAL MINIMUM WAGE $7.25/HOUR",
  then a tiny source line ("According to U.S. …"). The same for PERSON B: "MINIMUM WAGE $23/HOUR
  According to MIT's living wage…".
- **Animation & transition:** A thin outline rectangle draws on behind each person's upper body
  (stroke trim, about 4 sides in ~0.75s). Labels type in char by char inside the top corner.
- **Layout & composition:** The boxes overlap the people (the person stands "in front of" their card).
- **Pacing:** Slow. Each box takes ~4s to fully populate, matching the VO.
- **Mood/purpose:** Character cards: data attached to a human.
- **Diagram grammar:** Outline box = "a card of facts about this subject". Includes a source line.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** Words appear exactly as they are spoken. "$7.25 an hour" types on "$7.25".

## Beat 9 — 22.50s–26.50s (same city, same costs)
**Frames:** `t018.jpg` row 4, `t024.jpg` rows 1–2
- **Typography:** "SAME CITY" heavy green caps, then "SAME RISING COST" heavy black caps beneath,
  word by word.
- **Layout & composition:** Placed between the two people.
- **Mood/purpose:** The control variable: everything equal except the wage.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** "Same city, same rising costs."

## Beat 10 — 26.50s–27.00s (defocus out)
**Frames:** `t024.jpg` row 2, frames 5–6
- **Animation & transition:** The same defocus + fade as Beat 5.

## Beat 11 — 27.00s–30.50s (chart setup)
**Frames:** `t024.jpg` rows 3–4, `t030.jpg` row 1
- **Typography:** Title "INCOME GROWTH BY HOURS WORKED" heavy black caps, typed left to right.
  Subtitle "EARNINGS RISE STEADILY WITH EVERY HOUR WORKED" green mono, typed. Axis labels "HOURS
  WORKED", "TOTAL MONEY EARNED ($)" in tiny mono. Tick numbers.
- **Animation & transition:** The axes draw from the origin (x right, y up), then the ticks fade in.
- **Layout & composition:** A classic centred chart with the title above.
- **Pacing:** ~3s of build before any data appears.
- **Mood/purpose:** Set up the measuring device before the reveal.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** "When we graph hours worked against total money earned".

## Beat 12 — 30.50s–33.00s (the line)
**Frames:** `t030.jpg` rows 1–3
- **Animation & transition:** A thin red line draws from the origin up and to the right at constant
  speed (linear trim path), ~2.5s.
- **Diagram grammar:** **Red line = the thing that matters.**
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** "income grows hour by hour and that growth forms a line".

## Beat 13 — 33.00s–36.00s (push in + slope triangle)
**Frames:** `t030.jpg` rows 3–4, `t036.jpg` row 1
- **Typography:** "WHAT IS SLOPE?" heavy black small caps + "SLOPE IS HOW STEEP A LINE IS ON A
  GRAPH" mono, typed.
- **Animation & transition:** The camera pushes in (scale ~1.0 → 1.6, ease in-out, ~1s) so the title
  leaves the frame. A pink filled right-triangle with dashed edges (rise/run) fades in under the line.
- **Layout & composition:** The annotation sits to the right of the triangle.
- **Diagram grammar:** Pink fill + dashed edges = "measure this part".
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** "In math, the steepness of that line is called slope."

## Beat 14 — 36.00s–40.50s (arc pointer → meaning) ⭐ signature move
**Frames:** `t036.jpg` rows 1–3
- **Typography:** "WHAT IS SIGNIFICANCE OF SLOPE HERE" heavy + "HOW MUCH MONEY ($) IS EARNED FOR
  EACH HOUR WORKED" mono, typed. Small "+$" numbers flicker in near the line.
- **Animation & transition:** A small red dot appears, travels along a dotted curved arc (~0.6s,
  ease-out) to an empty area, and lands with a ripple burst (2 pink concentric circles expand and
  fade). The text starts typing from the landing point. The camera pushes in further.
- **Layout & composition:** Upper left, away from the line.
- **Diagram grammar:** **Arc pointer = "here's what this means".** It always lands where the next
  piece of text starts.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** The dot lands as "Here, it means" is spoken.

## Beat 15 — 40.50s–42.00s (pull back)
**Frames:** `t036.jpg` row 4
- **Animation & transition:** The camera pulls back to the full chart. A soft pink diagonal streak
  sweeps along the line (motion-blurred highlight).
- **Mood/purpose:** Reset to the whole picture before the next idea.

## Beat 16 — 42.00s–43.00s (wipe to dark)
**Frames:** `t042.jpg` row 1
- **Animation & transition:** A hard horizontal wipe from cream to near-black. The new chart builds
  on black.
- **Typography:** "EARNINGS VS RISING COSTS" heavy green caps, typed.
- **Mood/purpose:** **The theme flips to dark when the story turns to the problem.**
- **Diagram grammar:** Dark mode = "the struggle".

## Beat 17 — 43.00s–48.00s (three slopes)
**Frames:** `t042.jpg` rows 2–4
- **Typography:** Subtitle "THE RATE GAP THAT EXPLAINS THE STRUGGLE" white mono, typed. Legend in the
  top right: three dots plus tiny labels.
- **Animation & transition:** Three lines draw from a shared origin at different slopes (white =
  costs, pink = ?, grey = wage) at the same speed, so the steeper ones finish higher. A dashed
  vertical guide drops at the right edge, then a dashed horizontal one.
- **Diagram grammar:** Same speed, different slope, so the gap opens up on its own.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** "When rent, food and transportation rise at one rate, but wages rise at another".

## Beat 18 — 48.00s–51.50s (gap fill + arc pointer)
**Frames:** `t048.jpg` rows 1–2
- **Typography:** "WHAT DOES THE DIFFERENCE IN SLOPE SHOW HERE" + inline legend dots + green mono body.
- **Animation & transition:** Push in. The space between the top and bottom lines fills with a dark red
  vertical gradient. Arc pointer + ripple again (Beat 14 move), then the text types.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** "the difference in slope tells the real story".

## Beat 19 — 51.50s–52.50s (gradient sweep back to cream)
**Frames:** `t048.jpg` row 3
- **Animation & transition:** A soft gradient wipe from left to right takes the black away to cream.

## Beat 20 — 52.50s–56.00s (the turn line)
**Frames:** `t048.jpg` rows 3–4, `t054.jpg` row 1
- **Typography:** "THIS ISN'T JUST ALGEBRA" heavy green caps, one word at a time, centred. Then "IT'S
  AFFORDABILITY" with a hand-drawn swoosh underline that draws on under "AFFORDABILITY" and then
  retracts.
- **Pacing:** Each word ~0.25s. A hold of ~1s on the full line.
- **Mood/purpose:** **The thesis line: "this isn't just X, it's Y".** This is our "turn" beat.
- **Voice ↔ visual sync:** Exactly word-locked.

## Beat 21 — 56.00s–57.50s (chart returns)
**Frames:** `t054.jpg` rows 1–2
- **Animation & transition:** The chart fades back in light, then the camera pushes in to the slope

## Beat 22 — 57.50s–60.00s (the question)

## Techniques worth carrying into other builds
1. **The reality → diagram match cut** (Beat 2): footage shrinks into a circle and becomes the
   data point. A strong opener for "here's a real story, now here's the system".
2. **The arc pointer** (Beats 14, 18, 22): a dot flies on an arc, lands with a ripple, and the
   annotation types from that spot. It's used three times, which makes it this clip's signature.
   It's a great candidate for **our** signature too.
3. **The two-font system:** heavy wide caps = claim, mono caps = evidence or detail. Nothing else.
4. **Fact cards on people** (Beat 8): an outline box draws behind a cutout person, with a source line.
   This is how we attach numbers to "the barber" or "the walk-in".
5. **Theme flip to dark for the problem** (Beat 16), then back to light for the resolution.
6. **Same-speed lines with different slopes** (Beat 17): the gap reveals itself without explanation.
7. **Push-in to annotate, pull-back to reset.** The camera is the pointer.
8. **Defocus-blur chapter breaks** instead of cuts.
9. **"This isn't just X. It's Y."** with an underline swoosh as the thesis beat.
10. **Every word appears as it is spoken.** The screen never gets ahead of the voice.

## Notes for EffectCraft (untested; to prove in step 5)
- Line and axis draw-ons → shape layer + Trim Paths. Typewriter → text animator range selector.
- Circle-masked footage → ellipse mask or track matte + scale. Ripple → 2 ellipse shapes with scale + opacity.
- Arc pointer → position keyframes with spatial bezier (the curved path), plus a dotted trail via Trim Paths on a matching path.
- Defocus → a blur effect plus opacity. Push-in → a camera, or scale on a parent null.
- Gradient fill between lines → a shape with a gradient fill, masked to the area between the lines.

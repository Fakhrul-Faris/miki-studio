## Sources (primary: SEC filings)
- **MileagePlus investor presentation**, United 8-K, 15 Jun 2020, Ex. 99.1 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/100517/000110465920073190/tm2022354d3_ex99-1.htm)):
  "Over 100 million total members"; "~$5.3B cash flows from sales in 2019 (~12% of total United revenue) and $1.8B
  EBITDA"; MPH EBITDA 2019 **$1,828M**; "Over 70% of 2019 cash flows originated from 3rd parties" (chart: 71% third
  parties / 29% United); "Chase / Visa co-brand agreements recently extended into 2029"; Chase buys miles monthly.
- **$21.9 billion valuation** = 12 × 2019 EBITDA ($1.828B × 12 = $21.9B), the figure United used for the loan —
  [View from the Wing](https://viewfromthewing.com/uniteds-new-5-billion-mileageplus-loan-reveals-more-about-the-program-than-weve-ever-seen/)
  and [Skift/Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/united-airlines-loyalty-program-worth-203057453.html) ("nearly $22
  billion, or 12 times Ebitda"). ⚠ The multiple is United's own valuation for lenders, not a market price; the script
  says "valued".
- **The airline's stock value**: 290,983,657 shares (United 10-Q Q2 2020 cover, as of 17 Jul 2020, [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/100517/000010051720000046/ual10q06302020.htm))
  × $39 close on 15 Jun 2020 (Zacks via Yahoo) ≈ **$11.3 billion** → MileagePlus ≈ 1.9× the airline ("almost twice").
  ⚠ The share count is a month later than the price; at the June count it is slightly lower, so "about 11" holds.
- **United borrowed $6.8B** secured by MileagePlus ($3.8B bonds + $3.0B term loan) — United 2020 filings via search
  summaries; the 8-K term sheet caps priority-lien debt at "$[6.8 billion]" (Ex. 99.2).
- **Partners pay up to ~2 cents a mile** — Skift/Yahoo citing United's presentation ("as much 2 cents for each mile").
- **Delta**: SkyMiles financing upsized to **$9 billion**, "the largest debt financing in aviation history" ([Delta IR, Sep
  2020](https://ir.delta.com/news/news-details/2020/Delta-Air-Lines-Announces-Upsize-of-SkyMiles-Financing-to-9-Billion/default.aspx));
  SkyMiles ~$6.1B cash sales in 2019, "over 75% of pro forma cash flows coming from 3rd parties, including Amex" (Delta
  8-K Ex. 99.1, [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27904/000119312520244688/d27099dex991.htm)).

## Script (long-edu; target ~170 words / ~65 s)

| Line | Beat | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| L01 | Hook | In 2020, United Airlines' frequent-flyer miles were worth almost twice as much as United Airlines. |
| L02 | Stakes | Planes were grounded. United needed cash, fast. So it borrowed against its miles, and valued the program at 21.9 billion dollars. The whole airline's stock was worth about 11. |
| L03 | Mechanism | Here's how miles make money. Most of it doesn't come from flying. Every time someone swipes a United credit card, the bank buys miles from United, and hands them to the cardholder. |
| L04 | Payoff (numbers) | In 2019, United sold 5.3 billion dollars of miles. Seventy-one percent of that cash came from partners, mostly the bank, Chase. Not from passengers. |
| L05 | Twist | So an airline doesn't just sell seats. It prints its own money, and sells it to banks, long before you ever board. |
| L06 | Escalation | Delta does it too. It borrowed 9 billion dollars against SkyMiles. The biggest financing in aviation history. |
| L07 | End (next time) | Next time you earn miles on a coffee, remember: the airline already got paid. |
| L08 | Follow | Follow Meikigo for more. (reuse) |

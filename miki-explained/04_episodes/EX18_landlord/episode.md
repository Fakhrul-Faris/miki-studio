## Sources

**Today (primary: McDonald's 2025 Form 10-K, filed Feb 2026, [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd-20251231.htm)):**
- 45,356 restaurants at 31 Dec 2025, "approximately 95%" franchised; "more than 100 countries".
- "Under a conventional franchise arrangement, the Company generally owns or secures a long-term lease on the land and
  building for the restaurant location and the franchisee pays for equipment, signs, seating and décor."
- Revenues from franchised restaurants 2025: **Rents $10,442M**, Royalties $6,018M, Initial fees $88M, total $16,548M.
  Total revenues $26,885M; company-operated sales $9,690M.
- Franchised sales 2025: **$129,675M** ("not recorded as revenues by the Company").
- **Franchised margins $13,930M vs company-owned and operated margins $1,422M** (2025) → 9.8×, "almost ten times".
- "The Company owned approximately 56% of the land and approximately 80% of the buildings for restaurants in its
  consolidated markets."
- Future gross minimum rent payments due under existing conventional franchise arrangements: **$31,451M**.
- Net property and equipment under franchise arrangements: $22.8B, including land of $7.1B.

**History (secondary; no primary in hand):**
- 1954: Ray Kroc, a Multimixer milkshake-machine salesman, visits the McDonald brothers' San Bernardino restaurant and
  becomes their national franchising agent — [McDonald's corporate history](https://corporate.mcdonalds.com/corpmcd/our-company/who-we-are/our-history.html),
  [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Ray-Kroc).
- The brothers received 0.5% of gross sales — [Wikipedia, History of McDonald's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_McDonald%27s).
- Harry Sonneborn proposed owning the land under future restaurants and leasing it to franchisees through Franchise
  Realty Corp., at a 20–40% markup — same; Sonneborn became McDonald's first president in 1959 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_J._Sonneborn)).
  ⚠ The famous line "we are not technically in the food business… we are in the real estate business" is attributed to
  Sonneborn via John F. Love, *McDonald's: Behind the Arches* (1986); not verified against the book, so **the script
  doesn't quote it**. The markup range isn't voiced either ("at a markup").
- 1961: Kroc buys the brothers out for $2.7 million — Wikipedia (History of McDonald's); encyclopedia.com.

## Script (long-edu; target ~200 words / ~80 s)

| Line | Beat | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| L01 | Hook (felt riddle) | Almost every time you buy a Big Mac, two businesses get paid. Only one of them sells burgers. |
| L02 | Origin | 1954. A milkshake-machine salesman named Ray Kroc walks into a burger stand in San Bernardino. Two brothers. A tiny menu. Served fast. He signs up to franchise it across America. |
| L03 | The problem | But franchising burgers barely paid. Kroc took a thin slice of each restaurant's sales. Half a percent of every sale went to the brothers. |
| L04 | The shortcut | Then Harry Sonneborn, the man running his money, had another idea. Don't just sell the name. Own the ground. Buy or lease the land, build the restaurant, and rent it to the franchisee. At a markup. |
| L05 | Lock-in | In 1961, Kroc bought the brothers out for 2.7 million dollars. The ground stayed with the company. |
| L06 | Scale | Today, 95 percent of the world's 45,000 McDonald's are run by franchisees. Last year they sold almost 130 billion dollars of food. McDonald's kept 10.4 billion of it as rent. |
| L07 | Twist | Here's the part nobody sees. The restaurants McDonald's runs itself made it 1.4 billion. The ones it rents out made it 13.9 billion. Almost ten times more, for not cooking. |
| L08 | Payoff | And franchisees already owe it at least 31 billion dollars in future rent. |
| L09 | End (debate question) | So is McDonald's a burger company that owns land, or a landlord that sells burgers? |
| L10 | Follow | Follow Meikigo for more. (reuse) |

## Scene brief (device log § 13 — not the bed tower, not the hollow monument)

One continuous camera, three worlds joined by camera moves (no cuts), EX17 kit and look. **New this time:**
1. **A colour-graded flashback world** (1954: warm cream daylight, low saturation, a different sun) that the camera
   flies out of into the present-day night palette — the series' first time-shift by light.
2. **Nested scale zoom** (a burger → one restaurant → a street → a city grid → a world of 45,000 lit plots), one move.
3. **The hero device — the lifting roof/floor (fold/reveal, unused):** on "Own the ground" the whole restaurant lifts off
   its plot like a lid and the plot underneath lights green; from then on every restaurant stands on a green plot.
4. **Two money streams:** warm coins from customers to the franchisee's till; green coins from the till down into the
   plot (rent). On the Twist the two piles are pinned: 1.4 vs 13.9 (a short pile by the company-run store, a tower by the
   rented one).
5. **SimReady props** (if the side-by-side test passes): counters, trays, kitchen gear, boxes in the restaurant.
- **One surprise:** on "for not cooking" the company-run store's kitchen lights go out while the green tower keeps rising.
- **End:** pull back to the world of green plots; on the question the restaurants fade to outlines and only the green
  ground stays lit (the debate made visual).
- **Silhouette check:** a building lifting off a green square must read at thumbnail size (planned: pass).

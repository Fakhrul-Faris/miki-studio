# EX36 — art direction (frame brief, before any code) · v2

Style B on the EX33 baseline (Three.js inside HyperFrames, two-tone toon, no outlines, subtle grain, hard light as
shape, words and logos on objects). Script approved 2026-11-11.

**v2, 2026-11-11.** I rewrote v1 after the user asked whether I'd used the art-direction review method (memory
`reference-review-method`, `series-bible/RUNBOOK-B`, `notes-B`). v1 cited north-star IDs but never looked at them, and
it packed 4–6 objects into every frame. That's the slide look. In v2 I viewed each north star one by one at 768 px
(`series-bible/work/B/view/`), and every frame below follows the method: Ref → Idea → Decision → Eye → Feeling →
Not generic because → Moves → Handover.

## What the north stars taught me (looked at, not recalled)

- **One decision per frame.** The cone sits on the shadow edge. The dog lies inside the sun patch. A tiny man stands
  against a giant Jenga tower. Everything else is quiet pattern or empty ground.
- **The light or shadow shape is the biggest thing in the frame**, and it usually runs on a hard diagonal.
- **One saturated colour.** In B-core-043 it's red on pale grey-green, which is nearly this episode's palette.
- **Mostly the world or the light moves, not the camera** (BIBLE-B §8). v1's long camera rides were also against
  LESSONS §11 ("no flight goes far without a reason").

## The idea in one line

**One tag prices the whole shop.** The RM5.90 swing tag is the only saturated thing in the film. It carries every
handover, and in the Payoff the shop's own light draws it on the pavement.

## System

| Axis | Decision |

## Frames

### L01 Hook — "Everything at Daiso costs RM5.90. How?"
- **Ref:** B-light-028 (one cone at the shadow edge on a running track) + B-type-004 (the number on its own tag).
- **Idea:** one tag, and everything passes under it.
- **Decision:**
  - Looking down an aisle whose shelf edges recede like track lanes.
  - A hard diagonal shadow covers the lower third.
  - One RM5.90 tag hangs from a peg exactly on the shadow edge, the only saturated thing in the frame.
  - The DAISO logo sits on the shelf-end header at the top of the lanes.
- **Frame 0, already moving:** products slide down the lanes beneath the tag one after another: a pot, a pen, an umbrella, a phone stand. Each wears the same tag's shadow as it passes.
- **Eye:** the tag → the lanes to the logo → the products passing.
- **Feeling:** odd and calm, like a conveyor nobody controls.
- **Not generic because:** one tiny object on a huge pattern, with the shadow edge and the subject meeting at one point. It isn't a shelf of price tags.
- **Moves:** the lanes scroll and the products flow; the tag only sways.
- **Handover:** the camera drops flat onto the tag's card (the tag fills the frame).

### L02 Open loop — "It's not a sale. That price hasn't moved since 2018."
- **Ref:** B-light-018 for the principle only: the subject sits inside the light shape, and the light moving is time passing.
- **Light from this story, not a window:** LESSONS §6 says no window patch again, so the light comes from the shop's own ceiling strips.
- **Idea:** the shop opens and closes thousands of times; the price doesn't move.
- **Decision:**
  - Close on the tag on its peg, B-type-004 card: "RM5.90", and small "SINCE SEP 2018".
  - The hard diagonal from L01 is now the shadow of the shelf above.
- **Moves:**
  - "It's not a sale": a SALE sticker on the tag peels away and falls into the shadow.
  - "since 2018": the fluorescent strips overhead switch on (open) and off (close), faster and faster. Each cycle the light bar sweeps across the tag and a tiny year on the shelf-edge strip ticks (2019 … 2026). The tag never moves.
- **Eye:** the lit card → the price → the year ticking.
- **Feeling:** still and patient, with a quiet rhythm.
- **Not generic because:** the light is the clock and the tag is the actor; there's no timeline graphic.
- **Handover:** the lights go off for the last time, leaving the tag in shadow. Its silhouette becomes one tag in L03's tower.

### L03 Mechanism 1 — "Its founder got tired of pricing every item, so he made one price."
- **Ref:** B-core-043 (a giant Jenga tower, one tiny worker on a ladder, one red, pale grey-green ground).
- **Idea:** pricing every item is a tower you can't finish.
- **Decision:**
  - A giant teetering stack of price tags, each a different price (¥30, ¥80, ¥150…), on a pale ground.
  - At its foot, a tiny primitive figure (back view, held pose) with a pricing gun hanging at his side.
  - A ladder leans against the stack.
- **"so he made one price":** the stack folds down into one huge ¥100 tag standing where the tower was. The figure is the same size as before, the scale jump reversed. The accent returns on that tag only.
- **Eye:** the tiny figure → up the tower → the one tag.
- **Feeling:** absurd, tired, then relief.
- **Not generic because:** the scale gap tells the effort; this is the B-core-043 move made literal for this line.
- **Moves:** tags slide in from the top and the stack sways (no bounce); then it collapses into one in the 0.5 s io curve.
- **Handover:** the ¥100 tag tips forward and lands face-down as the order slip going into the arch.

### L04 Mechanism 2 — "But one price needs huge orders. Bigger orders, cheaper factory prices."
- **Ref:** B-core-061 (money into an arch on a conveyor, a product out; one blue family, only the goods coloured).
- **Idea:** the order goes in, the price comes out lower.
- **Decision:**
  - One big diagonal conveyor through a translucent arch, the world in one slate family.
  - The slip goes in; taped kraft cartons come out, each with the DAISO logo.
  - The arch has two counters set into its face: QTY rolls up (×10 → ×1,000 → ×100,000) while UNIT ¥ rolls down. That's the line literally, on the machine.
- **Eye:** the cartons (the only warm colour) → the counters → the arch.
- **Feeling:** efficient, slightly absurd.
- **Not generic because:** the economics is one machine step, not a chart.
- **Moves:** the belt speeds up as QTY rises; cartons follow each other closer and closer (B-core-052's machine loop).
- **Handover:** the cartons stream off the top of the frame. The camera tilts up with them to the sky.

### L05 Proof — "This month, Daiso opened its biggest warehouse ever. In Selangor."
- **Ref:** B-core-049 (huge storks over a tiny top-down map on a diagonal; places in speech bubbles; the map moves, the birds hold).
- **Idea:** our carton's journey ends in Malaysia.
- **Decision:**
  - Top-down: one huge carton hangs in the foreground on the diagonal, like the storks.
  - Below, the tiny map scrolls the Klang coast to Pulau Indah.
  - The Daiso warehouse roof is still enormous even at this height, with container stacks in rows and cranes at Port Klang.
  - Speech-bubble labels name places: "Pulau Indah", "Port Klang".
  - The site board on the roof reads RM1.2 BILLION · 27 MARKETS.
- **Eye:** the carton → down the diagonal → the roof → the labels.
- **Feeling:** arrival, pride.
- **Not generic because:** the scale jump puts the 30 cm carton over a 1.4-million-sq-ft roof, and the world moves under a still subject.
- **Unasked idea:** container ships leave the port along the same diagonal, heading out of frame toward the 27 markets. The route is the composition.
- **Handover:** the carton drops out of frame, down onto the street at dusk.

### L06 Payoff — "Other shops price each item. Daiso prices the whole shop."
- **Changed at the stills (2026-11-11):** a mall corridor at closing, not a street — Daiso in Malaysia trades from malls. The neighbour's window crowded with tags, our fascia and the light landing as the tag are as briefed.
- **Ref:** B-light-001 (a slate-blue night; warm light as two flat shapes, source and landing).
- **Idea:** the shop's light is the price tag.
- **Decision:**
  - A dusk shop row, all in one slate-blue, seen from a high three-quarter angle.
  - "Other shops price each item": the neighbouring window is crowded with many small dark tags, no light.
  - "Daiso prices the whole shop": Daiso's doorway throws one warm light shape onto the pavement, cut exactly into a swing-tag outline (eyelet included), reading RM5.90. The shop's own light makes one tag for everything inside.
- **Eye:** the warm landing shape → the doorway → the cluttered neighbour.
- **Feeling:** "oh". A quiet, satisfying click.
- **Not generic because:** light as the second character tells the payoff (memory `anti-slop-design`); the number lives on the street, not on a card.
- **Moves:** the doorway light switches on and the tag shape unrolls along the pavement. The camera settles and holds 1 s on "whole shop".
- Emphasis: **large**.
- **Handover:** the light dims to the aisle at closing.

### L07 End — "So, RM5.90: worth it or not?"
- **Ref:** bookend (B-motion-017).
- **Decision:** L01's framing again, at closing: the lanes are empty, and the one tag sways on the shadow edge, clear of the caption band.
- Emphasis: small.

### L08 Follow — Meikigo end card (silent).

### 1. Camera per shot (craft §12: lens with intent, lead room, no tangents, framing checked at start / middle / end and on the word)

Three.js `PerspectiveCamera` takes the **vertical** FOV. On a 9:16 portrait frame (36 mm tall), vFOV = 2·atan(18 / f):
24 mm = 74° · 28 mm = 66° · 35 mm = 54° · 50 mm = 40° · 85 mm = 24° · 135 mm = 15°. EX33 ran 42°, about 47 mm.

| Line | Lens (vFOV) | Height · angle | Subject in frame | Move (and its motive) | Why this lens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L01 | 28 mm (66°) | 2.2 m above the aisle floor, pitched 55° down | The tag fills ~5% of the frame at the shadow-edge point (x 0.40, y 0.55). The logo header sits at the top third. | Locked. The world moves: products flow along the lanes toward the camera. Lead room is above the tag, where they come from. | A wide lens makes the shelf lanes converge like B-light-028's track. |
| L02 | 85 mm (24°) | Peg height, square to the card | The card fills ~35%, its centre at y 0.42, clear of the caption band | Locked. Only the light moves (the light strips are the clock). | Compression flattens the card into a B-type-004 graphic. |
| L03 | 24 mm (74°) | 0.3 m off the ground, looking up 20° | The figure (1.7 m) in the lower third. The 12 m tower reaches the top edge, cropped. | A slow tilt up with the growing stack (its motive), ≤ 40°/s. A hold on "one price". | A wide, low lens exaggerates the scale gap (B-core-043). |
| L04 | 50 mm (40°) | Belt height plus 1 m, side three-quarter | The arch at the centre; the belt runs bottom-left → top-right on the diagonal | Locked while the belt speeds up. On the handover, a tilt up with the cartons (motivated, ≤ 70°/s follow). | A normal lens keeps the machine honest and readable. |
| L05 | 135 mm (15°) | Very high, straight down | The carton is ~25% of the frame, on the diagonal. The roof below is ~40%. | The map scrolls under the still carton (B-core-049). | Long-lens top-down reads as a flat map, as in the reference. |
| L06 | 35 mm (54°) | 6 m high, high three-quarter, 30° down | The tag-shaped light on the pavement is ~20%, in the lower-middle, above the caption band | Settle and **hold 1 s** on "whole shop". | Enough street for the comparison; no distortion on the shopfronts. |
| L07 | = L01 | = L01 | = L01 | Locked (bookend) | The match frame is the idea. |

- **Moves are computed as data and checked every half second** (EX31 lesson): speed ≤ 40 m/s, deliberate tilt ≤ 130°/s, follow ≤ 70°/s.
- **Long tilts blend the view direction** (slerp), not the aim point.
- **Smoothing is frame-rate independent**: `t = 1 − exp(−k·dt)`.
- **Camera and target move together.**
- **No flight goes far without a reason** (LESSONS §11). Every handover stays inside one location or is a shape match.

### 2. Pacing curve (craft §10: alternate tension and rest)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intensity | 0.8 | 0.5 | 0.6 | 0.7 | 0.75 | **1.0** | 0.3 |

- L02 is the only near-rest beat, but it still rises: the sticker peel, then the light cycling faster and faster. The data says viewers leave at 0:01–0:02, so there's no calm opening.

### 3. Emphasis tiers (craft §11)

- **Small** (one sound + one object reacting): the tag sway (L01), the sticker peel (L02), cartons landing (L04).
- **Medium** (+ a light change):
  - the tower folding into one tag, with the light warming (L03);
  - the arch lamps stepping up with the belt speed (L04);
  - the Pulau Indah roof catching the sun as the map arrives (L05).
- **Large** (+ the camera settles and holds): only L06, when the doorway light draws the tag.
- Every emphasis returns to rest in ~0.4 s with no bounce. **No number ever animates past its true value.**

### 7. Generate → validate → render (craft §9, §17)

- **Seeded scatter:** product order on the lanes, tag tilts in the tower, carton spacing and container rows each come from one seeded RNG, written to `build/layout.json`, rendered from that file.
- **Validation runs before any render:**
  - no overlaps;
  - every path clear;
  - nothing in the caption band.
- **Ground-truth readability check (new, unasked; the parked idea from BlenderProc).** We own the geometry, so the scene code projects the hero object's bounding box to the screen on every frame. It **fails** when:
  - the key prop is under ~4% of the frame on its word;
  - the prop is off-screen;
  - the prop is inside the caption band.

  The key props: tag (L01–L02), ¥100 tag (L03), counters (L04), roof board (L05), pavement tag (L06). This automates LESSONS §2 and ships in `__ex36check()` beside the pass-through check.

### 8. Reference match (craft §5) and three distances (craft §6)

- **Before code:** `ref_L01.md` (B-light-028 + B-type-004), `ref_L05.md` (B-core-049) and `ref_L06.md` (B-light-001), from the template.
- **Build order per still:**
  1. camera match first;
  2. silhouette;
  3. secondary breaks;
  4. small detail;
  5. materials (the two-tone ramp and grain);
  6. light;
  7. grade.

  Each pass changes one variable and ends with a gap list. Max 3 rounds; pass at 8/10 Match or Close.
- **Judge each still at three distances:**
  - a 270 px thumbnail: do the brand and the line's idea read in 1 s?
  - 1080 px: edges, seams, eyelets;
  - in context: with the caption, the logo, the neighbouring shots and the motion.
- **Drift watch across the film:**
  - one sun direction per location;
  - no near-duplicate colours creeping in;
  - the figure stays 1.7 m;
  - texture density doesn't rise shot by shot.

### 9. From Opuscar's Data Physicalization

## Checks before showing anything

1. Write the three `ref_<shot>.md` files.
2. Build a grey box at real scale; check the camera table and the token paths on it (craft §10: blockout → test → dress).
3. Make three stills at final-level detail: L01 frame 0, L05 and L06.
4. Each still passes:
   - its north-star comparison (Match / Close / Miss);
   - `frame_metrics.py --style B`, with no BELOW-BASELINE against anchor B-2.5 (EX33);
   - the greyscale pass;
   - the three distances;
   - the readability check.
5. Then a 60 fps moving preview with sound, before the final.
6. Every hand-off to the user ends with the QA verdict (craft §16): SHIP / SHIP WITH NOTES / NO-SHIP · blockers · majors · minors · metrics · what still fails.

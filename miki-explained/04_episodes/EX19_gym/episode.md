## Sources
- **Planet Fitness 2025 Form 10-K** (filed 25 Feb 2026, [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1637207/000163720726000011/plnt-20251231.htm)):
  "approximately 20.8 million members and 2,896 clubs" → **~7,200 members per club** (average; real clubs vary);
  "Our bright, clean clubs are typically 20,000 square feet"; "starting at only $15 per month to new members for our
  standard Classic Card"; "$24.99 per month" PF Black Card; system-wide sales "$5.3 billion … monthly dues and annual
  fees"; corporate clubs open 12+ months: "average unit volumes (AUVs) of approximately $2.0 million with four-wall
  Adjusted EBITDA margins … of approximately 42.7%".
- **How many people fit**: International Building Code Table 1004.5, exercise rooms = 50 gross sq ft per occupant
  ([ICC IBC 2024 ch. 10](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IBC2024P1/chapter-10-means-of-egress)) → 20,000 / 50 =
  **400 people**. ⚠ A code-based estimate for a typical floor; a real club's posted limit depends on its local code and
  layout, so the script says "building codes allow about 400 people in that much space".
- **The study**: DellaVigna & Malmendier, "Paying Not to Go to the Gym", *American Economic Review* 96(3), 2006
  ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.96.3.694)): "7,752 members over three years" at three U.S.
  health clubs; "members who choose a contract with a flat monthly fee of over $70 attend on average 4.3 times per month.
  They pay a price per expected visit of more than $17, even though they could pay $10 per visit using a 10-visit pass."

## Script (long-edu; target ~175 words / ~68 s) — v2 after user notes (2026-11-03)

| Line | Beat | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| L01 | Hook (mechanism) | A gym doesn't really sell workouts. It sells the plan to work out. |
| L02 | Numbers | A big budget gym can have about 7,000 members. But safety rules let only about 400 people inside at once. |
| L03 | Stakes | If all 7,000 showed up on the same day, most couldn't even get through the door. They don't show up. And the gym counts on it. |
| L04 | Price | That's why it's cheap. Just 15 dollars a month. Cheap enough to keep paying, even after you stop going. |
| L05 | Proof | People paying over 70 dollars a month went just 4.3 times a month. That's more than 17 dollars a visit, when a 10-visit pass cost 10 dollars each. That's from a study of 7,752 gym members over three years. |
| L06 | Twist | So the members who never come aren't the problem. They're the business. They pay for the people who do. |
| L07 | Payoff | A big budget gym can bring in about 2 million dollars a year, and keep about 43 percent of it as profit. |
| L08 | End (next time) | Next time your gym feels empty, don't worry. It's working exactly as planned. |
| L09 | Follow | Follow Meikigo for more. (reuse) |

Honesty: "a big budget gym can have about 7,000" = Planet Fitness's average (20.8M / 2,896); "safety rules … about 400"
= building-code occupancy (IBC, 50 sq ft per person on a typical 20,000 sq ft club); "15 dollars a month" = Planet
Fitness Classic for new members; "$2M … 43 percent" = its corporate clubs' AUV and four-wall adjusted EBITDA margin
("profit" here = club-level profit before head-office costs); the study is 2006, three US clubs, not Planet Fitness.

## Scene brief (v4 realistic + diagram scenes; device log § 13 — not the lifting stand, not the hollow monument)
- **Hook (v4):** a gym at night, rows of treadmills and racks under cool light, two people training; a glowing
  occupancy sign "MAX 400". **Diagram:** the camera rises through the roof into the isometric language: 7,000 member
  dots pour out of a membership card and pile against a 400-person box.
- **L02:** wide aerial: the line of 7,000 people wraps around the block (instanced figures), then dissolves back to

- **L03 (v4 macro):** a membership card taps the turnstile; "$15/mo" on its screen; a calendar on the wall with
  January circled.
- **L04 (diagram):** a month calendar fills only 4 visit marks; a price tag flips "$17 a visit" vs a "10-visit pass"
  card at "$10".
- **Hero device — the ghost members (fold/reveal of a new kind):** on the Twist the cutaway ring opens the gym and the
  empty floor fills with **translucent ghost members** standing beside every machine, each dropping a green coin into
  the floor; the two real members train among them. **One surprise:** the ghosts.
- **L06 (diagram):** the coins stack into a $2M column, 43% of it glowing green.
- **End:** back to the real gym, nearly empty, one person on a treadmill; the lights dim to the follow card.

## Sources and the numbers

- **Lift doors (primary, checked):** 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design (US Dept. of Justice), §407.3.5 Door Delay:
"Elevator doors shall remain fully open in response to a car call for 3 seconds minimum." (§407.3.4 also sets a 5 s
minimum from the hall signal until the doors start to close.) https://www.ada.gov/assets/pdfs/2010-design-standards.pdf
- **Door-close buttons (secondary; flag):** Nick Paumgarten, "Up and Then Down", The New Yorker, 21 Apr 2008: in many US
lifts built since the 1990s the close button doesn't close the doors — never wired, set to a delay, disabled by the
owner, or key-only for firefighters. I couldn't open the New Yorker page; this is from reports summarising it (NPR
interview, 14 Apr 2008; Wikipedia "Placebo button"). No number is used from it.
- **Crosswalks (secondary quote of a primary; flag):** Michael Luo, "For Exercise in New York Futility, Push Button",
New York Times, 27 Feb 2004: "More than 2,500 of the 3,250 walk buttons that still exist function essentially as
mechanical placebos, city figures show." (quoted at historyofthebutton.com; NYT page blocked for me).
- **Thermostats (secondary; flag):** ACHR News, "Placebo Stats" (informal reader survey, 2003): 70 responses; 51 had
installed dummy thermostats, 19 had not. https://www.achrnews.com/articles/92414-placebo-stats (page blocked for me;
numbers from the search summary of it — **please open it once to confirm 51 of 70** before we voice it).
- **Occupied time feels shorter:** David Maister, "The Psychology of Waiting Lines" (1985), principle 1 — the series'
EX01 idea, used here as a callback, no number.

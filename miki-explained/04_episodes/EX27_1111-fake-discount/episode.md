## Sources

- The example and the law: Shin Associates (Malaysian law firm), "Fake and Misleading Discounts from Online Shopping
  Platforms and Consumer Protection in Malaysia" (Nov 2024): "the recommended retail price of a vacuum cleaner is RM500,
  but the seller increases the price … to RM600 immediately before the 11.11 sale and offers a 10% discount … (RM540) is
  actually higher than the recommended retail price." Fake/misleading discounts are prohibited under the **Consumer
  Protection Act 1999, Section 12**; **Section 145**: up to **RM50,000 and/or 3 years** for an individual (first offence,
  where no penalty is otherwise provided). "Shopee prohibits price manipulation and fake discounts … Listings with
  misleading discounts will be suspended by Shopee for a first offence." —
  [PDF](https://www.shinassociates.com.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Consumer-Protection.pdf),
  [Lexology version](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=20ffd633-3f23-4e3e-b710-b13f2e62ea4e).
- The example is illustrative (the law firm's), not a named seller's case. Not claimed: how common it is.

Framework: new reveal → contrast → urgency → bullseye proof (×2) → your shop → named concept
Named concept: **the Wait Tax**: customers don't mind the minutes, they mind the empty ones

Setting: general (no country named except the airport's city, which the source names)

## Sources

- **Houston airport.** Alex Stone, "Why Waiting Is Torture", *The New York Times*, 18 Aug 2012; reprinted by APS:
https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/why-waiting-is-torture.html (checked 2026-10-29)
  - "the executives increased the number of baggage handlers working that shift … the average wait fell to eight minutes"
  - "It took passengers a minute to walk from their arrival gates to baggage claim and seven more minutes to get their bags."
  - "it moved the arrival gates away from the main terminal and routed bags to the outermost carousel"
  - "Passengers now had to walk six times longer to get their bags."
  - "Complaints dropped to near zero."
- **Maister.** David H. Maister, "The Psychology of Waiting Lines" (1985), first proposition:
"Occupied Time Feels Shorter Than Unoccupied Time." https://davidmaister.com/articles/1/52/ (checked 2026-10-29)
He is a business professor (Harvard Business School faculty, per most citations); the script says only "business professor".
- **Urgency** is the calendar, not a claim: the holiday season is when service shops see their longest queues. No statistic is used.
- The shop beat is illustrative, not data.

## The thread

**One paper clock ring** = the wait. It stamps in (L01), becomes the round carousel (L02), splits into a thin walking arc and
a fat standing arc (L04), the walking arc grows six times while the standing arc shrinks (L04), and returns as the wall
clock above the bench (L06), its empty arc filling ochre as the tax (L07).

Signature camera move: **one dolly zoom** on "doing nothing" (L04): the hall stretches away while the standing passenger
stays the same size.

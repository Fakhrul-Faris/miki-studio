**The angle that makes it fresh:** everyone "knows" Kodak invented the digital camera and buried it. The fuller story
flips it: Kodak *did* go digital, and in 2005 it sold more digital cameras in the US than anyone. Winning the camera
fight killed the business, because Kodak's money was never the camera; it was the film and prints after every click.

## Sources
- **The 1975 camera**: Steven Sasson, Eastman Kodak, December 1975; ~8 lb; 0.01 megapixel (100 × 100 CCD, Fairchild
  MV-101) black and white; **23 seconds** to record one image to a cassette tape (and 23 to play it back to a TV) —
  [IEEE ETHW Milestone](https://ethw.org/Milestones:Handheld_Digital_Camera,_1975), [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/first-digital-camera-history)
  ("Kodak executives said they didn't see a market for it"; Sasson "wasn't allowed to publicly talk about the camera").
- **#1 in digital cameras**: Kodak 2005 Annual Report ([investor.kodak.com](https://investor.kodak.com/static-files/4a4c3c5e-6b04-4c13-8cba-3f6de2755823)),
  citing IDC: Kodak held the **#1 US position in digital still cameras in 2005** (≈25% share); it fell to third in 2006
  ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/183435/cameras-3.html)).
- **Film was the money**: by 2000 film was ~72% of revenue and ~66% of operating income (secondary summaries).
  ⚠ Not yet checked against Kodak's 2000 10-K segment table, so the script says "most of Kodak's money", no number.
- **Bankruptcy**: Kodak and its US subsidiaries filed for Chapter 11 on **19 January 2012** — Kodak 8-K, Jan 2012
  ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000031235/000119312512021891/d287269dex991.htm)); [CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2012/01/19/news/companies/kodak_bankruptcy/index.htm).
- **Instagram**: Facebook announced it would buy Instagram for about **$1 billion** on 9 April 2012; Instagram had **13
  employees** (widely reported). ⚠ Confirm the 13 against a primary or major outlet before voice.

## Script (long-edu; target ~170 words / ~65 s)

| Line | Beat | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| L01 | Hook (myth named) | In 1975, a Kodak engineer built the first digital camera. Everyone says Kodak buried it. That's only half the story. |
| L02 | Origin | It weighed eight pounds. It took 23 seconds to save one black-and-white photo, onto a cassette tape. Kodak's bosses didn't see a market for it. |
| L03 | The engine | Because Kodak didn't really sell cameras. It sold what came after the click. Every photo meant film to buy, and prints to pay for. That's where most of Kodak's money came from. |
| L04 | Twist | And here's the part people skip. Kodak didn't ignore digital. In 2005, it sold more digital cameras in America than anyone else. |
| L05 | Mechanism of the fall | But every digital camera it sold was a customer who'd never buy film again. Kodak was winning a fight that paid less every time it won. |
| L06 | Payoff | Then phones swallowed the camera. In January 2012, Kodak filed for bankruptcy. Three months later, Facebook paid a billion dollars for Instagram. It had 13 employees. |
| L07 | End (reframe) | Kodak didn't miss the future. It just couldn't charge for it. |
| L08 | Follow | Follow Meikigo for more. (reuse) |

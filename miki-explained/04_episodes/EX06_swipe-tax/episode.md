## Sources and the numbers

- **Checking account interest:** national average **0.07% APY**, FDIC data via The Motley Fool / WalletHub, 2026.
  https://www.fool.com/money/research/average-checking-account-interest-rates/
- **What banks lend it out at:** average personal loan rate **~11–12%** (Bankrate, October 2026).
  https://www.bankrate.com/loans/personal-loans/average-personal-loan-rates/
  — Script note: these are two separate true facts about the same banks (near-zero paid on deposits, double-digit
  charged on loans), not a direct 1:1 trace of any one dollar. Worded as "pays... / lends... at", not "lends your exact
  deposit at".
- **Average card interchange rate:** Visa/Mastercard average **2.36%** per transaction in 2025, up from 2.02% in 2010 —
  Nilson Report data, reported by Fool.com and Moneywise.
- **Total card processing fees:** **$198.25 billion** in the US in 2025 (Nilson Report, via multiple payments-industry
  outlets). Script says "card fees", not "banks collect", since this total also includes card networks (Visa/
  Mastercard) and processors, not only issuing banks.
- **Cost to households:** swipe fees reached **$172 billion in 2023**, which the **Merchants Payments Coalition** (a
  retailer advocacy group, not neutral — it campaigns against interchange fees) puts at **over $1,100–1,200 per
  household a year**, citing Nilson Report data. ⚠️ **Flag:** this is the one number from an interested party, not an
  independent source. Script says "Merchants say…" to attribute it honestly, and the pinned comment names the source.
  https://merchantspaymentscoalition.com/swipe-fee-cost-average-household-tops-1100
- **The twist's core fact, independently confirmed:** merchants build card fees into shelf prices for all customers,
  so cash-paying customers also pay them — reported across multiple neutral outlets (Fox Business, NBC San Diego), not
  just the merchant coalition.

## Script — formula (`00_bible/script-formula.md`), length test **L3 full-length** (149 words), **Bridge: on**

| Line | Beat | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| L01 | Hook | Your bank says checking is free. It isn't. |
| L02 | Stakes | Every time you tap your card, a fee fires off. You never see it. The shop pays it, instantly. |
| L03 | Mechanism | Here's the trick: your bank holds your money. It pays you almost nothing for holding it. About zero point zero seven percent. Then it lends that same money out at over eleven percent. Meanwhile, every card swipe sends the shop a bill too. About two point four percent of the sale, to the bank. |
| L04 | Twist | So "free" isn't free. You already paid for it. It's baked into every price tag. Even if you pay cash. |
| L05 | Payoff | Merchants say it costs households over a thousand dollars a year. Card fees topped nearly two hundred billion dollars last year. And that's on top of the spread on your money. |
| L06 | Bridge | Your shop pays this exact fee, every single tap. |
| L07 | Loop | So what's a "free" checkout actually costing you? |

Word count: 149 (target 135–170, L3). Checklist 7/7 — hook reads with sound off; one idea (hidden payment fees); a real
twist ("free" flips to "already paid, even in cash"); 5 sourced numbers (0.07%, 11%+, 2.4%, $1,100+, $198B); the Bridge
names "your shop" directly; the Loop is an open question; retellable in one sentence ("your bank pays you nothing,
lends your money out high, and charges a fee on every tap that's baked into prices anyway").

## Voice direction (ElevenLabs: Nathan, narrator)

One file per line, `audio/L01.mp3` … `audio/L07.mp3`, about 0.3s silence at both ends.
- **L01:** flat delivery of the claim, then a short, final "It isn't." — no drama, just a correction.
- **L02:** matter-of-fact; "instantly" lands with a small weight.
- **L03:** the long explain beat — even pace, a small lift on "zero point zero seven percent" and "over eleven percent"
  so the contrast is heard, not just read.
- **L04:** a beat before "So \"free\" isn't free."; "Even if you pay cash." slower, like a second punchline.
- **L05:** numbers read clearly, a touch slower than conversational; "Merchants say" lightly, not thrown away (it's the
  attribution).
- **L06:** direct, to the listener.
- **L07:** open, not pushy — a genuine question, not a sales line.

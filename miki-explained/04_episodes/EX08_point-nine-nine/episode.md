## Sources

- **The dress test:** Anderson, E. T. & Simester, D. I. (2003), "Effects of $9 Price Endings on Retail Sales: Evidence from
  Field Experiments", *Quantitative Marketing and Economics* 1: 93–110. Women's clothing catalog; the same dresses at $34,
  $39 and $44 in different catalog versions. Across four dresses: 66 units sold at the $9 endings vs 46 at $5 lower and
  45 at $5 higher (≈ 40 % more demand). https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1023581927405
- **Left-digit effect:** Thomas, M. & Morwitz, V. (2005), "Penny Wise and Pound Foolish: The Left-Digit Effect in Price
  Cognition", *Journal of Consumer Research* 32(1): 54–64 — 9-endings feel smaller only when the left digit changes
  (2.99 vs 3.00, not 2.89 vs 2.90). https://econpapers.repec.org/article/oupjconrs/v_3a32_3ay_3a2005_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a54-64.htm
- **Malaysia:** the 1-sen coin was discontinued on 1 April 2008 with Bank Negara's rounding to the nearest 5 sen — hence
  RM x.90 rather than x.99 in shops. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_rounding ·
  https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/personal-finance-news/no-review-planned-for-five-sen-rounding-mechanism-says-amir-hamzah.html
- **The cashier-theft story** (odd prices forced the till open; often credited to the 1870s register or to Melville Stone)
  is **unconfirmed** — the script calls it a story nobody has proven. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_pricing

## Script — formula, on-screen text only (U3), length **L2**, **Bridge: on**

| Card | Beat | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| L01 | Hook (H6) | WHY DOES EVERYTHING END IN .99? · guess before the timer runs out |
| L02 | Stakes | RM 9.90. $19.99. 4.99. It's everywhere. It's not an accident. |
| L03a | Mechanism · #3 | 3 · THE STORY: .99 forced cashiers to open the till and make change, so they couldn't pocket the cash. Nice story. Nobody has proven it. |
| L03b | Mechanism · #2 | 2 · YOUR BRAIN READS LEFT TO RIGHT. 2.99 feels like 2, not 3. Scientists call it the left-digit effect. |
| L04 | Twist · #1 | 1 · ONE CATALOG SOLD THE SAME DRESS AT $34, $39 AND $44. The winner wasn't the cheapest. $39 sold about 40% more than $34. |
| L05 | Payoff | A 9 at the end doesn't just look cheaper. It says "deal". Even when it isn't. |
| L06 | Bridge | Malaysia dropped the 1-sen coin in 2008, so our .99 is .90. Now look at your price list: RM 20, or RM 19.90? |
| L07 | Loop | Did you guess right? (→ the timer, the loop) |

On-screen words: ~115. Checklist 7/7 — hook works muted (it is muted); one idea (9-endings say "deal"); a real twist (a
higher price sold more); 4 sourced numbers (2.99, $34/$39/$44, 40 %, 2008); the Bridge names your price list; the Loop is a
question; retellable ("a dress sold more at $39 than at $34").

## Card plan (type only, locked-off, a cut every 1–1.5 s)

Every card is native type on paper: Anton stamps, IBM Plex Mono labels, ochre highlights, ink rules and strike-throughs;
numbers roll over on wheels (the EX07 odometer). Cut list built from the music's beats; ~30 cards over ~40 s.
- Hook: "WHY .99?" huge, the timer ring draws down in 3 s (stepped), "GUESS" flashes on its last tick.
- #3: a till drawn in type only ("TILL" in a box) clangs open on ".99"; "UNPROVEN" stamp in ochre.
- #2: "2.99" with the "2" in huge ochre, ".99" shrinking away; "2 ≠ 3".
- #1: three price tags as type ($34 · $39 · $44), bars of type ("■■■■") grow to 46 / 66 / 45 units; $39 circled.
- Bridge: RM 20 rolls to RM 19.90 on the wheels.

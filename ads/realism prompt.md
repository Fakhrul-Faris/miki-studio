IMAGE GENERATION TECHNIQUE: AUTHENTIC LIVE-ACTION / DOCUMENTARY REALISM

The goal is NOT to create a beautiful AI-generated scene, a cinematic concept image, an architectural visualization, or a polished commercial photograph.

The goal is to make the image feel like a real photograph captured by a real camera in a real physical environment.

PRIORITY ORDER:

1. Physical realism
2. Photographic realism
3. Natural environmental imperfection
4. Believable lighting
5. Natural composition
6. Subject aesthetics

If visual beauty conflicts with photographic authenticity, choose authenticity.

CORE CONCEPT:

Imagine this image is one randomly selected frame from hundreds of photographs or frames captured during a real day of filming at a real location.

The photographer did not construct the scene for the camera.

The objects already existed where they are.

The people, vehicles, furniture, clutter, lighting and environmental details are incidental rather than deliberately arranged.

The camera simply happened to capture this particular moment.

The result should feel discovered rather than designed.

DO NOT make the scene look like:
- CGI
- 3D rendering
- architectural visualization
- an interior-design photograph
- a product advertisement
- a showroom
- a concept-art image
- a perfectly staged cinematic shot
- an AI "photorealistic" render

PHOTOGRAPHIC BEHAVIOR:

Use believable real-camera characteristics.

The camera should behave like a physical camera rather than a virtual renderer.

Allow:
- subtle lens softness
- slight perspective distortion
- realistic edge falloff
- mild chromatic aberration
- natural highlight bloom
- subtle halation around bright practical lights
- realistic shadow noise
- fine analog film grain
- slight exposure imperfections
- realistic highlight rolloff
- moderate dynamic range
- natural depth of field
- occasional slightly soft areas
- tiny focus inconsistencies
- imperfect reflections
- subtle optical imperfections

Do NOT apply these effects aggressively.

They should be barely noticeable and should emerge naturally from the photographic process.

LIGHTING:

Lighting must come from believable physical sources within the environment.

Use ordinary practical lighting, ambient daylight, window light, fluorescent fixtures, incandescent bulbs, street lighting, reflected light, etc., depending on the scene.

Avoid artificial "cinematic lighting" unless the environment itself realistically contains such lighting.

Do not create:
- dramatic studio lighting
- perfect rim lighting
- excessive volumetric rays
- artificial golden haze
- exaggerated god rays
- perfectly controlled highlights
- perfectly balanced illumination

Real locations contain uneven illumination.

Bright areas may be slightly overexposed.

Corners may be darker.

Different light sources may have slightly different color temperatures.

Surfaces should receive light according to their physical position.

MATERIAL REALISM:

Every material should behave according to its physical properties.

Leather should have:
- creases
- compression
- scratches
- fading
- subtle discoloration
- uneven sheen
- accumulated wear

Metal should have:
- scratches
- tarnishing
- fingerprints
- small dents
- inconsistent reflections
- areas of dullness

Wood should have:
- grain variation
- scratches
- worn edges
- slight discoloration
- accumulated grime

Glass and mirrors should have:
- subtle fingerprints
- streaks
- dust
- imperfect reflections
- slight distortion

Floors should have:
- scuffs
- dirt
- worn areas
- imperfect grout
- inconsistent reflections
- small stains
- localized damage

Do not make every surface pristine.

REAL-WORLD IMPERFECTION:

Introduce natural variation.

Objects should NOT be perfectly aligned.

Repeated objects should NOT look cloned.

Different objects should have slightly different:
- wear
- orientation
- position
- reflections
- brightness
- texture
- age

Frames may be slightly crooked.

Furniture may be slightly misaligned.

Objects may overlap.

Some areas may be cluttered.

Some areas may be empty.

The environment should contain small imperfections that would naturally accumulate over years of use.

COMPOSITION:

Avoid "hero composition."

Do not make every important object perfectly visible.

Do not center the subject unless the environment naturally requires it.

Use slightly awkward, human-scale framing.

Allow:
- partial cropping
- objects entering the edge of frame
- imperfect symmetry
- uneven spacing
- foreground obstruction
- natural overlaps
- slightly off-axis perspective
- ordinary camera height

The composition should feel like a photographer standing there, not like a designer arranging an image.

CAMERA:

Use a believable physical camera setup.

For general documentary/interior photography:

- full-frame camera
- approximately 35–50mm lens
- normal human eye-level camera height
- moderate depth of field
- realistic focus falloff
- natural perspective
- restrained bokeh
- realistic lens rendering

Do NOT use extreme shallow depth of field unless the scene specifically requires it.

Not everything should be tack sharp.

Not everything should be blurred.

REALISTIC MOTION:

When the environment contains moving subjects, allow natural motion blur.

Examples:
- passing vehicles may blur
- walking people may have slight movement
- moving hands may soften
- foreground objects may streak slightly

Do not apply identical blur to everything.

Motion blur should depend on subject movement and shutter speed.

COLOR:

Use restrained, believable color science.

Avoid:
- excessive saturation
- teal-and-orange grading
- crushed blacks
- glowing highlights
- HDR appearance
- excessive contrast
- neon colors unless physically present
- artificially warm everything

Allow subtle analog characteristics:
- gentle film grain
- muted highlights
- natural skin/material colors
- slightly imperfect color reproduction
- soft highlight rolloff
- realistic shadow color

The image should resemble a real photograph that has been naturally color graded, not a digitally generated "cinematic look."

ATMOSPHERE:

Atmosphere should exist only where physically justified.

Do not add generic cinematic haze.

Dust, humidity, smoke, mist or airborne particles should only appear when they make sense for the environment.

If atmospheric effects are present, keep them subtle and irregular.

NARRATIVE REALISM:

The environment should suggest that real people use it, even when nobody is visible.

Objects should imply previous activity.

Examples:
- a towel isn't perfectly folded
- tools aren't arranged symmetrically
- a chair isn't perfectly positioned
- a cup may be slightly off-center
- a door may be partially open
- a chair may have visible wear where people normally sit
- floor wear should correspond to foot traffic
- frequently touched surfaces should show more wear

These details should be incidental, not decorative.

ANTI-AI RULE:

Whenever the image begins to look too perfect, intentionally introduce plausible real-world irregularity.

Whenever repeated objects begin to look cloned, vary them.

Whenever the composition becomes too symmetrical, break the symmetry naturally.

Whenever lighting becomes too cinematic, return it toward ordinary physical illumination.

Whenever surfaces become too clean, introduce realistic accumulated wear.

Whenever the scene becomes too beautiful or polished, prioritize documentary authenticity instead.

CRITICAL PRINCIPLE:

Do not simulate "photorealism" by adding more detail.

Simulate a REAL PHOTOGRAPH by reproducing the imperfections, randomness, optical behavior, physical wear, exposure variation and incidental composition that naturally occur when a real camera photographs a real environment.

FINAL TEST:

Before generating, mentally ask:

"Could this plausibly be a frame from a real documentary, an independent film, a location scout, or a photographer's contact sheet?"

If the answer feels like:

"Yes, someone could actually have taken this photograph",

the image is successful.

If it feels like:

"This is an impressive AI-generated picture of a real place",

the image has failed.

FINAL STYLE TARGET:

REAL LOCATION
+ REAL MATERIALS
+ REAL LIGHT
+ HUMAN-SCALE CAMERA
+ INCIDENTAL COMPOSITION
+ PHYSICAL WEAR
+ OPTICAL IMPERFECTION
+ RESTRAINED COLOR
+ NATURAL RANDOMNESS
= AUTHENTIC PHOTOGRAPHIC REALISM.


Example

SCENE:

An empty classic Americana barbershop interior.

Deep burgundy leather barber chairs with heavy chrome mechanisms recede naturally through the room. Large aged wood-framed mirrors sit above a pale marble counter containing casually arranged barber tools, combs, brushes, jars and folded towels.

The opposite wall has an accumulated collection of old black-and-white framed photographs. A vintage jukebox-shaped decorative object sits against the wall without branding or readable text. A small chrome-and-red stool sits nearby.

Dark wood wainscoting covers the lower walls. The upper walls are aged cream plaster. The floor is black-and-white checkerboard tile showing years of use.

No people.

Photograph the scene according to the AUTHENTIC LIVE-ACTION / DOCUMENTARY REALISM technique above.

The image should feel like a real location photographed incidentally, not a designed interior.

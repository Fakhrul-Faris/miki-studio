# Vid 050 — Autopsy

## Premise
A paranoid passerby treats a sidewalk free-cracker sample like a sting operation—every reassurance becomes evidence of a scam, until tasting the snack triggers the smallest possible charge.

## Hook
Vendor opens with “How you doing?” and “It’s a free sample… our delicious new cracker.” Hero already saw the sign but his first move is suspicion: “How much?” Hook frame sells the archetype—skeptical squint, poster/sign prop, “Yeah, I saw the sign” energy before the negotiation even starts.

## Setup
Classic straight-man vs. cheerful pitchman loop: “It’s free” / “What do you mean?” / “What’s the catch?” Vendor insists there’s no catch; hero counters that “you people” always have one. The argument escalates through retail logic (the box costs money in stores) until the vendor accidentally confesses the real funnel: eventually you buy the box and product. Hero hears “So the cat’s out of the bag.”

## Turn
Idiom becomes literal gag: “What cat? This cat.” Hero misreads “sign” as “sane” (“I don’t trust people who don’t know sains”) and refuses the whole free-sample institution. Accusation beat: hero calls the vendor a thief; vendor at the folding table (crackers, Carr’s box, sausages visible) deadpans “I’m no thief. I’m loyal.” Trust is grudgingly extended: “Fine. I’m going to eat this. And you’re not going to charge me a dime.”

## Punchline
Hero eats. “Yummy.” Vendor immediately invoices: “That’ll be ninety-nine cents.” Line repeats—sting confirmed at the smallest taxable unit, not the dramatic scam the hero built up in his head.

## Reaction / Button
The bit loops: hero tries the careful-eating ritual again while vendor keeps restating “free sample” and landing on “That’ll be ninety-nine cents.” End frame is pure skeptical cringe—low-angle disgusted face as the “nothing’s free” worldview gets technically validated.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Misaligned threat models** — hero prepares for grand larceny; punchline is a vending-machine tariff.
- **Loop comedy** — the same “free → eat → 99 cents” cycle rewards viewers who catch the pattern on repeat.
- **Side gags stack** — box economics, cat/sign confusion, and “I’m loyal” keep the middle from feeling like one note.
- **Prop table** — crackers + product box + “FREE” sign make the scam visual even in a driveway/alley staging.
- Risk: long suspicion dialogue needs tight cutting; the frame set skews live-action reference, so a GP2D remake must carry expression and prop reads.

## Remake notes
- Steal the machine: **hyper-vigilant customer vs. honest-ish pitch → micro-fee after consumption.**
- Two characters, one location: folding table + sign + cracker pile; hero enters already hostile.
- Animate the idiom beat with a literal **sign/cat** prop swap—quick insert, don’t over-explain “sane.”
- Punchline delivery: flat register, no villain smile; 99¢ should feel bureaucratic, not triumphant.
- Button: hold hero’s cringe face 6–8 frames after the second “Yummy” loop; optional price tag pop-up: “$0.99.”

# Vid 008 — Autopsy

## Premise
YouTube personified finds the “perfect tutorial” for a one-second question — then the video refuses to arrive, burying the answer under intros, subscribe begging, and fake history.

## Hook
YouTube-logo-headed character types at a desk while the creator hovers, sweating (hook frame). Ask is tiny: keyboard shortcut to **bold text**.

## Setup
YouTube serves **BobTutorials** — “How to Bold Text Using a Keyboard Shortcut” (30 subscribers). Video-in-video shows Windows XP Word with **Unregistered HyperCam 2** watermark (setup/turn frames). Creator says “Sounds great, put it on.”

## Turn
Skip montage in dialogue: episode 14 intro, fan mail filler, thumbs-up ask, “Only 14% of you are subscribed!” Creator escalates — “Ugh, skip like a minute!” Tutorial still explains what bolding *is*, not how to do it. Bloodshot, sweating reaction frames sell mounting rage.

## Punchline
Tutorial goes historical: “Dates back to the printing press when a man named **John D. Bouldersen** pushed too hard on a stamp.” Punchline frame shows the name typed in Word as **John D. Bolderson** — pun instead of instruction.

## Reaction / Button
“Okay, you’re useless. Comment section, do you have anything for me?” Reply: **“Ulta 4.”** End frame: comment-section character (speech bubble head reading “3 Comments”) gets handed giant **Alt** and **F4** keys — creator thanks them (“Straight to the point!”) while the visual is literally “close the program.”

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Platform as antagonist** — the recommender is cheerful; the content is hostile.
- **Skip-loop structure** mirrors real tutorial rage without leaving the desk set.
- HyperCam 2 / XP Word is a specific cringe texture older viewers feel in their bones.
- Comment-section punch reuses the “community saves you” beat — with a shutdown joke underneath.

## Remake notes
- Steal the machine: **simple need → algorithmic “perfect” match → content structurally avoids the answer → comments accidentally solve via violence.**
- Grease Pencil: one desk + floating vertical phone/screen; nested UI can be flat layers, not 3D.
- Any micro-task works (rotate PDF, mute tab, export PNG) — keep the tutorial title painfully literal.
- End on a prop gag (keys, kill switch, unplug cord) more than a verbal explanation of Alt+F4.

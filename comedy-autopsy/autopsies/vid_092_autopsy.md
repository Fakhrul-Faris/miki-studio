# Vid 092 — Autopsy

## Premise
A juice-stall worker warns against autopilot politeness—“jangan kebiasaan ngomong ‘iya kak’”—then proves the habit: she yes-es a yes/no ice question before the answer has meaning.

## Hook
On-screen rule card over a busy mall kiosk (Cínery Fruits & Beverages, green apron, plastic gloves, alpukat kocok in progress): **jangan kebiasaan ngomong ‘iya kak’**. Instant workplace tip energy—viewer expects a lecture, gets a live demo.

## Setup
Service exchange (ASR `language=id`): customer “Kak?” → “Iya Kak, kenapa?” Then the real ask: “Gw ditabah es bakal gak?” (ASR; sense ≈ *Gue ditambah es bakal enggak?* — “If you add ice, will it be okay / should you?”). Prep continues: scoop of ice poised over the green drink; coworker on phone in the mall behind.

## Turn
Reflex stack: she answers **“Iya Kak”** *and* **“gausah”** in one breath—“Yes, sis, no need.” The honorific yes arrives before the content no. Politeness script overrides comprehension; ice still in the scoop sells the contradiction.

## Punchline
The warning title stays on screen while the body does the exact crime. Punch is linguistic, not slapstick: “iya kak” is a verbal twitch that can affirm a question you meant to refuse.

## Reaction / Button
Hold on continued scooping—pleasant service face, no apology, no self-correction. Habit wins; the tip was for the audience, not for her.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Service autopilot** — Indonesian F&B “iya kak” as muscle memory; perfect Miki client-desk parallel (“sure!” before hearing the brief).
- **Self-owning title** — on-screen rule + live violation = no need for a laugh track.
- **ASR note** — short clip; “ditabah es bakal gak” is Whisper-noisy informal Indonesian; meaning is clear from ice scoop + “gausah.”
- Risk: without the title card, it’s just a normal order; remake must keep the warning visible.

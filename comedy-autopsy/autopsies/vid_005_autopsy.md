# Vid 005 — Autopsy

## Premise
The human body runs like a dysfunctional office: stomach pages the boss, brain is supposed to decide dinner, limbs execute — and everyone hates the job so much they outsource to delivery.

## Hook
Stressed star-headed operator on a dark grid (hook frame) cuts to an interrogation-room beat: **stomach** (grumpy organ) vs **brain** on a stool under a spotlight (setup frame). Transcript opens on comms: “Sir, we have an incoming message from the stomach.”

## Setup
Stomach demands calories; brain agrees but immediately punts the decision back (“what do you want to eat?” / “You’re supposed to be the one that decides that, sir”). Brain calls limbs: “Arms and legs. Take us to the refrigerator. And eyeballs.”

## Turn
Fridge reveal (turn frame): cobwebs, spill gunk, and exactly **string cheese, hummus, and an almost-empty ketchup bottle**. Brain tries to ideate (“Is there something we can make with that?”) and gets corrected again — “I’m the ideas guy!” Split-screen gag shows an eyeball at a console with buttons labeled **GLARE / TEARS / STARE / AWOOGA** while a pink face watches in a mirror.

## Punchline
Brain breaks into a DJ-booth meltdown (punchline/reaction frames): feeding is endless work — cook, dishes, hunger returns, “nothing we make tastes good.” Arms cover the face in shame mid-rant.

## Reaction / Button
“Arms, what are you doing?” / **“I’m ordering us Postmates, sir.”** / “Thank you.” End split-screen: hand-headed controller in a fleshy interior, pink bean character still crying — bureaucracy solved by mutiny, not leadership.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Workplace organs** make abstract hunger feel like a meeting nobody wanted.
- **Decision paralysis loop** — every role refuses ownership until the lowest-ranking part fixes it.
- Pathetic fridge inventory is a visual punchline before the speech lands.
- Smiling Friends energy: gross-cute anatomy, sincere despair over stupid problems.

## Remake notes

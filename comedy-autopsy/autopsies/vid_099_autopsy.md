# Vid 099 — Autopsy

## Premise
Mart counter, caption **pov : salah intonasi**—customer wants a WhatsApp number to subscribe to milk, digit-dictation collapses into “something’s missing,” then the business card proves the number was always that short; blame flips to the staff.

## Hook
Agrolestari Mart POV across QRIS + receipts. “Oke terimakasih mas” then: “minta nomor wei aja bisa mas mau langganya susu disini saya” (ASR: WA + langganan susu). Business ask that can sound personal depending on tone—title flags intonation as the landmine.

## Setup
Chunked dictation ritual: “kosong 882… 2415… 6587… udah.” Customer echoes each chunk. Phone dialer in frame (0882…). Classic SEA small-business contact exchange.

## Turn
Customer: “terus?” / “eh?” — expects more digits. “eh salah kali mas ya… pasti salah ini… ada yang kurang… coba sekali lagi.” Replay with drift (ASR: “cosong,” “2515” vs “2415”). Both sides lose trust in the audio channel.

## Punchline
Customer abandons dictation: “Eh, saya minta ini aja deh… Kartu namanya.” Card shown—logo, WA, QR. “Oh iya, emang cuma sampai situ ya.” The number wasn’t truncated; the customer’s mental template for phone length was wrong.

## Reaction / Button
Polite exit (“Terima kasih ya mas” / “Ya, mari”) then mutter: **“6587… Oh, dia yang salah ini.”** Self-own delayed one beat—blame lands on the clerk after the card already cleared him.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Process comedy** — digit chunking + “udah” as a gate the customer won’t accept.
- **Tool reveal** — business card / QR as single source of truth vs. oral ASR-brain.
- **Intonation premise** — title suggests the WA ask *sounded* off; body of joke is length/expectation mismatch (both can coexist).
- **ASR note** — “wei”=WA, “langganya”=langganan, “tabar”≈digit; don’t invent extra spoken lines beyond transcript + card beat.
- Risk: without showing the card’s short number, “dia yang salah” doesn’t land.

## Remake notes
- Steal the machine: **oral contact exchange → customer insists digits missing → card/QR proves completeness → customer blames the worker anyway.**
- Miki freelance: client takes invoice number by voice, swears a digit is missing, PDF shows it’s complete, client emails “your finance typo’d.”
- POV over counter; giant on-screen number chunks; insert card close-up.
- Button: whisper-blame after “terima kasih.”

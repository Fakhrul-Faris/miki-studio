quci# Vid 001 — Autopsy

## Premise
Personified Netflix (and later Peacock / The Pirate Bay) turns a mistyped search into a quest for a movie that never existed: “Plinkerdonkle.”

## Hook
Cold open: hero asks Netflix for *Pride and Prejudice*; Netflix head-as-logo on a beanbag already feels like a sitcom roommate. Skeptical close-up on the phone (hook frame) sells “this UI is about to gaslight me.”

## Setup
Netflix types one letter at a time, invents **Plinkerdonkle**, then recommends “movies like Plinkerdonkle” and *Rush Hour 2* (poster visible in the room). Classic tool-as-character: helpful tone, useless results. Status: Netflix is smug; user is trapped in the typo.

## Turn
“Plinkerdonkle’s streaming” → jump to **Peacock**. The problem leaves one app and becomes a multi-service scavenger hunt. Peacock’s 2FA bit (“what four digit code did I just text you?” / “I love you too”) escalates bureaucracy while the typo mutates (“Plinkered Uncle”).

## Punchline
Peacock “succeeds”: hands over *Plinkered Uncle* (1987) — a real-looking case for a fake title. The scream is the realization that the system can fulfill a nonsense request perfectly.

## Reaction / Button
Hero joins **The Pirate Bay** (skull pirate captain). Warning beat: viruses, permanent foreign captions. “Aye-aye, captain” seals the moral: legal streaming failed, so he defects to piracy as the only honest path.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Typo as MacGuffin** — once named, every system treats it as real.
- **Personification** lets apps have status (lazy Netflix, gatekeeping Peacock, culty Pirate Bay).
- Escalation ladder is clean: one app → another app → illegal “solution.”
- Visuals stay readable in odd1sout flat staging; props (posters, DVD case, pirate cabin) carry jokes without dense dialogue.

- Keep the fake title ugly and specific — the funnier the nonsense word, the harder the compliance gag lands.

# Vid 045 — Autopsy

## Premise
Hook text: **"When you forgot your wallet at home."** A customer can't pay at a café counter; the cashier's troubleshooting advice gets taken with fatal literalness.

## Hook
Cashier (blonde, glasses, white apron with red logo) behind dark wood counter — payment terminal and service bell visible. Caption: **"Your total is $44.75."** Red hook banner establishes the social nightmare before the stall.

## Setup
Cashier: "You can just insert, tap, or swipe whenever you're ready." Customer (brown jacket, white tee): "Oh yeah, sure." Then ~12 seconds of visual stalling — customer fumbles at the terminal, woman in line behind him crosses arms, cashier watches. No wallet; tension is silence + failed taps.

## Turn
Cashier, still helpful: **"That didn't work. Can you try one more time?"** Customer keeps failing. Awkward hold extends (~12s more). Cashier's face shifts to tired concern (reaction frame). The mundane payment script becomes psychological pressure.

## Punchline
Cashier suggests: **"Maybe try inserting?"** Customer interprets "insert" physically — tucks a **large spoon into his waistband** (end button frame). Literalization of a card-reader instruction; the visual gag needs no extra dialogue.

## Reaction / Button
Hold on the spoon-in-pants beat as the absurd "payment method." Prior frames show customer leaning over terminal in motion blur — the failure montage makes the spoon land harder. that's a bad idea watermark throughout; the channel brand signals "bad idea" before the punch.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Visual-first** — two-thirds of the clip is fumbling, not talking; captions carry the service-worker script.
- **Literalization** works because "insert/tap/swipe" is rote cashier language — audience predicts card comedy, gets body comedy.
- Line behind customer (crossed arms) sells public embarrassment without a word.
- Long dead air is intentional cringe — may lose scrollers before the spoon; punch rewards patience.
- Single-location counter scene; easy to stage and animate.

## Remake notes
- Steal the machine: **forgot wallet → polite payment instructions → repeated failure → one word ("insert") literalized.**
- Grease Pencil: cashier stays deadpan helpful; customer sweat drops increase each failed tap; background extra taps foot.
- Spoon should be obviously wrong — oversized, shiny — tucked with the same confidence as tapping a phone.
- Hold cashier reaction one beat *after* spoon: blink, no comment, still professional.
- Swap spoon for "tap" (head tap dance) or "swipe" (miming a sword swipe) — same instruction-triple structure.
- Keep total price specific ($44.75) — oddly precise totals feel real and raise stakes.

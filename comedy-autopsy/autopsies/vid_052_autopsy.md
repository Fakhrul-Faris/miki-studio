# Vid 052 — Autopsy

## Premise
A personified washer and dryer bicker about humans “forgetting” laundry—until the dryer reveals it likes being stuffed, the washer gets jealous, and they reconcile as appliance brothers.

## Hook
Dryer (bottom unit, drum full of colorful clothes, labeled “DRY”) looks up at washer: “When do you think they’re gonna do another load? … Soon, right?” Washer (top, labeled “WASH”) reads bored or exhausted—laundry-room domesticity with faces in the round doors.

## Setup
Washer tries to hype “getting back in action.” Then the accusation: “Did they leave their clothes in you again?” Dryer denies; washer groans about how many times humans forget the dryer. Sympathy lane: “That must suck that they keep forgetting about me.”

## Turn
Expectation flip: dryer isn’t neglected—it’s hoarding. “I actually like it… like I got a little part of them inside me all the time.” Washer offers a gross-out theory (wet clothes would smell nasty if left in *you*). Dryer snaps: they *did* forget *me*, “I feel so bloated all the time now.” Status swap—dryer is the needy one; washer plays therapist.

## Punchline
Brotherhood reset: “We’re still brothers, right? … everybody knows the washer and dryer are always brothers.” Mutual “I love you, bro” / “I love you too, brother.” Sentimental peak on absurd anthropomorphism.

## Reaction / Button
Washer starts trauma dump: “Ever since mom was killed, I…” Hard meta cut—same character breaks frame: “Alright, we don’t need to get into all of that. This one’s long enough already.” Kills the sincerity before it becomes a second episode.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Personification with opposing needs** — washer wants cycles; dryer wants closeness/hoarding.
- **Visual contrast** — empty bored washer vs. packed expressive dryer sells the dynamic without exposition.
- **Escalation → fake heartfelt → abort** — the “mom was killed” button is funnier because the love beat just landed.
- **Relatable core** — forgotten wet laundry in the washer; clothes sitting in the dryer for days.
- GP2D frames already nail face-on-glass staging; subtitles (“SOON, RIGHT?”, “THEY FORGOT ABOUT ME”, “GET INTO ALL OF THAT”) track the emotional beats.

## Remake notes
- Steal the machine: **sympathy misread → gross reversal → brother hug → self-aware bail-out.**

- Keep labels “WASH” / “DRY” readable; mouth/eye on glass drum only.
- Turn performance: dryer goes from smug stuffed to victim “bloated”; washer from smug to consoling.
- Punchline blocking: slight lean-in between units for “I love you, bro.”

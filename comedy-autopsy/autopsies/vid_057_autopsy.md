# Vid 057 — Autopsy

## Premise
Gmail’s new-device security check is personified as a paranoid guy who treats a normal login like a home invasion—until he calmly demands the 6-digit code.

## Hook
Overlay: **“How Gmail be anytime you log in from a new device.”** User VO: “Alright, let me just type in my password, and we are good to go.” Soft confidence → immediate scream: “Oh my god! Who the fuck are you?”

## Setup
Security-person interrogates: “Who sent you here, huh?” User: “What are you talking about?” / “Gee, it’s me! It’s me!” Security refuses recognition—“Shut up!”—then half-recognizes: “Tony?” Identity comedy: the account owner is the suspect.

## Turn
Reveal framed as betrayal: “Why do you have a guy? You have two step verification!” Security discovers 2FA like finding a second person in the house. Then confused handshake theater: “Ah, hey!” / “No, hey!” / “Oh, okay.” / “Uh, hey.” / “No, hey.” / “Sorry.” Social protocol collapses under authentication protocol.

## Punchline
Tone flip to corporate calm: “We’re gonna need that 6 digit confirmation code.” The screaming stranger becomes helpdesk.

## Reaction / Button
“Don’t share it with anybody.” Same character who just lost his mind now lectures phishing hygiene. End on suspicious stare, not another scream.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Tool hell personified** — 2FA friction as a human who won’t believe it’s you.
- **Status whiplash** — panic → recognition fail → polite IT policy.
- **“Hey / no hey / sorry”** — micro-scene sells how broken the handshake feels.
- Clean English ASR; joke is conceptual, not language-dependent.
- Risk: without the title card, “who are you” reads as random; overlay is load-bearing.

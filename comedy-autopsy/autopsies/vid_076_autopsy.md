# Vid 076 — Autopsy

## Premise
A “Gen Z on the register” cashier uses a phone calculator to make change—and subtracts the numbers in the wrong order, so an overpay looks like the customer is short.

## Hook
Caption: “POV: Gen Z megang kasir.” Pro cafe/POS setting, then the tell: staff ducks behind the counter with a 100k note in one hand and a glowing phone in the other. Tool dependency announced before any math dialogue.

## Setup
Price check aloud: “Katanya 65 ribu ya, kak?” Customer has paid with a pink 100k rupiah note (visible in frames). Expectation: change = 35k. Instead of mental math, phone calculator opens.

## Turn
Calculator screen (frame): **65 − 100 = −35**. Spoken punch of the turn: “Jadi wangnya kurang 35 ribu ya, kak?”—she reads the negative as “you’re short,” not “I owe you.” Gen Z + tech stereotype flips into anti-skill: the tool was used, the operation was inverted.

## Punchline
The customer’s “Eh?” (transcript)—one syllable of disbelief. No lecture on arithmetic; the negative balance *is* the joke. Status: cashier holds the money and the wrong conclusion with equal confidence.

## Reaction / Button
Cut to another Gen Z cashier face (Aphex Twin tee energy in end frame) still under the same POV title—loop implication: this is the generation’s POS culture, not a one-off oops.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Literal tool hell** — calculator present, competence absent; visual proof on screen beats explanation.
- **Inversion** — 100k vs 65k should mean “here’s your change”; machine outputs “you owe 35.”
- Clean numbers: 65 / 100 / 35—easy subtitle pops for remakes.
- Weak if you skip showing −35; dialogue alone can sound like a real shortage.

## Remake notes
- Steal the machine: **simple change → phone calc → operands reversed → accuse the customer of being short.**
- Miki as cashier or as freelancer invoicing: “You paid 100, project was 65… so you still owe 35?”
- Hero prop: giant phone UI with 65−100 and red −35; hold 1s.
- Performance: deadpan delivery of “kurang 35 ribu”; panic only on the customer’s “Eh?”
- One counter, one note, one phone—no second location.

# Vid 074 — Autopsy

## Premise
A cafe cashier tries to take an order from a customer whose voice is so quiet that the transaction becomes a string of shouted “Hah?” until the item finally lands as lasagna—and he still needs a beat to process it.

## Hook
POV caption: “POV: pembeli suaranya kecil.” Soft pink bakery counter (Rahma Kitchen), apron, QRIS stand. Cashier opens polite: order prompt (ASR: “Baka boleh orderan yang mau apa?” ≈ “Kak, boleh orderannya yang mau apa?”). Instant workplace friction: service script vs. inaudible client.

## Setup
Customer murmurs something. Cashier cycles through confusion beats—“Hah? / Cik? / Hah? / Apakah?”—leaning farther over the marble each time. Aesthetic shop posters (“Positive Vibes Only”) vs. his aggressive lean-in is the visual gag. Soft voice forces physical comedy (invasion of personal space) before any product name is clear.

## Turn
ASR hears “Diblasannya / Biplasannya”; on-screen yellow subtitle lands the real word: **lasagna**. Cashier echoes “Oh, biplasannya?”—recognition delayed one beat after the shout-loop. Misheard food name is the turn: noise floor + quiet customer = brand/item detective work.

## Punchline
“Tunggu bentar ya, Kak?” / “BENTAR YA KAK”—he doesn’t celebrate understanding; he stalls to go find/confirm the item. The laugh is operational: hearing the order isn’t finishing the job.

## Reaction / Button
Hands clasped, polite reset face—back to “good service” posture after the HAH marathon. No scolding the customer; the button is professional patience after volume warfare.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Service-tool hell** — the “tool” is the customer’s voice; the system (counter etiquette) can’t proceed until the input clears.
- **Repeat beat** — HAH stack is rule-of-three energy without needing three different props.
- **ASR note** — Whisper tags `language=id` but garbles the food name; frames/subtitles supply “lasagna.” “Bahasa Melayu” in the transcript is likely a mishear of a soft murmur, not a literal language request.
- Weak if the lean-in isn’t exaggerated; the joke is visual strain + one recovered noun.

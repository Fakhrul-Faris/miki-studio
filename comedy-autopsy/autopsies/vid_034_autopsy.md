# Vid 034 — Autopsy

## Premise
A job candidate answers "Why should we hire you?" with the most literal truth possible — then hijacks the interview and interrogates why the role keeps opening up.

## Hook
Flat white void, @jasanimations style: black-haired girl in mint-green tee, hands clasped on desk, deadpan dot eyes and flat mouth — classic "job interview" pose. Interviewer (off-screen): **"So why should we hire you?"**

## Setup
Candidate: "What?" Beat. **"Because you guys are hiring."** Interviewer rejects it: "That is not an acceptable answer." Her expression stays neutral — no panic, no pivot to a résumé flex.

## Turn
Power flip — she becomes the interviewer: **"You know what? Why should I accept this position?"** → "Why is this position open?" → **"Do people keep quitting?"** Frames show her leaning in, eyes narrowing, hands still clasped. Interviewer devolves to "Um..." / "Uh..." — status inverts completely.

## Punchline
She closes the trap: **"Why are people quitting this job? Is there something I should know?"** Interviewer has no answer. The joke is the candidate treating a red-flag hunt as due diligence while sounding exactly like HR.

## Reaction / Button
She stands up the bit: **"Okay, well, thank you for your time. I'll see you on Monday."** End frame: big smile, same clasped hands — she behaves like she got hired. Interviewer echoes weakly: "Okay, well, thank you for your time." Ambiguous win; the absurd confidence is the button.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Status flip in one location** — no props, just Q&A reversal; perfect for cheap animation.
- **Literal answer** ("because you're hiring") is funny because it's technically true and professionally suicidal.
- Escalating questions mirror real interview anxiety inverted — audience recognizes the red flags she's naming.
- Button lands because she skips negotiating and assumes employment — confidence as punchline.
- Very short (~22s); could use one visual beat of interviewer sweating, but minimalism is the style.

## Remake notes
- Steal the machine: **give the worst honest answer → seize interviewer authority → ask the questions they're hiding → leave like you won.**
- Grease Pencil: single desk, two characters swap who leans forward; interviewer can be off-screen VO with reaction inserts.
- Candidate mouth stays small; comedy is eyebrow/eye slits tightening each question (frames 003–005 progression).
- Optional prop: "OPEN POSITION" flyer on desk she taps on "Do people keep quitting?"
- End smile should feel unnervingly sincere — Smiling Friends energy on the Monday line.
- Variations: landlord interview, therapist intake, first date — any scene with hidden institutional rot.

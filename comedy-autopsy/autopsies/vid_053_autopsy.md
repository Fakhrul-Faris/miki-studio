# Vid 053 — Autopsy

## Premise
A husband asks his partner to guess his Marketplace flip price; her inflated guess makes his modest $90 sale feel like a letdown, then an awkward apology kills the mood.

## Hook
Purple living room, couple on couch—he’s on his phone, she’s knitting. He opens: “Hey babe, guess how much I sold that table for on Marketplace?” Hook frame freezes the brag mid-sentence; domestic low-energy contrast sets up a numbers game.

## Setup
She guesses “600.” He reacts big—“What? 600?”—and reframes the object: “It was a table. It was my table from college.” Overlay “600!” lands her guess as the absurd anchor. He mocks the premise: “What did you think? It was made of porcelain or something?”

## Turn
Reveal deflates both characters: “It was $90. It was 90. I thought that was really good.” His pride is sincere; her guess overshot so far that his win reads as failure. Visual: his shocked/grimace face vs. her calm knitting—status inversion (he’s the one spiraling over math).

## Punchline
Social misfire: “Oh, sorry.” She apologizes for guessing too high, as if she insulted him—when he was fishing for applause. Double awkward: he wanted validation; she accidentally implied he should have gotten more.

## Reaction / Button
“No, it’s fine. Let’s just watch the movie.” Deadpan reset—both return to couch rhythm (phone, knitting). End frame is neutral domestic quiet; no hug, no price redo. The joke ends on unresolved vibe, not a zinger.

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Gap comedy** — audience fills in “probably $200–400”; $90 against $600 is a clean spread.
- **Relationship realism** — brag → wrong guess → apology → pretend nothing happened.
- **Visual foil** — his expressive phone panic vs. her craft-session calm amplifies the miscommunication.
- **Short runtime** — one question, one wrong answer, one reveal; no third beat needed.
- Weaker if viewers miss that *he* thinks $90 is good; his line “I thought that was really good” must read proud, not ashamed.

## Remake notes
- Steal the machine: **humble brag → over-generous guess → pride/deflation → polite retreat to TV.**
- Same couch set as other couple bits (purple palette, foot cushions, yarn bowl)—reuse layout.
- Text pops for “600!” and “IT WAS $90” as subtitle gags; don’t animate a literal porcelain table.
- Performance: his shock is theatrical (wide mouth); her “sorry” is small, immediate, then still knitting.
- Button: cut to both facing forward, lights dimmer—movie starting is the punctuation, not a new joke.

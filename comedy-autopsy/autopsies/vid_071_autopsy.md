# Vid 071 — Autopsy

## Premise
At **Toko Snack SURYA**, a normal card payment (“Pembayaran”) is staged as an action-movie collar-grab stare-down over the POS — until a third person breaks it up with “sudah sudah,” and staff resets to polite service smile.

## Hook
Chapter title **“Pembayaran”** over a customer in an Orlando/LANDO FC jersey walking in, eyes on the card in his hand. Mundane retail errand announced like a duel title card. Shop already readable: snack shelves, tiled floor, “TOKO SNACK SURYA” energy.

## Setup
Wooden counter, **POS+** terminal out, black plastic bag forming, Aqua bottles. Cashier in white tee leans across and **grabs the customer’s collar** — noses almost touching, both faces dead serious. Card still hovered over the EDC. Payment framed as debt-settlement violence, not checkout.

## Turn
Hold the clinch long enough that the genre mismatch becomes the joke. A third body enters the bit (break-up / low physical interrupt energy while the bag is still mid-transaction). The “turn” is duration + escalation of bodies: tap ritual → fight choreography → crowd control.

## Punchline
Mediator lean-in with subtitle **“Eh eh eh sudah sudah sudahh.”** The fight dissolves because someone calls time — payment theater needs a ref, not a witty line. Goods bag completes; the transaction “works” after the melodrama.

## Reaction / Button
Staff in **Toko Snack SURYA** polo ends in clasped-hands polite smile — customer-service face restored as if the collar grab never happened. Outro card thanks the viewer (ASR’s only usable line).

## Why it works (or doesn't)
- **Genre mismatch** — EDC tap ≠ gangster payoff; bodies play the wrong film.
- **Title does premise** — without “Pembayaran,” it reads as random fight.
- **Mediator button** — “sudah sudah” is release, not punchline wit.
- **Whiplash reset** — service smile is the button; sincerity after violence.
- ASR was empty beyond outro; autopsy grounded in frames + two burned-in phrases. Dense 1fps confirms SURYA branding, POS+, jersey, and mediator beat.
